MEP Engineering Services

Mechanical, electrical, and plumbing systems engineered together — clash-checked against the structure, documented for permit, and sized from real load calculations rather than rules of thumb.

One Coordinated Model, Three Disciplines

Uncoordinated MEP drawings are the single largest source of construction RFIs and change orders. A duct that collides with a steel beam, a panel with no working clearance, a sanitary line with nowhere to slope — each one costs days in the field. Because our mechanical, electrical, and plumbing engineers work in the same model alongside our structural team, those collisions are resolved on screen, before the drawings ever reach a bidder.

Mechanical design begins with ACCA Manual J / ASHRAE-based heating and cooling load calculations — room by room, with actual envelope constructions, orientation, and internal gains, never a square-footage rule of thumb. From the loads we select equipment with local climate derating, route duct and refrigerant piping, design ventilation to ASHRAE 62.1 outdoor-air rates, and document economizer, exhaust, and control requirements the energy code imposes.

Electrical design covers service entrance sizing, one-line diagrams, panel schedules with NEC Article 220 load calculations, feeder and branch circuit design, voltage-drop checks, short-circuit and coordination considerations, lighting layouts with fixture schedules and controls narratives, and emergency/egress power where occupancy requires it. Plumbing design covers domestic water sizing by fixture-unit method, water heater and recirculation design, sanitary waste and vent sizing, storm drainage, gas piping with pressure-drop calculations, and backflow prevention coordination with the water purveyor.

Energy Code Is Engineered In, Not Bolted On

Every MEP package we deliver includes the energy compliance documentation the jurisdiction requires — COMcheck or state-specific forms in most of the country, and full Title 24 Part 6 documentation for California projects, produced in-house. Because compliance is modeled during design rather than after it, we never have to strip efficiency features out of a bid set or re-engineer a system that failed the compliance run at the end.

The same integration pays off in plan check: mechanical, electrical, plumbing, and energy comments arrive at one firm, get resolved against one coordinated model, and go back to the reviewer in one response cycle instead of ricocheting between separate consultants.

What an MEP Engagement Looks Like

You send architectural backgrounds — CAD or even a clean PDF — plus the project address and a description of the use. We confirm the governing mechanical, electrical, plumbing, and energy codes with the permitting jurisdiction, then return a fixed-fee proposal that itemizes the sheets, calculations, and compliance documents you will receive and the date you will receive them. No hourly meters, no scope ambiguity: if the jurisdiction later adds a requirement we did not anticipate, that conversation happens openly rather than through an invoice.

During design, the three trades are developed against each other in structured coordination passes. Ductwork is routed around structure with real depths, not single-line diagrams; electrical rooms are sized with NEC working clearances drawn to scale; plumbing mains are sloped from the fixture farthest from the point of connection back to it, so the invert elevations actually work. Where the building is existing, we start from a field walk or photo survey of installed conditions, because designing to record drawings that no longer match reality is how change orders are born.

The permit package ships with everything the reviewer needs to say yes: load calculations bound into the set, equipment schedules with model numbers and capacities matching the calcs, energy compliance forms signed and matched to the specified equipment, and a code summary on the cover sheet keyed to the editions the jurisdiction enforces. After permit, we review contractor submittals against the design intent — a substituted RTU with a different electrical load touches three trades, and we check all three — respond to RFIs, and support inspections through closeout. For multi-site operators rolling out the same concept across states, we maintain a prototype set and adapt it jurisdiction by jurisdiction, which is dramatically cheaper than re-engineering each location from scratch.

Special-use projects get discipline-specific attention rather than a generic template. Restaurants carry kitchen hood exhaust, makeup air, grease waste, and heavy gas loads that interact with each other; medical and dental suites add vacuum, compressed air, and equipment-specific electrical circuits with isolated grounding; industrial tenants bring process loads, ventilation for heat and contaminants, and utility services far beyond a standard shell allowance. In every case the question we answer first is whether the base building can support the use — service capacity, gas availability, structural support for rooftop additions — because discovering a $150,000 utility upgrade after the lease is signed is the failure mode we exist to prevent. A one-week due-diligence review of the base building's mechanical, electrical, and plumbing capacity — panel schedules, gas meter sizing, RTU condition, sewer capacity — is the cheapest insurance a tenant or buyer can purchase, and we offer it as a standalone scope for exactly that reason.

MEP Engineering FAQs

Can you take on just one trade, or is it all three?
Either. Many clients bring us all three trades to capture the coordination benefit, but we regularly deliver mechanical-only, electrical-only, or plumbing-only scopes — for example, a panel upgrade or an RTU replacement package.
Do you do Title 24 / energy compliance?
Yes, in-house. California Title 24 Part 6 documentation, COMcheck for IECC states, and state-specific compliance forms elsewhere. Energy compliance is included in our MEP proposals by default.
What do you need from me to start?
Architectural backgrounds (CAD or PDF), the project address, and a description of the use. From that we confirm the governing codes with the jurisdiction and return a fixed-fee proposal, typically within 12–24 hours.
Do you size equipment with real load calculations?
Always. Rule-of-thumb tonnage is how buildings end up with oversized, short-cycling systems and failed energy compliance. Every system we specify traces back to a documented load calculation in the permit set.
Can you respond to plan-check corrections on drawings you didn't produce?
Yes. We review the correction list and the existing drawings, then quote either a targeted response or a re-engineered package — whichever is genuinely cheaper for you.
