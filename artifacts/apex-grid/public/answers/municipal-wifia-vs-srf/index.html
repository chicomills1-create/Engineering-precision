WIFIA vs. SRF: Which Water Infrastructure Financing Fits?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-10-02

WIFIA (EPA's Water Infrastructure Finance and Innovation Act program) and the State Revolving Funds both finance water infrastructure at below-market rates — but they serve different projects. SRF is the workhorse for typical municipal projects of any size, applied for through the state with annual cycles. WIFIA targets large projects (generally $20M+; $5M+ for small communities) with long terms up to 35 years, applied for directly to EPA through a competitive national process. Many large projects use both.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Two programs, two lanes

The State Revolving Funds — Clean Water and Drinking Water — are state-administered, federally capitalized loan programs that have financed water infrastructure since the late 1980s (CWSRF) and late 1990s (DWSRF). Every state runs them; every municipality knows the drill: annual Intended Use Plan, priority ranking, engineering report, below-market loan, with principal forgiveness for disadvantaged communities. SRF handles projects from the smallest main replacement to nine-figure treatment plants, and it's the default financing for municipal water work.

WIFIA, created in 2014, is EPA's direct-lending program modeled on transportation's TIFIA: EPA lends directly to borrowers for large water infrastructure projects — treatment plants, major conveyance, desalination, large storage, water recycling. The thresholds: generally $20 million minimum project cost ($5 million for communities under 25,000 population). Terms up to 35 years, at Treasury-rate-plus-a-spread — rates comparable to or better than SRF in many cases. WIFIA can fund up to 49% of project costs, with the balance from SRF, bonds, or other sources. It's competitive, national, and slower — and for the right project, the cheapest capital available.

Head-to-head: how they compare

Size: SRF has no minimum — a $500,000 main replacement is a fine SRF project. WIFIA's $20M floor ($5M small-community) excludes small projects by design. Rate: both below-market; WIFIA's Treasury-based rate is often the lowest available long-term rate in water infrastructure, while SRF rates (0-3% typical) can be lower still for disadvantaged borrowers with forgiveness. Term: WIFIA up to 35 years from substantial completion; SRF up to 30 (40 for disadvantaged in some states) — both far beyond bond-market norms for the sector.

Process: SRF runs through the state's annual IUP cycle — predictable, relationship-driven, with state staff who know the program. WIFIA runs through EPA's competitive letter-of-interest process: project readiness, creditworthiness review, engineering due diligence, NEPA environmental review, then loan negotiation — a 12-24 month process demanding serious borrower capacity. Federal requirements: both carry Davis-Bacon, American Iron and Steel / Build America Buy America, and environmental review — WIFIA's federal nexus adds NEPA and more intensive underwriting. Speed: SRF is the faster path for projects that fit; WIFIA rewards borrowers who can carry the longer process for the rate advantage on large programs.

  • SRF: any size, state-administered, annual IUP cycle, faster
  • WIFIA: $20M+ ($5M small community), EPA direct, up to 35-year term
  • Rates: both below-market; compare all-in cost including effort
  • Both carry Davis-Bacon, AIS/BABA, environmental review
  • Combined financing (WIFIA + SRF + bonds) is common on large programs

The combined play on large programs

The sophisticated move on large water programs is stacking: WIFIA for up to 49% at the Treasury rate, SRF for a share (with principal forgiveness where the community qualifies), and revenue bonds or cash for the balance. Each source has its own requirements, timelines, and compliance regimes — the financing plan is a project in itself, and it has to be built before the engineering schedule locks, because the funding calendar drives everything.

This is where the engineer earns the financing fee: the facilities plan that supports both the SRF engineering report and the WIFIA letter of interest; the rate analysis proving repayment capacity to two underwriters; the environmental review scoped to satisfy both state and NEPA requirements without doing the work twice; the project packaging (phasing, bid packages) that matches disbursement schedules. Our municipal practice builds the financing strategy with the engineering from day one — because on a $50M program, the financing structure is worth as much as the design optimization.

How to choose: a decision framework

Choose SRF when: the project fits the state's IUP cycle and priority ranking; the community qualifies for principal forgiveness (which can beat WIFIA's rate advantage outright); speed matters; or the borrower's staff capacity suits the state relationship better than a federal underwriting. Choose WIFIA when: the project exceeds $20M; the borrower wants the longest term and lowest Treasury-based rate; the program is large enough to absorb the application effort; or the capital plan needs WIFIA's 49% alongside other sources.

And seriously consider both when the program is large: the blended cost of capital from WIFIA + SRF-with-forgiveness routinely beats any single source. The deciding factors are never just the interest rate — they're the all-in cost including staff effort, the schedule fit with the project's compliance or capacity deadlines, and the borrower's capacity to manage parallel federal processes. We advise municipalities through this decision with real numbers: modeled debt service under each structure, timeline to first disbursement, and the compliance burden honestly described. The cheapest rate with a two-year delay can be the most expensive financing — the analysis has to include time.

What else do project teams ask?

What is WIFIA?
The Water Infrastructure Finance and Innovation Act program — EPA direct lending for large water infrastructure projects ($20M+, or $5M+ for small communities), at Treasury-based rates with terms up to 35 years. Created in 2014, modeled on the transportation TIFIA program.
Can a project use both WIFIA and SRF?
Yes — and large programs commonly do. WIFIA can fund up to 49% of project costs, with SRF, bonds, or other sources covering the balance. Each source has its own application, timeline, and compliance requirements, so the financing plan coordinates them.
Is WIFIA a grant?
No — it's a loan, at below-market Treasury-based rates. Principal forgiveness (the grant-like element) comes through the SRF programs, not WIFIA. Borrowers seeking grant-equivalent subsidy should evaluate SRF disadvantaged-community provisions.
How long does WIFIA approval take?
Typically 12-24 months from letter of interest through loan closing: EPA project selection, creditworthiness and engineering due diligence, NEPA environmental review, and loan negotiation. Borrowers need the staff or advisor capacity to sustain a federal underwriting process.
