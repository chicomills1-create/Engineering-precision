How Are Mass Timber Schools Designed in the Pacific Northwest?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Mass timber schools are designed with CLT panels and glulam frames as structure, finish, and fire protection in one: char-rated member sizing, protected ductile connections for seismic loads, engineered CLT diaphragm action, acoustic floor buildups between classrooms, and moisture protection from fabrication through occupancy.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Char Design and Connection Fire Protection

The engineer sizes every exposed timber member for two conditions: the structural loads with the full cross-section, and the fire condition with the charred sacrificial layer removed. This dual sizing is what makes the fire rating calculable rather than hopeful — the remaining core after the rating period's charring must still carry the fire-level loads. The engineer documents the char assumptions and the resulting residual sections on the structural drawings so the reviewer can verify the rating claim member by member. Connections receive the most careful fire detailing in the building. Exposed steel plates and bolts at beam-column joints are the weak link in a fire, so the engineer conceals connectors within the timber, protects them with additional wood cover, or specifies intumescent protection where concealment is impossible. Panel bearing connections — CLT floors on glulam beams or walls — are detailed so the bearing survives the char period, and the engineer verifies that shrinkage and movement at connections do not open gaps that would let fire into the joint. Every connection detail is drawn at a scale the fabricator can build, because timber connection performance lives or dies in the shop.

Seismic Connections, Diaphragms, and Acoustics

The lateral system's ductility lives in its steel connections. The engineer designs hold-downs, shear brackets, and beam-column connectors to yield before the timber crushes or splits — capacity design applied to wood — and specifies the fastener patterns, edge distances, and member thicknesses that make the ductile mechanism govern. CLT shear walls are detailed with vertical panel joints that can rock and dissipate energy, and the engineer checks the overturning restraint at the foundation for the full seismic demand, since a light timber building still overturns. Floor diaphragm design makes the panels act as one. The engineer specifies the spline or lap joints between CLT panels, the screw patterns that transfer diaphragm shear across those joints, and the boundary connections that drag the shear into the walls — then verifies the diaphragm's stiffness and strength under both wind and seismic loads. For acoustics, the engineer works from the consultant's targets: mass-plus-resilience floor buildups for impact isolation, sealed perimeters and penetrations for airborne sound, and isolated ceiling or topping systems where classroom-to-classroom privacy demands more than the bare CLT provides. The MEP engineer routes to planned, sealed penetrations so the acoustic detailing survives construction.

What else do project teams ask?

How does mass timber achieve a fire rating without sprinklers or drywall?
Through charring: mass timber chars at a slow, predictable rate, and the engineer sizes each member with sacrificial wood beyond the structural requirement. In a fire, the outer layer chars while the inner core keeps carrying load for the rating period. Steel connections are concealed or protected so they do not fail before the wood — the rating is engineered into the member sizes and details.
Is a timber school safe in a Pacific Northwest earthquake?
Yes when engineered for it. Timber's light weight reduces seismic forces compared to concrete or masonry, and the engineer designs ductile steel connections that yield in a controlled way, CLT shear walls detailed for energy dissipation, and full overturning restraint. Nonstructural components — ceilings, equipment, shelving — get the same seismic bracing any school requires.
How is classroom-to-classroom noise controlled with exposed CLT?
Bare CLT alone does not meet classroom acoustic targets, so the engineer coordinates floor buildups with resilient layers or floating toppings for impact sound, seals every MEP penetration and perimeter joint against airborne flanking, and adds isolated ceiling or wall treatments where privacy demands it. The acoustic detailing is drawn, not left to field improvisation.
What protects the timber from rain during construction?
A specified moisture-management plan: just-in-time panel delivery, temporary weather protection or tenting during erection, and detailing that sheds water from the partially built structure. The engineer writes the protection requirements into the specifications because timber that absorbs Pacific Northwest rain during construction carries elevated moisture into the finished building.
