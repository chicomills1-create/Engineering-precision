What Does the New Madrid Seismic Zone Mean for Memphis Building Design?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Memphis's New Madrid hazard puts most buildings in Seismic Design Category D, requiring full ASCE 7 seismic detailing, and liquefiable river alluvium forces deep foundations below the liquefiable layers. Engineers also brace nonstructural systems for seismic forces and permit through the Memphis and Shelby County Office of Construction Code Enforcement, the AHJ that decides approval.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Seismic Design Categories and Detailing

The seismic design category is the code's way of scaling requirements to risk. In Memphis, the combination of high mapped accelerations and common Site Class D or worse soils lands most commercial buildings in Category D, where the code demands ductile detailing: reinforcement arranged so concrete frames and walls yield gradually rather than failing suddenly, steel connections detailed for cyclic loading, and diaphragms with collectors that can deliver earthquake forces to the vertical system without tearing. Irregularities get special attention because earthquakes punish them. Soft stories, torsional layouts, and vertical discontinuities trigger additional analysis and detailing rules, and some configurations are prohibited outright in high categories. The engineer also designs foundations for seismic overturning and sliding, including tie beams between pile caps where the geotechnical report calls for them. Special inspection of seismic-force-resisting elements, from rebar placement to structural welding, is part of the permit package because ductile details only work when they are built as drawn.

Liquefaction, Deep Foundations, and Existing Buildings

Liquefaction analysis starts with the boring log: saturated loose sands and silts below the water table are the susceptible layers, and standard penetration or cone penetration data feeds the triggering calculation. When layers are found liquefiable, the foundation design has two jobs: bear below the problem soils, and survive their settlement. Driven piles and drilled shafts both work; the choice depends on loads, vibration limits near neighbors, and the depth to competent bearing. The structural engineer adds downdrag loads to the pile design so the settling soil does not overload the shafts. For existing buildings, the question is what the original construction assumed, which in Memphis is often very little seismic resistance. Unreinforced masonry and non-ductile concrete frames are the priority building types for evaluation. The retrofit menu includes adding shear walls or steel braced frames, anchoring floors and roofs to walls so diaphragms cannot separate, and jacketing deficient columns. Each retrofit is permitted through the same Office of Construction Code Enforcement, with the evaluation report forming the technical basis the plan reviewer approves against.

What else do project teams ask?

What was the New Madrid earthquake sequence?
The New Madrid sequence of 1811-1812 was a series of very large intraplate earthquakes centered near the Missouri bootheel, felt across the eastern United States. It remains the reference event for central U.S. seismic hazard. Modern building codes translate that hazard into mapped ground motions that drive Memphis seismic design, even though the last major sequence was two centuries ago.
What is a seismic design category?
A seismic design category is a classification from A to F that scales the code's earthquake requirements to the hazard at the site and the building's importance. Higher categories trigger stricter detailing, analysis, and irregularity limits. Most Memphis commercial buildings land in Category D because of the New Madrid hazard combined with local soil conditions.
What is liquefaction?
Liquefaction is the loss of strength in loose, saturated sands and silts during strong shaking, when pore water pressure rises until the soil behaves like a liquid. Buildings on liquefied soil can settle, tilt, or have buried structures float upward. Memphis's Mississippi River alluvium contains such soils, so engineers evaluate liquefaction from boring data and design deep foundations that bear below the liquefiable layers.
Can an existing Memphis building be seismically retrofitted?
Yes. An ASCE 41 seismic evaluation documents the existing lateral system and quantifies its deficiencies, and the retrofit adds shear walls, braced frames, diaphragm anchorage, or column strengthening as the evaluation indicates. The work is permitted through the Memphis and Shelby County Office of Construction Code Enforcement, and the AHJ decides what is approved.
Who reviews structural permits in Memphis?
The Memphis and Shelby County Office of Construction Code Enforcement is the joint city-county authority for building permits, covering Memphis and unincorporated Shelby County. Suburban municipalities inside the county may have their own additional requirements, so the engineer confirms the AHJ from the parcel address.
