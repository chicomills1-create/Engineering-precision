How Is Facade Lighting Designed for Dealership Buildings?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Engineers design dealership facades with wall washing, grazing, and sign integration matched to brand standards, using shielded fixtures and scheduled controls that present the building without glare or light trespass.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Brand Standards and Architectural Techniques

Automaker brand standards often govern the facade: sign size and illumination, permitted color temperatures, sometimes even fixture types. The engineer starts by collecting the current brand requirements — they change — and designs the architectural lighting to complement rather than compete with the signage. The building and the brand mark read as one composition from the street. Wall washing delivers even illumination across flat facade areas; grazing from close-set fixtures emphasizes texture in masonry or metal panel; canopy and entry accents draw the eye to the doors. The engineer spaces fixtures from mockups or calculations to eliminate scalloping, and keeps the color temperature consistent with the showroom interior visible through the glass so the whole building glows as a single statement.

Glare Control, Ordinances, and Energy Compliance

Every facade fixture is aimed and shielded to put light on the building, not into drivers' eyes or neighboring properties. The engineer checks aiming angles against the roadway geometry, uses shielded fixtures at the building base where uplighting could spill, and verifies compliance with local lighting ordinances that increasingly restrict facade brightness and hours. Energy code lighting power limits apply to facade lighting as they do to the lot, so the design uses efficient LED sources and earns its brightness through optics rather than wattage. Controls provide the presentation schedule — full output during evening hours, reduced levels overnight — with the sign circuit scheduled separately to meet brand requirements without lighting the whole facade all night.

Dealership Facade Lighting Design Checklist

A complete facade package presents the brand without the glare: • Current automaker brand standards collected and incorporated • Wall washing, grazing, and accent techniques matched to facade materials • Fixture spacing verified against scalloping with consistent color temperature • Sign integration with independent scheduling per brand requirements • Shielded, aimed fixtures keeping light off roadways and neighboring properties • Local lighting ordinance and energy code compliance documented • Presentation scheduling: full evening levels, reduced overnight output

What else do project teams ask?

Do automakers dictate dealership facade lighting?
Often, yes. Many manufacturers publish brand standards covering exterior signage illumination, facade color temperature, and sometimes specific fixture approaches — the building is brand advertising. The engineer designs within those standards while handling the engineering the brand book does not cover: fixture aiming, glare control, electrical distribution, controls, and code compliance. When brand standards and local ordinances conflict, the engineer documents the conflict and designs the compliant path.
What is the difference between wall washing and grazing?
Wall washing places fixtures at a distance from the facade to deliver smooth, even illumination across the surface — the clean modern look. Grazing mounts fixtures close to the wall so light rakes across it at a shallow angle, emphasizing texture in stone, brick, or metal panel. The engineer chooses per the facade material and the desired effect, and both techniques need careful fixture spacing to avoid the scalloped pattern of visible hot spots between fixtures.
How do you light a facade without blinding drivers?
With shielded fixtures, careful aiming, and respect for the roadway geometry. Uplighting at the building base is aimed to terminate on the facade, not continue past it; fixtures near the street get house-side or glare shields; and the engineer checks the design from the driver's viewpoint, not just the architect's rendering. Facade brightness is also kept in proportion — a facade dramatically brighter than its surroundings creates the adaptation glare that troubles drivers.
Should facade lighting run all night?
Rarely at full output. The typical schedule runs full presentation levels through evening sales hours, then drops to reduced accent or security levels overnight, with the brand sign often on its own longer schedule. This satisfies brand visibility, supports security, and controls energy cost and light trespass. The control design documents each level and its hours so the intent survives staff changes — facades stuck on full bright all night are usually a scheduling failure, not a design choice.
