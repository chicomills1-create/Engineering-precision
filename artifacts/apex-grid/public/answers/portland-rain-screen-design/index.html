How Is a Rain Screen Wall Assembly Designed for Portland?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

A Portland rain screen is designed as a vented outer cladding over a drained, pressure-equalized cavity, with a continuous air and water barrier on the sheathing as the true weather line and fully flashed windows, bases, and penetrations so wind-driven rain drains out before reaching the structure.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Pressure Equalization and Cavity Design

Pressure equalization is the physics that makes a rain screen work in wind-driven rain. Wind pressing on the cladding face creates a pressure difference that would drive water through any joint; venting the cavity to the exterior lets cavity pressure rise to match, eliminating the driving force. The engineer sizes vent openings to balance two competing needs: enough open area to equalize pressure quickly during gusts, but baffled or compartmented so wind does not blow straight through and pressurize the cavity unevenly. Horizontal and vertical compartment seals divide the cavity into zones, each vented independently, so a gust on one elevation does not drive cavity air — and water — sideways into a sheltered zone. Cavity depth and drainage capacity are engineered, not guessed. The depth must break capillary bridging between cladding and barrier, provide room for the expected volume of incidental water to drain down the barrier face, and accommodate cladding movement and installation tolerance. The engineer details the cavity base with a sloped through-flashing and weep vents at regular spacing, and specifies insect screening that keeps pests out without choking airflow. At floor lines and shelf angles, the cavity must continue uninterrupted or be flashed across with the same drainage logic — a shelf angle that dams the cavity is a leak manufactured into the drawings.

Flashing Details That Decide Success or Failure

Windows are the highest-risk element in the assembly, and the engineer details each one as a small drainage system. Sill pan flashing with welded or sealed end dams catches water that gets past the window frame and directs it out over the cladding; jamb flashing laps over the pan ends; head flashing extends beyond the jamb flashing and kicks outward. All of it integrates shingle-fashion with the air and water barrier so water always moves from an upper layer onto a lower layer, never behind one. The engineer shows these laps in section details at a scale the installer can actually build from, because a detail nobody can read is a detail nobody follows. Penetrations — hose bibs, light fixtures, vents, conduit — get individual flashing boots or boxes tied into the barrier, and the engineer lists them on the envelope schedule so none are field-improvised. Tops of walls get coping or parapet flashing that sheds water outward in both directions, and the engineer extends the air barrier up and over parapets or seals it to the roof membrane to keep the plane continuous. The final protection is inspection: the engineer specifies phased envelope inspections — barrier complete before cladding starts — because every one of these details is buried and uncheckable once the cladding goes on.

What else do project teams ask?

What is the difference between a rain screen and face-sealed cladding?
Face-sealed cladding, like traditional stucco or EIFS, relies on its outer surface and sealant joints to keep all water out — one line of defense that fails as sealant ages. A rain screen accepts that some water gets past the cladding and provides a drained, ventilated cavity behind it plus a continuous barrier on the sheathing as the real weather line. In Portland's months of wind-driven rain, the two-stage system is far more forgiving of aging and movement.
How deep should the rain screen cavity be?
Typically three-quarters of an inch to two inches, depending on cladding type, expected water volume, and drainage needs. The cavity must be deep enough to break capillary action between cladding and barrier, drain incidental water down the barrier face without bridging, and accommodate installation tolerances. The engineer sets the depth on the wall sections and keeps it consistent at transitions like floor lines and shelf angles.
Why do rain screen windows leak if the concept is sound?
Almost always because of flashing or drainage failures, not the concept. Common field causes: sill pans without end dams, head flashing that dumps water behind the cladding below, barrier laps reversed so water runs behind the plane, and weep vents blocked by mortar or sealant. The engineer's defense is buildable section details and phased inspections that verify the barrier and flashing before cladding conceals them.
Can a rain screen go over mass timber or wood framing?
Yes, and the ventilated cavity is especially valuable there because it lets the assembly dry between storms. The engineer keeps the cavity vented top and bottom, specifies corrosion-resistant furring and fasteners for the constant dampness, and makes sure the air and water barrier protects the timber or sheathing as the true weather line. Cladding attachment must account for the structural capacity of the timber substrate.
