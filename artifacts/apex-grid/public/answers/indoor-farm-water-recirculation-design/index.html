How Is Water Recirculation Designed for Indoor Hydroponic Farms?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Water recirculation is engineered as a facility water utility: surge-rated capture hydraulics, sterilization dosed to flow rate and water clarity with verifiable performance, loop detailing that denies biofilm any habitat, and a metered water balance of makeup, bleed-off, and discharge that holds the chemistry stable.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Surge Hydraulics and Collection Design

The return network is designed for peaks, because averages do not flood rooms — peaks do. The engineer models the simultaneous drain-down of irrigation zones, sizes the return mains for that coincident surge with the slopes that maintain self-cleansing velocity in organic-laden water, and details cleanouts at every bend and direction change. Collection sumps get coarse screening to stop root debris and media at the entry, level controls staged across lead-lag-standby pumps where the consequence of failure warrants it, and high-water alarms that report before the sump becomes a fountain. Tank sizing absorbs the cycle. The return or day tank must swallow the largest simultaneous drain-down without overflowing and still hold enough working volume to feed the treatment train steadily — the engineer sizes it from the irrigation schedule's worst-case overlap, not from a rule of thumb. Tank interiors are smooth and fully drainable for the periodic cleaning the sanitation plan requires, with access hatches a person can actually use. Overflow and emergency drainage are piped to a safe receptor, because the tank that cannot overflow safely eventually will overflow unsafely.

Sterilization Dosing and Biofilm Control

UV dose is engineered from two inputs: the flow rate through the unit and the clarity of the water carrying the organisms. The engineer takes the recirculation flow, applies the required log-reduction for the target pathogens, and selects units whose dose curves deliver it at the measured UV transmittance of the loop water — then designs the upstream filtration to hold that transmittance, with the pressure gauges and service schedule that keep it there. Lamp age derating is included in the sizing, because a UV system sized to new-lamp output is undersized within a year. Ozone systems add contact-tank hydraulics to the design: the contact time at peak flow determines the kill, and the off-gas destruction keeps residual ozone away from people and plants. Biofilm control is a detailing discipline. The engineer lays out the loop with continuous circulation — no stagnant branches, no rarely used bypasses left valved in — and specifies piping materials and joint methods that do not offer crevices for colonization. Flushing velocities are verified for the periodic high-velocity flushes that scour developing film, and the chemical cleaning provisions (isolation, injection points, neutralization before return to service) are designed in rather than improvised later. Zone isolation valves complete the biology defense: when monitoring flags a suspect zone, its return is quarantined at the valve while the rest of the farm keeps irrigating.

Indoor Farm Water Recirculation Design Checklist

Use this checklist before the recirculation design is finalized: • Return piping and sumps sized for coincident drain-down surges with self-cleansing slopes and full cleanout access • Collection tanks sized for the worst-case irrigation overlap with smooth, fully drainable interiors and safe overflow • Pumps selected for return duty with standby capacity and high-water alarming where flooding is the failure mode • UV or ozone sterilization dosed from flow rate, required log-reduction, and measured water clarity with lamp derating • Upstream filtration designed to hold the clarity the sterilization dose assumes, with service gauges at each stage • Loop detailing with no dead legs, scouring flush velocities, and chemical-cleaning provisions designed in • Sample ports before and after each treatment stage so sterilization performance is verifiable • Metered water balance documented: makeup, bleed-off, and discharge with the bleed stream to approved outlet

What else do project teams ask?

Why size the return piping for surges instead of average flow?
Because irrigation returns arrive in waves. When zones drain or benches cycle together, the instantaneous inflow dwarfs the daily average — and undersized return piping backs up, overflows sumps, and floods grow rooms. The engineer models the coincident drain-down from the irrigation schedule and sizes pipes, sumps, and tanks for that peak, with high-water alarms as the backstop. Averages describe the water bill; peaks describe the plumbing.
How is UV dose actually calculated for recirculated water?
From the flow rate, the required pathogen kill, and the water's clarity. The engineer selects units using the manufacturer's dose curves at the loop's measured UV transmittance — because murky water shields organisms — sizes for the end of lamp life rather than new-lamp output, and designs the upstream filtration to maintain the assumed clarity. Sample ports before and after the unit let the grower verify the dose is being delivered, not just designed.
What is biofilm and why does loop detailing matter?
Biofilm is the slimy microbial layer that colonizes wet surfaces — and in a recirculating loop it harbors pathogens, clogs emitters, and shields organisms from sterilization. It establishes wherever water stagnates or surfaces offer crevices, which is why the engineer eliminates dead legs, maintains circulation everywhere, specifies smooth joint methods, and designs scouring flushes. Detailing is the difference between a loop that stays clean and one that cultures problems.
How is the bleed-off rate determined?
From the salt ledger. Source water and fertilizers continuously add dissolved salts that plants uptake only selectively, so without an export path the concentration climbs until the crop suffers. The engineer calculates the bleed rate that holds salts at the crop's tolerance given the makeup water quality and the fertilizer program, and routes the bleed stream to the approved discharge. Too little bleed poisons the loop slowly; too much throws away the water savings recirculation was built for.
