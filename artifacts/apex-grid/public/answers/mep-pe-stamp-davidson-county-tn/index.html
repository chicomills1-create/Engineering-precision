MEP PE Stamp for Davidson County / Nashville, TN Permits

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

MEP sets in Davidson County are permitted through Metro Codes (the consolidated Nashville/Davidson County AHJ) and require Tennessee-licensed PE review-and-seal — design to Metro's adopted codes plus Tennessee and local amendments, with energy compliance documented, and submit a fully reviewed set.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Working with the consolidated Metro AHJ

Davidson County's consolidated government is a structural advantage for permitting: one building department, one amendment package, one portal, one inspection sequence. I take full advantage of it by treating the Metro Codes submittal as a single coordinated package — architectural, structural, and MEP designed to the same adopted codes and reviewed together before anything is sealed. The consolidation does not make the review lenient; Metro's reviewers are experienced and detail-oriented. What it does is remove the jurisdictional guessing that slows projects in fragmented counties, so the engineering quality of the set becomes the deciding factor in how fast it moves.

What our MEP review-and-seal covers

Our Tennessee-licensed PEs take responsible charge of every Davidson County MEP set we seal: complete review of the mechanical, electrical, and plumbing drawings against Metro's adopted codes and amendments, verification of HVAC and electrical load calculations, equipment selection and efficiency checks, plumbing sizing and fixture counts, and energy compliance documentation. Every required revision is marked and made before the seal goes on. The sealed set reflects engineering a licensed professional has reviewed and stands behind — which is what the seal certifies and what Metro's reviewers expect to find.

Davidson County MEP submittal checklist

An MEP package for Metro Nashville is ready when these items are resolved. One AHJ means one standard — meet it completely at first submittal. • Parcel status confirmed through Davidson County assessor records; Metro Codes verified as AHJ • Current Metro-adopted codes plus Tennessee and local amendments identified and designed to • MEP calculations verified: HVAC loads, electrical loads, plumbing sizing, energy compliance documented • Tennessee PE seal, signature, and date on every MEP sheet • Cross-discipline coordination checked — MEP vs. structural vs. architectural conflicts resolved pre-submittal

What else do project teams ask?

Who permits buildings in Davidson County — the county or Nashville?
Both, through one department: Nashville and Davidson County consolidated their governments, so the Metropolitan Department of Codes and Building Safety (Metro Codes) is the single AHJ for the county. There is no separate county building department to choose between. I still verify the parcel's status through the Davidson County assessor's records on every project — satellite cities and special districts can create edge cases — but for the vast majority of projects, Metro Codes is the reviewer, the portal, and the inspector. One AHJ means one amendment package to design to, which is a genuine advantage over fragmented counties.
Does Metro Nashville require a Tennessee PE seal on MEP drawings?
Yes — MEP engineering documents submitted to Metro Codes must bear the seal of a Tennessee-licensed Professional Engineer in responsible charge. The seal, signature, and date go on the MEP sheets, certifying the engineer reviewed the design and takes professional responsibility for it. An out-of-state PE's seal does not satisfy Metro. Our Tennessee-licensed PEs perform the complete review — calculations verified, energy compliance checked, revisions marked — before sealing anything.
What energy code provisions does Metro Nashville enforce on MEP designs?
Metro enforces energy conservation provisions as part of its adopted code package, and MEP reviewers check the compliance documentation — load calculations, equipment efficiency selections, lighting power densities, and the compliance forms or reports that demonstrate the design meets the code. This is one of the most common sources of first-cycle corrections on MEP sets that were designed without the local package in mind. Our review verifies energy compliance as a formal step before sealing, so the documentation Metro's reviewer expects is already in the set at first submittal.
How does MEP coordination with structural work in Metro review?
Because Metro Codes reviews the whole project under one roof, discipline conflicts surface during plan check rather than in the field — which is good if the set is coordinated and painful if it is not. Our MEP review includes a coordination pass against the structural and architectural drawings: shaft and chase locations, equipment weights and supports, ceiling space and routing, and penetration details. Catching a duct-versus-beam conflict during our review costs a revision; catching it during Metro's review costs a review cycle; catching it in the field costs real money.
