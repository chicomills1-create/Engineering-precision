MEP PE Stamp in Palm Beach County, FL: What Gets Reviewed Before Sealing?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

An MEP PE stamp in Palm Beach County certifies the HVAC, plumbing, and electrical design after a Florida-licensed PE verifies the loads under responsible charge — with coastal wind anchorage for equipment, flood-zone elevations, and verified dehumidification design, submitted to the county or the municipality that owns the site.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Coastal MEP: wind, salt, and surge

The Palm Beach County MEP review treats the coastal environment as three engineering problems. Wind governs equipment anchorage: I review rooftop unit curbs, rails, and connections as a structural problem — design pressures for the site's exposure, calculated uplift and lateral forces, and anchorage details with the capacities to resist them, coordinated with the structural set so the forces have somewhere to go. Generic manufacturer details get verified against the site's actual pressures, not accepted on faith. Salt governs material selection: the review checks that equipment, supports, fasteners, and enclosures specified for the coastal zone carry appropriate corrosion protection, because a perfectly anchored unit that corrodes off its curb in five years is not a durable design. Surge and flood govern elevations: outdoor equipment, electrical service, and emergency systems at flood-compliant heights that agree with the structural flood data. The sealed drawings show all three — anchorage, materials, elevations — explicitly, because the AHJ will verify each.

Humidity control beneath the coastal design

Underneath the coastal layer, the mechanical review is South Florida humidity engineering. I verify the load calculations used realistic design conditions with the latent load broken out explicitly, and check that the selected equipment's latent capacity covers it — ventilation air in this climate is a major dehumidification load that has to be in the capacity with a strategy that works in sustained humidity. The occupancy shapes the stakes: hospitality and multifamily dominate much of the county's building stock, and both punish poor humidity control with mold, complaints, and damage. Electrical and plumbing get the standard coordination discipline: risers tracing to plans, panels agreeing with the riser, fixture counts matched to occupancy, energy compliance documentation reconciled against the sealed set. When the coastal engineering is complete, the humidity design is verified, and every sheet agrees with every other sheet, the PE seals and the package goes to the county or the municipal AHJ.

MEP submittal checklist for Palm Beach County

An MEP package is ready for the PE's review — and then for the AHJ — when these items are resolved. Coastal engineering and humidity control are the dual themes. • AHJ confirmed by parcel: Palm Beach County vs the specific municipality • Florida-licensed PE in responsible charge of the MEP review • Rooftop and exposed equipment anchorage engineered for the site's wind pressures, with calculations • Corrosion protection specified for equipment, supports, and fasteners in the salt-air zone • Flood-compliant elevations for outdoor equipment, electrical service, and emergency systems • HVAC loads with latent broken out; equipment latent capacity verified; ventilation as dehumidification load • Plumbing risers, fixture counts, and electrical panels/riser fully coordinated • Energy compliance documentation reconciled against the actual sealed design

What else do project teams ask?

How does coastal exposure change the MEP review?
Three ways. First, wind: rooftop and exposed mechanical equipment needs engineered anchorage for the site's wind design pressures — the PE verifies curbs, rails, and connections against the calculated uplift rather than accepting generic details. Second, corrosion: salt air attacks equipment, supports, and fasteners, so the review checks that specifications call for appropriate materials and coatings in the coastal zone. Third, flood and storm surge: equipment elevations must satisfy the flood requirements and agree with the structural flood design. A coastal MEP design that handles the humidity but ignores the wind, the salt, or the surge is incomplete, and plan check in this county knows to look for all three.
Which AHJ reviews my Palm Beach County MEP submittal?
The county or the municipality, depending on the parcel — and with 39 municipalities plus unincorporated land, verification is mandatory. Each jurisdiction runs its own intake portal and checklist under the Florida Building Code. I confirm by parcel at kickoff on every project. The MEP review-and-seal does not change across AHJs, but the submittal mechanics do, and the sealed package must be assembled for the department that will actually review it. Guessing wrong here means re-submitting into a different workflow entirely.
What flood-related MEP detailing does the review check?
The PE verifies that outdoor mechanical equipment, electrical service equipment, generators, and transfer switches sit at flood-compliant elevations consistent with the structural flood design — the two disciplines must tell the same flood story, with the zone, base flood elevation, and design flood elevation shown on the drawings. I check equipment elevations against that data and confirm the drawings show the flood detailing explicitly rather than in a narrative. In a county where storm surge reaches far inland, MEP equipment below the flood level is a failure the review is supposed to catch before the AHJ does.
What MEP drawings does a Palm Beach County submittal require?
A complete MEP set typically includes mechanical plans with equipment, ductwork, and diffusers; the HVAC load calculation summary with latent loads broken out, plus equipment schedules; equipment anchorage details with calculated wind capacities; plumbing plans with fixture layouts; plumbing riser diagrams for water, waste, vent, and gas; electrical plans with lighting and power; panel schedules with calculated loads; the electrical riser diagram from service through distribution; emergency and standby power documentation where required; and energy compliance documentation. The PE verifies coordination across all of them — flood elevations, anchorage engineering, and sheet-to-sheet agreement — because mismatches are the most common corrections.
