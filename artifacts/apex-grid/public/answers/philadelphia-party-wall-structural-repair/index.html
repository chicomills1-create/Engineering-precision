How Are Philadelphia Party Walls Structurally Repaired?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Philadelphia party walls are repaired by documenting shared-wall condition from both properties, repointing deteriorated mortar, stitching or rebuilding cracked wythes, adding ties where walls separate, and underpinning settled sections - permitted through Licenses and Inspections with party-wall agreements between owners.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Diagnosing Party Wall Distress

The investigation starts with access to both buildings, because a party wall can look sound from one side and be failing on the other. The engineer maps every crack, bulge, and separation on elevation drawings, measures out-of-plumb with a level or laser, and probes mortar joints for depth of deterioration. Tell-tale monitoring - simple crack gauges read over weeks or months - separates active movement from historic settlement, which changes the repair from stabilization to cosmetic. The survey also records floor framing bearing on the wall, since joist ends pocketed into a bulging wall may need temporary support during repairs. Context matters as much as symptoms. The engineer asks about the buildings' histories: a demolished twin next door, a new deep excavation nearby, a recently added third story, or a chronic roof leak above the wall. Philadelphia's rowhouses were often built in runs by a single builder, so the wall's original construction - typically eight or nine inches of brick in lime mortar - is consistent, but each pair of houses has lived a different century since. The diagnosis names the mechanism - settlement, moisture-driven mortar loss, overload, or lateral movement - because each mechanism gets a different repair.

Ties, Underpinning, and Neighbor Coordination

Stitching is the workhorse repair for cracked but fundamentally sound walls. Stainless helical ties are grouted into drilled holes across cracks and between separated wythes, restoring the wall's ability to act as a unit without the disruption of rebuilding. Where brick is crushed or mortar is gone beyond repointing depth, the engineer specifies partial rebuilding - removing and relaying the failed zone in matched brick and lime mortar, toothed into the sound wall each side. Bulges that exceed stability limits are taken down and rebuilt; there is no tie strong enough to straighten a wall that has lost its geometry. Underpinning addresses the foundation when settlement is the cause. The work proceeds in short sequenced sections - typically four to five feet - excavating beneath the existing footing, pouring new concrete to a competent bearing stratum, and packing the joint before moving to the next section, so the wall above is continuously supported. Throughout, the engineer coordinates access, shoring, and temporary weather protection with both owners, because the work zone straddles the property line by definition. Vibration and dust monitoring protect both buildings, and the repair drawings note the party-wall agreement as a permit condition.

What else do project teams ask?

Who pays for party wall repairs in Philadelphia?
Generally both owners share the cost where the wall is genuinely common, under Pennsylvania party-wall law. The exact split depends on the cause - if one owner's leaking gutter destroyed the mortar, that owner typically bears more - and on the written agreement the neighbors sign before work starts. The engineer's condition report documents the cause, which is what the agreement and any dispute resolution rely on.
What causes party walls to bulge?
Loss of bond between the wall's wythes is the usual cause. Moisture softens the lime mortar, freeze-thaw cycles wedge the joints apart, and the outer wythe slowly separates and bows outward under its own weight and floor loads. Overloading from added stories and lateral push from a failing roof structure can accelerate it. A bulging wall is a stability problem, not a cosmetic one, and it needs an engineer's assessment.
Can I repoint a party wall with modern mortar?
Not with hard Portland-cement mortar on old soft brick. The original lime mortar is deliberately softer than the brick so the joints absorb movement; a harder modern mortar forces the brick faces to spall instead. The engineer specifies a repointing mortar matched to the original's lime content and compressive strength, and the work is tooled to shed water rather than trap it.
What permits does party wall work need in Philadelphia?
Structural party wall repairs go through Philadelphia Licenses and Inspections with drawings sealed by a licensed engineer, and the application generally requires sign-off from both adjoining owners. Where the wall is exposed by demolition or new construction next door, the permit may also trigger bracing and protection requirements for the remaining building. The AHJ reviews the structural design; the owners' agreement covers access and cost sharing.
