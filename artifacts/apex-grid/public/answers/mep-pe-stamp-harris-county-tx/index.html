MEP PE Stamp in Harris County, TX: County Permitting and Gulf Coast Design

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

An MEP PE stamp in Harris County means a Texas PE has taken responsible charge under TBPELS Rule 137.33 — verifying humid-climate HVAC design, energy-code consistency, and plumbing/electrical coordination — for projects reviewed by the Harris County Engineering Department in unincorporated areas.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Mechanical review for the Gulf Coast

Our Harris County mechanical review starts with load calculations built for hot-humid reality: the building's orientation, envelope, occupancy, and internal gains modeled for a cooling season that barely ends. We verify equipment selection against those loads with margins for continuous operation, then follow the chain through distribution sizing, duct and piping layout, and control sequences. Dehumidification gets dedicated verification — outdoor-air treatment, part-load moisture control, and ventilation per the adopted mechanical code — because humidity is the Gulf Coast's quiet building-killer. Rooftop equipment anchorage is checked for hurricane-region winds and coordinated with structural. The Texas PE seals when the mechanical system is engineered for Harris County's climate, verified link by link.

Electrical, plumbing, and flood-aware coordination

Electrical review verifies service and distribution sizing for the cooling-dominated load profile, panel schedules matched to the mechanical equipment's actual characteristics, lighting within energy-code allowances, and fire-alarm and life-safety interfaces. Plumbing review covers fixture counts, water and sanitary sizing, backflow prevention, and equipment elevations in floodplain areas. The coordination pass verifies risers in real shafts, code-required working clearances, and structural penetrations the framing drawings account for — plus floodplain coordination with the civil and structural designs. We review the MEP set against every other discipline before sealing, because in Harris County the water table, the floodplain, and the heat all punish designs that were never truly coordinated.

Harris County MEP submittal checklist

An unincorporated Harris County MEP package is ready when the AHJ is confirmed and Gulf Coast conditions are engineered. • AHJ confirmed: Harris County Engineering Department vs. City of Houston from parcel records • Cooling and dehumidification loads calculated for hot-humid conditions, traceable to equipment • Rooftop equipment anchorage verified for hurricane-region winds • Energy-code compliance forms consistent with final drawings and schedules • Floodplain equipment elevations and flood-resistant detailing verified where applicable • Electrical and plumbing coordinated with architectural and structural sets • Sealed documents show firm name and Texas firm registration number per TBPELS requirements

What else do project teams ask?

Does the MEP review differ between Harris County and the City of Houston?
The engineering standard doesn't change, but the review process does: unincorporated Harris County projects go through the Harris County Engineering Department's submittal path and checklists, while Houston projects go through the city's process. Reviewer emphasis can differ — the county's floodplain and drainage awareness, for example, colors how it reads MEP submittals for sites in or near floodplains. We confirm the AHJ first, then build the review package for that office's expectations. The responsible-charge engineering review we perform before sealing is identical in rigor either way.
What mechanical issues matter most in Harris County's climate?
Sustained heat and extreme humidity: cooling loads that run for most of the year, dehumidification that has to work at part load, and ventilation that meets code without importing moisture problems. Our review verifies genuine load calculations, equipment selected for continuous hot-humid operation, outdoor-air treatment and dehumidification design, and control sequences that manage both peak and shoulder conditions. We also verify rooftop equipment anchorage for hurricane-region winds. Reviewers check the loads-to-equipment chain closely, and our pre-seal review walks it first.
How does floodplain location affect MEP design in Harris County?
Equipment in floodplain areas needs elevation or flood-resistant design: electrical gear, mechanical equipment, and plumbing systems below the design flood elevation must be addressed per the applicable requirements. Our review checks the site's floodplain status, verifies equipment elevations and flood-resistant detailing, and confirms the MEP design coordinates with the project's floodplain compliance strategy. In a county where flooding is a defining risk, reviewers read MEP drawings with water in mind — we make sure the sealed set shows that the design does too.
What should the MEP review package include for Harris County?
The sealed MEP drawings, HVAC load calculations, equipment schedules, energy-code compliance forms, plumbing riser diagrams and fixture schedules, and electrical panel schedules and riser diagrams — plus a basis-of-design narrative so the reviewer can follow our assumptions. We complete cross-discipline coordination against the architectural and structural sets before the PE seals, and we organize the package for the AHJ's correction process with clouded revisions and written responses. A package that reads as one consistent, flood-aware, heat-engineered design moves through review.
