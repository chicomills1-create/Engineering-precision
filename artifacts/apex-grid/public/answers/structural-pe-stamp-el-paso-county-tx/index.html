Structural PE Stamp in El Paso County, TX: What Gets Reviewed?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

A structural PE stamp in El Paso County certifies the gravity, lateral, and foundation systems after a Texas-licensed PE verifies the calculations under TBPELS Rule 137.33 — with desert-soil foundation design verified against the geotechnical report, submitted to the county or the City of El Paso depending on the parcel's AHJ, with the firm's F-number on the seal.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Foundations on desert ground

The structural review in El Paso County starts with the geotechnical report, because the desert ground varies more than owners expect. Sandy and gravelly soils can offer excellent bearing — and the PE verifies the design takes proper advantage of it rather than defaulting to an overdesigned generic foundation. Caliche layers change the excavation story and sometimes the bearing story, and the review checks that the foundation design acknowledges what the borings actually found. Where the report flags soluble or collapsible soils, the moisture-management and foundation recommendations become design requirements, not suggestions. I check the foundation design against the report line by line: bearing pressures, settlement expectations, pier or footing depths, and the drainage detailing that keeps water away from the foundation in a climate where rare heavy rains do the damage. The sealed foundation sheets reference the report they were designed from, because a county or city reviewer will check that reference — and because responsible charge means the engineer tied the design to the ground, not to a template.

County development outside city limits

A large share of El Paso County's current development sits outside the City of El Paso — county subdivisions, corridor commercial, industrial and logistics sites — and the county's permitting process governs all of it. My groundwork on these projects includes confirming the county's current submittal requirements, because county processes evolve with the development volume, and verifying that the structural package addresses any county-specific conditions: grading and drainage coordination, access-related requirements, and subdivision infrastructure interfaces that the structural drawings need to acknowledge. The engineering review itself holds the same standard regardless of AHJ — responsible charge, verified calculations, marked revisions, then the seal — but the package is assembled for the jurisdiction that will review it. A complete first submittal with the F-number block correct, the geotechnical report referenced, design criteria shown on the drawings, and the calc package organized moves fastest through either the county's or the city's intake.

El Paso County structural submittal checklist

A structural package is ready for the PE's review — and then for the AHJ — when these items are resolved. Desert-ground foundation verification and jurisdiction confirmation lead the list. • AHJ confirmed by parcel: City of El Paso vs unincorporated El Paso County • Texas-licensed PE in responsible charge under TBPELS Rule 137.33; firm's F-number on the seal block • Geotechnical report with site-specific foundation recommendations for the desert soils • Foundation design verified against the report: bearing, settlement, depths, drainage detailing • Terrain-driven detailing where applicable: stepped footings, retaining, slope drainage • Complete gravity and lateral framing with a detailed, continuous load path • Calculations package organized so a reviewer can trace members to math

What else do project teams ask?

How do I know if my site is in the City of El Paso or unincorporated county?
Check the parcel against the city limits — the City of El Paso's boundaries are extensive but the county's unincorporated areas are significant, especially along the development corridors to the east and west and in the county's rural stretches. County subdivisions outside city limits are common, and commercial and industrial sites along the corridors frequently sit in county land. I verify by parcel at kickoff and confirm with the AHJ where it is ambiguous, because the city and the county run separate intake portals, checklists, and fee schedules. The jurisdiction determines the submittal mechanics, so this check comes before design, not at intake.
What soil conditions does the structural review address in El Paso County?
Desert soils with real variability: sandy and gravelly deposits with good bearing in some areas, caliche layers that change excavation and bearing assumptions, and zones where soluble soils or collapsible behavior demand specific geotechnical recommendations. The PE verifies the foundation design against the actual report — bearing pressures, settlement, deep vs shallow foundation decisions, and any soil-treatment or moisture-management recommendations. Near the Franklin Mountains, slope and rock conditions add retaining and stepped-foundation design to the review. A generic foundation detail has no place in this county; the review ties every foundation decision to the boring logs.
What does TBPELS Rule 137.33 require of the sealing engineer?
Rule 137.33 is the Texas Board of Professional Engineers and Land Surveyors rule on sealing: a PE may only seal work they performed or that was performed under their direct supervision, with the PE taking responsible charge. For your El Paso County project that means the sealing engineer actually reviewed the design — verified the load paths, checked the calculations, confirmed the foundation against the geotechnical report, marked the revisions — before the seal went on. The seal is the engineer's professional statement that the work meets the standard of care. That accountability is the entire value of the stamp, and it is why a legitimate seal is never a same-day formality.
What is in the structural calculations package?
The package documents the engineering behind the drawings: design criteria and load combinations, gravity member sizing, lateral analysis with drift checks, connection designs with calculated capacities, and foundation calculations tied to the geotechnical recommendations. For terrain-driven sites it also covers retaining and slope-related design. It is organized so a county or city reviewer can trace any member on the drawings back to its calculation. I treat the calc package as part of the sealed deliverable because it is the evidence that the review behind the stamp actually happened.
