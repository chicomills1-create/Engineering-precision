What Does an MEP PE Stamp Cover in Glendale, AZ?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

An MEP PE stamp in Glendale certifies the mechanical, electrical, and plumbing design after an Arizona-licensed PE verifies load calculations, equipment selections, risers, and distribution under responsible charge — with extreme desert heat making correct HVAC sizing the centerpiece of the review.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Designing for Glendale's desert heat

The mechanical review starts with the load calculations, because everything downstream inherits their assumptions. I check that the calculations used Glendale-appropriate outdoor design conditions — the kind of afternoons that define a West Valley summer — with realistic internal gains for the occupancy and ventilation rates per code. Equipment selection gets matched against the calculated loads at both peak and part-load conditions, and I look hard at the ventilation strategy: code-required outside air in this climate is a major cooling load, so energy recovery and proper controls sequencing matter more here than in mild climates. Filtration gets attention too, because monsoon dust will find any weakness in the airside design. The sealed drawings need to show a system that works as a system at 115 degrees, not a collection of catalog cuts.

What plan check reads closely in an MEP set

Glendale's reviewers read an MEP set the way a contractor builds from it: does the logic hold together? Riser diagrams get checked against the floor plans — every fixture on the plan should trace to the riser, and the riser should show pipe sizing, slopes, and venting that satisfy the plumbing code. Panel schedules get checked against the electrical riser: calculated loads, breaker sizes, and feeder sizes must agree, and the service calculation must support the whole building. Fixture counts get checked against occupancy, and specialty items like commercial kitchens, grease waste, or rooftop units get extra attention because they carry their own code requirements. The PE's pre-seal review runs this same gauntlet, so corrections come from the engineer — who can fix the design — instead of from the city, which costs a review cycle.

Glendale MEP submittal checklist

An MEP package is ready for the PE's review — and then for Development Services — when these items are resolved. Heat-driven mechanical design and internal coordination are where most corrections originate. • HVAC load calculations with realistic West Valley design conditions and ventilation loads accounted for • Equipment schedules matching the mechanical plans, verified at peak and part-load conditions • Plumbing plans, riser diagrams, and fixture counts coordinated and code-compliant • Electrical plans, panel schedules, and riser diagram in full agreement on loads and protection • Energy compliance documentation reconciled against the actual sealed design • MEP layouts coordinated with structural framing so clashes surface before plan check, not in the field

What else do project teams ask?

Why is HVAC sizing the centerpiece of an MEP review in Glendale?
Because the design problem is extreme and unforgiving. Glendale summers run brutally hot, so the cooling load is enormous and equipment that is even slightly undersized will run flat-out and still lose the space on the worst afternoons. Oversizing is not the answer either — oversized equipment short-cycles, dehumidifies poorly during monsoon humidity spikes, and wastes energy all year. The PE verifies that the load calculations used appropriate West Valley design conditions, realistic internal gains, and correct ventilation quantities, then checks the equipment selections against both full-load and part-load performance. Getting this right before the seal goes on is the difference between a building that works in August and one that generates service calls.
What MEP drawings does Glendale's plan review expect?
A complete MEP set for Glendale typically includes mechanical floor plans with equipment, ductwork, and diffusers; the HVAC load calculation summary and equipment schedules; plumbing floor plans with fixture layouts; plumbing riser diagrams for water, waste, vent, and gas; electrical floor plans with lighting and power; panel schedules with calculated loads; the electrical riser from service entrance through distribution; and energy compliance documentation. The PE's review checks that these sheets agree with each other — equipment schedules matching the plans, panel loads matching the riser — because internal coordination errors are among the most common corrections. Submit the coordinated set once rather than feeding the portal piecemeal.
Does the PE verify energy compliance documentation before sealing?
Yes — it is part of the review, not a separate errand. The PE checks that the energy compliance forms reflect the actual sealed design: envelope values matching the architectural drawings, lighting power densities matching the lighting plans, and mechanical efficiencies matching the equipment schedules. Arizona jurisdictions enforce the adopted energy code at plan check, so a mismatch between the forms and the drawings earns a correction every time. I reconcile the compliance documentation against the sealed set before the stamp goes on, because fixing it after submittal means burning a review cycle on paperwork.
Can one PE seal both structural and MEP drawings on the same Glendale project?
Only if that engineer is genuinely competent in both disciplines and takes responsible charge of both reviews — which is rare in practice. Structural and MEP sealing are normally done by different licensed engineers, each reviewing their own discipline's design and calculations. A firm offering both shows its Arizona registration on the sealed documents and assigns each discipline to the PE who actually performed the review. What one PE must never do is seal a discipline they did not review just to keep the submittal moving; responsible charge cannot be borrowed. For a full building submittal in Glendale, expect two reviewers and two seals.
