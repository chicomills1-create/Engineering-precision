How Are Foundations Designed on Raleigh Red Clay?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Foundations on Raleigh's red clay are designed by testing the soil's plasticity and swell potential, bearing shallow footings below the active moisture zone or switching to drilled piers that bypass expansive clay entirely, detailing moisture barriers and drainage, and stiffening slabs, with borings confirming every bearing stratum and permits through Raleigh or Wake County depending on the site.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Expansive Clay and the Active Zone

Expansive behavior comes from clay mineralogy. Certain clay minerals absorb water molecules between their crystal layers and push apart, generating swell pressures that can exceed the bearing pressure of a light building, which is why a slab-on-grade can heave inches while the structure above barely loads the soil. The plasticity index from Atterberg limit testing is the engineer's first screen: higher plasticity generally means higher swell potential. Where the screening flags risk, consolidation-swell tests quantify how much pressure the soil can exert, and that number sizes the foundation's resistance. The active zone concept turns this science into a design rule. Seasonal wetting and drying only reach so deep, below which the clay's moisture content stays nearly constant year-round. In the Raleigh Piedmont that boundary commonly falls five to eight feet down, varying with drainage, vegetation, and soil type. Foundations that bear below it, or deep elements that pass through it, experience little of the cyclic movement that destroys shallow slabs. The geotechnical engineer establishes the active zone depth from borings, moisture profiles, and local experience, and the structural engineer treats it as a hard constraint.

Piers, Stiffened Slabs, and Moisture Control

Drilled piers are the definitive answer for difficult red clay. A pier drilled through the expansive zone into competent saprolite or rock carries the building on end bearing and side resistance the clay cannot disturb, and the engineer details the upper shaft, sometimes with a void-forming sleeve, so swelling clay cannot grip the pier and jack it upward. Belled piers spread the load where the bearing stratum allows. For lighter structures on moderate clay, stiffened slabs, with deepened edge and interior beams forming a rigid grid, bridge over localized heave, and post-tensioning adds active compression that keeps the slab tight as the ground moves beneath it. Moisture control is the quiet half of the design. The site is graded to fall away from the building, roof water is piped well clear of the foundation, and subsurface drains intercept lateral flow before it reaches the bearing soils. Moisture barriers under slabs slow vapor and differential wetting. And the planting plan matters structurally: large trees stay outside the distance where their roots desiccate foundation soils, or the foundation is designed for the additional movement. None of this eliminates every inch of movement, but together it keeps movement within what the structure tolerates.

What else do project teams ask?

What makes red clay expansive?
Its clay minerals absorb water between their crystal layers and swell, then shrink as they dry, and Raleigh's Piedmont residual clays contain enough of these minerals to move measurably with the seasons. The plasticity index from laboratory testing indicates the potential: highly plastic clays can generate swell pressures greater than the weight of a light building. That is why the same red clay can be perfectly good bearing material when kept at constant moisture, and destructive when it cycles wet and dry.
Drilled piers or spread footings on red clay?
It depends on what the borings show. Spread footings work where tested, low-plasticity material lies below the active moisture zone at an economical depth. Drilled piers are the choice where expansive clay runs deep, where the active zone is thick, or where the structure cannot tolerate the residual movement shallow foundations allow. The geotechnical engineer's recommendation, based on borings and swell testing, settles the question for each site.
How does landscaping affect a foundation on expansive clay?
More than most owners expect. Trees and large shrubs pull significant moisture from the soil, and on expansive clay that localized drying causes settlement under one corner of a building while the rest stays put, which is the classic recipe for diagonal wall cracks. The fix is distance: keep large plantings outside the foundation's zone of influence, use root barriers where space is tight, and irrigate consistently so the moisture regime around the building stays as uniform as possible.
Does foundation permitting differ between Raleigh and Wake County?
The technical standard is the same, but the submittal goes to different reviewers: the City of Raleigh's Development Services for sites inside city limits, and Wake County's planning and inspections department for unincorporated sites. Both expect a geotechnical report backing the foundation design, with boring logs, laboratory results, and the engineer's bearing recommendations. The AHJ for the site's jurisdiction makes the final approval decision.
