What Structural Work Revitalizes Downtown Huntington WV Buildings?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Downtown Huntington WV buildings are revitalized structurally by assessing existing framing and masonry, upgrading floor systems for new commercial loads, stabilizing facades and parapets, reinforcing lateral systems for current code wind and seismic demands, and permitting the work through the City of Huntington's building department.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

From Assessment to Load Rating

The assessment phase sets the engineering basis for everything that follows. The engineer surveys every structural element--foundations, columns, beams, floors, roofs, walls, and the connections between them--and records member sizes, spans, material types, and visible distress. Where members are concealed, selective demolition opens representative areas rather than the whole building. Material properties come from testing where the budget allows: masonry prism tests, timber grading, or steel coupon tests. Where testing is not practical, the engineer uses the code's default properties for historic materials, which are deliberately conservative. The load rating translates that survey into numbers the owner can use. Each floor gets a posted capacity in pounds per square foot for the planned occupancy, and the engineer identifies exactly which elements control the rating--often a single line of undersized joists or a masonry pier with limited capacity. This lets the owner make informed decisions: reinforce the controlling elements, relocate heavy uses to stronger bays, or accept a lower rating. The rating report becomes the reference document for the architect's space planning and the building department's plan review.

Adaptive Reuse Upgrades That Pay Back

The upgrades that pay back fastest are the ones that unlock leasable space. Converting an upper floor to apartments typically needs floor strengthening, fire-rated separations, and new stairs or elevator shafts cut through existing structure--each opening engineered with headers, new supports, and shoring during construction. Ground-floor commercial space needs storefront lintel upgrades and often a new lateral element at the open front, where the original construction relied on the storefront itself for stiffness. The engineer sequences this work so the building stays stable at every stage, because removing a bearing wall before its replacement is in place is how partial collapses happen. Lateral and diaphragm work is the invisible backbone. Many Huntington buildings have wood floors that act as flexible diaphragms with no reliable connection to the brick walls; the retrofit adds plywood overlays, steel collectors, and wall anchors that create a complete load path. New braced frames or shear walls go in where the lateral demand exceeds what the old masonry can deliver, detailed to yield in a ductile manner rather than failing brittlely. The engineer coordinates all of this with the MEP design, because new ductwork and sprinkler mains compete for the same ceiling space the strengthening steel needs.

What else do project teams ask?

What is usually the weakest part of a 1900s downtown commercial building?
The connections: floor joists that merely bear on brick walls without mechanical anchorage, parapets with degraded mortar, and open storefronts with no lateral system. Floors are frequently undersized for modern commercial or residential loads as well. The structural assessment identifies which of these controls the building's safety and prioritizes the retrofit accordingly.
Can upper floors be converted to apartments without a full structural rebuild?
Usually, yes--with targeted upgrades. The engineer rates the existing floors, strengthens the controlling elements, designs new stair and elevator openings, and adds the fire separations and lateral upgrades the code requires for the occupancy change. A full rebuild is rarely needed; the assessment determines exactly which elements need work and which are adequate as-is.
How does permitting work for downtown Huntington revitalization projects?
Projects permit through the City of Huntington's building department, which reviews structural drawings for the occupancy change and the upgrade scope. If the building sits in a historic district or the project seeks historic tax credits, preservation review runs alongside the building permit. The authority having jurisdiction decides approvals--the engineer confirms the submittal requirements, review timelines, and inspection sequence before design begins.
Do revitalized buildings have to meet current building code fully?
Existing buildings are generally evaluated under the code's provisions for existing structures, which require upgrades triggered by the scope of work--such as an occupancy change or substantial alteration--rather than full compliance as if new. The engineer documents which provisions apply, designs the required upgrades, and states plainly what the existing construction was allowed to remain.
