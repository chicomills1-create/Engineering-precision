How Is Brewery Wastewater Handled in Asheville?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Brewery wastewater in Asheville is handled by screening and equalization tanks, pH neutralization, and pretreatment that trims high-strength organic loads before discharge to the municipal system under an industrial pretreatment permit -- reviewed by the City of Asheville or the Buncombe County water and sewer authority for the service area.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

What Makes Brewery Wastewater Hard to Treat

Three characteristics define the challenge: extreme organic strength, batch flow patterns, and pH swings. The organic strength comes from dissolved sugars, yeast, and fine solids -- the same compounds that make beer also make wastewater that can be hundreds of times stronger than domestic sewage in oxygen demand. Batch operations mean the waste arrives in slugs -- a fermenter dumped, a bright tank rinsed -- rather than the steady flow municipal plants prefer. And clean-in-place cycles alternate caustic soda with acid sanitizers, swinging the pH from strongly alkaline to strongly acidic within hours. The waste characterization study quantifies all three. The engineer samples across a full production cycle -- brew days, packaging days, deep-clean days -- measuring flow, pH, biochemical and chemical oxygen demand, suspended solids, and temperature. That dataset sizes the equalization tank (big enough to absorb the worst slug), the neutralization system (chemical feed sized for the worst pH excursion), and any biological or physical treatment (sized for the peak organic load). Undersizing any of them from average data is the classic brewery pretreatment failure: the system works fine on a quiet Tuesday and violates its permit on a double-batch Saturday.

Pretreatment System Design

The treatment train follows the waste's logic. Screening comes first -- coarse screens or rotary drum screens that pull out grain husks, hop debris, and trub before they can clog pumps and instruments downstream. The equalization tank is the heart of the system: mixed and aerated to prevent septicity, sized for at least a full day of peak flow, it turns the brewery's batch discharges into the steady, moderate-strength feed the rest of the train -- and the municipal plant -- can handle. Neutralization and polishing follow. A pH adjustment system with caustic and acid feed, controlled by inline probes, holds the effluent within the permit's pH band despite the cleaning cycle swings. Where the sewer authority's limits demand more, the design adds dissolved air flotation to remove fats, oils, and fine solids, or high-rate anaerobic treatment -- an upflow reactor that digests the dissolved organics and produces biogas the brewery can burn for heat. The engineer selects the train by working backward from the permit limits: the cheapest combination of unit processes that reliably meets every number, with redundancy on the components whose failure would cause an immediate violation.

Permitting and Discharge Compliance

The industrial pretreatment permit is the legal framework the engineering serves. Issued by the sewer authority, it sets the numeric limits -- flow caps, pH range, oxygen demand and solids loadings, sometimes temperature and phosphorus -- plus monitoring frequencies, sampling locations, and reporting duties. The engineer designs the permit into the site: a sampling manhole the authority can access, magnetic flow metering on the discharge, and the pretreatment equipment arranged so each unit process can be sampled and maintained. Ongoing compliance is an operations discipline the design must enable. The O&M manual spells out chemical handling, probe calibration, sludge wasting, and the record-keeping the permit requires; the brewery's staff -- brewers, not treatment operators -- need procedures simple enough to follow during a production rush. The engineer also builds in growth capacity, because a successful Asheville brewery expands, and every added barrel of capacity adds waste load. Designing for the five-year production plan costs marginally more up front and avoids a second pretreatment project -- or a consent order -- down the road.

What else do project teams ask?

Why can't breweries just send wastewater to the sewer?
Because of its strength. Brewery effluent can carry hundreds of times the organic load of domestic sewage, and discharged raw it would overwhelm the municipal plant's biological treatment and violate that plant's own discharge permit. Sewer authorities therefore require breweries to hold an industrial pretreatment permit and treat their waste down to permitted limits -- flow, pH, oxygen demand, and solids -- before it enters the public system.
What is an equalization tank?
A buffer tank that absorbs the brewery's batch discharges -- tank dumps, rinses, cleaning cycles -- and meters the wastewater out at a steady rate. Brewing is inherently batch work, but both the pretreatment equipment and the municipal plant perform best on steady flow. The EQ tank, typically sized for a full day of peak production and mixed to prevent odors, is what makes the rest of the treatment train work reliably.
What does pH neutralization do?
It corrects the extreme pH swings from brewery cleaning chemicals -- caustic soda one hour, acid sanitizer the next -- back into the near-neutral band the sewer permit requires and downstream biology needs. An automated feed system doses acid or caustic based on inline pH probes, because manual adjustment cannot keep up with batch cleaning cycles. Without it, the brewery would violate pH limits on nearly every cleaning day.
Who reviews brewery wastewater projects around Asheville?
It depends on the sewer service area. Breweries inside the City of Asheville work through the city's development review plus the sewer authority's industrial pretreatment program for the discharge permit. In unincorporated Buncombe County, county land-use review applies while the same sewer authority governs the pretreatment permit. The discharge permit -- with its numeric limits and monitoring duties -- is the controlling approval regardless of jurisdiction, and the authority having jurisdiction over land use decides the site and building approvals.
