How Does Wind Exposure Drive Design in Aurora and Arapahoe County?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Aurora and Arapahoe County's open plains typically warrant ASCE 7 wind Exposure C, raising design pressures. Engineers design the main wind-force-resisting system plus components and cladding for the site's exposure, with heavy attention to roof edge and corner suction zones, parapets, and fastening, while foundations separately handle the area's expansive clays.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Exposure Categories on the Eastern Plains

Choosing the exposure category is a site-specific engineering judgment with real cost consequences. On Aurora's eastern edge, where subdivisions meet open prairie, Exposure C is the defensible call: flat, open terrain with scattered obstructions lets the wind build to full strength at roof height. Closer in, where established neighborhoods provide dense, closely spaced obstructions, Exposure B may be justified, but the engineer documents the upwind terrain in each direction because wind does not care about the site plan. Assuming B on an exposed site under-designs every cladding connection on the building. The payoff shows up in the components-and-cladding design. Windows, storefronts, siding, roofing, and their attachments are designed for localized pressures that exceed the main system's average loads, with roof corners and edges zoned for the highest suctions. Parapets change the pressure patterns and must be designed for the wind loads they attract, not just built to a standard height. During construction, the partially built structure is at its most vulnerable, so the engineer specifies temporary bracing for the wind the open frame will see before the skin goes on.

Aurora City vs. Arapahoe County Review

The city-county line redraws the entire permit process. Inside Aurora, the city's Building Division administers the building code with city amendments, runs its own e-permit portal, and sets its own review timelines and fee schedules; larger commercial projects also move through the city's planning and development review. In unincorporated Arapahoe County, the county's Building Division is the AHJ, with county forms, county amendments, and review by county plans examiners. The structural wind design follows the same national standards either way, but submittal checklists, correction processes, and inspection scheduling do not. Aurora's growth pattern makes the jurisdiction check routine but essential. The city has annexed aggressively eastward, so parcels along the development frontier flip from county to city jurisdiction as annexations record, and a project designed under county assumptions can land in city review mid-stream. The assessor's records and the city's limit maps settle the question, and the design team confirms the AHJ before finalizing the permit set. Either agency decides what is approved on its side of the line.

What else do project teams ask?

What is wind exposure category?
It is the ASCE 7 classification of the terrain upwind of a building: B for suburban areas with dense obstructions, C for open terrain with scattered obstructions, and D for flat unobstructed expanses. The category adjusts the velocity pressure in the wind calculation. Much of Aurora's eastern plains warrants Exposure C.
What are components and cladding?
Components and cladding are the building elements that receive wind directly: roofing, windows, doors, siding, soffits, and their attachments. They are designed for higher localized pressures than the main wind-force-resisting system because gusts hit the skin harder than the frame's average. Their fastening details are where wind failures usually start.
Why do roof edges and corners need stronger fastening?
Wind accelerating over a building separates at the edges and corners, creating suction peaks far above the field-of-roof pressures. The code zones the roof accordingly, with edge and corner zones designed for the highest uplifts. Enhanced fastening, perimeter securement, and rated edge metal keep the roof covering attached where the wind pulls hardest.
Do chinook winds change the structural design?
The code's basic wind speeds already reflect the Front Range wind climate, including chinook events and thunderstorm downbursts. The engineer's work is in the application: correct exposure category, topographic effects where terrain funnels wind, and cladding details that handle gusty, turbulent flow. There is no separate chinook provision; there is careful use of the standard ones.
Who permits a project in unincorporated Arapahoe County?
The Arapahoe County Building Division handles permits for unincorporated areas, with its own portal, amendments, fee schedule, and inspection process. Projects inside Aurora city limits go to the City of Aurora's Building Division instead. Confirm the parcel's jurisdiction with the assessor's records before designing, especially near the annexation frontier.
