How Are Duress Alarm Systems Designed for Courthouse Staff?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Engineers place fixed and wireless duress devices at every confrontation-prone position, zone each alarm to an exact location on the security display, integrate with CCTV and door control, and supervise every circuit and battery.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Device Placement and Zoning

Placement follows the threat: anywhere a staff member faces the public in a position of authority gets a device. Judges' benches, clerk and cashier counters, chambers desks, jury staff positions, screening lanes, the sally port transfer path, and holding area control points are the standard list, extended by the facility's own security assessment. Fixed buttons mount where a hand falls naturally — under a counter lip or desk edge — so activation is discreet and does not require looking away from the threat. Wireless pendants cover mobile staff: bailiffs, transport officers, and facilities personnel who move between zones. Each device — fixed or wireless — is its own alarm zone with a plain-language location label, so the dispatcher sees 'Courtroom 3 — judge's bench' rather than 'Zone 47.' The engineer documents the full device schedule with locations, and the security station's graphics show every device on the floor plan for instant visual dispatch.

Integration, Power, and Supervision

A duress alarm that only makes noise at a panel is half a system. The design integrates the alarm with CCTV — the nearest cameras automatically display and record on activation — and with access control, so the response plan can execute door actions like releasing a staff-only escape route. Where the jurisdiction uses mass notification, the design defines exactly what, if anything, is announced publicly; most response plans keep the alarm silent to avoid escalating the situation. Every circuit is supervised: hardwired loops report opens, shorts, and tampering, and wireless transmitters send regular check-in signals with battery status. The head-end and all field devices ride on emergency power with battery backup sized for the required standby duration. Commissioning activates every single device and verifies the full chain — button to panel to camera to dispatcher display — and the owner gets a written test schedule, because supervision electronics only help if someone acts on what they report.

Duress Alarm Design Checklist

A complete duress design puts silent help within reach everywhere it is needed: • Fixed devices at benches, counters, chambers, screening, and transfer paths • Wireless pendants for mobile staff with supervised check-in and battery reporting • Every device zoned to a plain-language location on the dispatcher's floor plan • CCTV auto-display and recording triggered by each alarm • Door control actions per the response plan (escape routes, containment) • Supervised circuits and emergency power for the full system • Commissioning activation of every device plus a written recurring test schedule

What else do project teams ask?

Where should duress buttons be placed in a courtroom?
At minimum under the judge's bench and at the clerk's station, mounted where a hand can reach them without visible movement — under a counter lip or desk edge. Depending on the court's security assessment, bailiff positions and witness stand areas may also get devices. Each button is zoned to its exact location so dispatch knows precisely where help is needed, and placement is reviewed with the court's security staff, who know where incidents actually start.
Should duress alarms be silent or audible?
Almost always silent at the point of activation. An audible alarm at the scene can escalate a confrontation and tells the aggressor that help is coming, which may provoke rather than deter. The alarm annunciates at the security station and to responders — often with CCTV auto-display — while the response plan defines any public notification separately. The engineer designs the annunciation path with the security team so silence at the scene never means silence everywhere.
How are wireless duress pendants kept reliable?
With supervised transmitters that check in to the panel on a schedule — if a pendant stops checking in, the system reports a trouble condition before anyone needs it. Low-battery reporting gives advance warning, and the radio survey during design verifies coverage in every corner of the building, including stairwells and parking areas. Pendants are tested on a schedule like fixed devices, because a dead pendant is worse than no pendant: it creates false confidence.
What happens when a duress button is pressed?
The panel identifies the exact device location, displays it on the dispatcher's floor plan, triggers nearby CCTV cameras to display and record, and executes the programmed response — which may include door control actions and responder notification per the facility's plan. The full sequence is designed with the court's security team and proven at commissioning by activating every device and watching the entire chain respond.
