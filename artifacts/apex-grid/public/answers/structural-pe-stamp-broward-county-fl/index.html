Structural PE Stamp in Broward County, FL: HVHZ Rules Explained?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

A structural PE stamp in Broward County certifies the gravity, lateral, and foundation systems after a Florida-licensed PE verifies the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone wind design under responsible charge — with the state's strictest wind pressures, continuous load paths, and product-approved envelope components, submitted to the county or the municipality that owns the site.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

HVHZ wind design: the strictest review in Florida

The structural review in Broward County starts and ends with wind. I verify the HVHZ design criteria are correctly established for the site — wind speed, exposure category, topographic effects — and check the main wind-force-resisting system analysis against them. Then the component and cladding pressures get their own pass, because the HVHZ assigns the state's highest localized pressures to roofs, walls, and openings, and underestimating them is the most common failure I see in out-of-town designs. The load path review is link-by-link: roof covering attachment, roof-to-wall connections, wall-to-foundation anchorage, shear wall holdowns, and foundation uplift resistance, each detailed with calculated capacities. HVHZ plan check is the most experienced wind review in the state — the reviewers have seen every shortcut — so the PE's pre-seal review has to be equally rigorous. Anything the drawings leave to the contractor's judgment on the wind path comes back as a correction, and in the HVHZ that correction list is long for vague sets.

Navigating 32 possible AHJs

Broward County's permitting landscape is the most fragmented in this batch: 31 municipal building departments plus the county, each with its own intake portal, checklist, fee schedule, and correction conventions, all under the county-wide code administration of the Board of Rules and Appeals. The engineering does not change across these jurisdictions — responsible charge, verified calculations, marked revisions, then the seal — but the submittal package must be assembled for the AHJ that will actually review it. My groundwork on every Broward project is a parcel-level jurisdiction confirmation before design begins, because guessing wrong here means re-submitting into a completely different department's workflow. Once confirmed, I scope the package to that AHJ's intake standards: the seal block, the calculation package organization, the product approval documentation format. A complete first submittal, built for the right AHJ, is the fastest path through any of the county's 32 doors.

Broward County structural submittal checklist

A structural package is ready for the PE's review — and then for the AHJ — when these items are resolved. HVHZ wind completeness is the thread through all of them. • AHJ confirmed by parcel: Broward County vs the specific municipality (31 possibilities) • Florida-licensed PE in responsible charge of the structural review • HVHZ wind design criteria, exposure, and component/cladding pressures shown and verified • Continuous roof-to-foundation load path with every connection detailed for calculated forces • Product approvals for envelope components verified against the design pressures • Geotechnical report with site-specific foundation recommendations; uplift resistance verified • Flood data and equipment elevations coordinated where the maps apply

What else do project teams ask?

What is the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone and why does it matter?
The HVHZ — covering Broward and Miami-Dade counties — is the wind region with the strictest requirements in the Florida Building Code. It mandates the state's highest design wind pressures, strict protocols for the building envelope, and product approval requirements for components like windows, doors, roofing, and cladding that must demonstrate tested wind resistance. For the structural review this means the wind analysis, the load path detailing, and the envelope coordination all face a higher bar than anywhere else in Florida. The PE verifies the HVHZ criteria are correctly applied to the site and that every element the code's HVHZ provisions touch is addressed on the sealed drawings.
How do I know which AHJ reviews my Broward County project?
Broward County has 31 municipalities, each with its own building department, plus the county's own building code services for unincorporated areas — so the parcel's jurisdiction must be confirmed, not assumed. Fort Lauderdale, Hollywood, Pompano Beach, Coral Springs, and the others each run separate intake portals and checklists, and the Broward County Board of Rules and Appeals overlays county-wide code administration. I verify by parcel at kickoff and confirm with the AHJ where boundaries are ambiguous. The engineering review is the same regardless, but the submittal mechanics follow the jurisdiction — and there are 32 possible answers in this county.
What does product approval coordination involve in the structural review?
In the HVHZ, envelope components must carry Florida product approvals demonstrating tested wind performance, and the structural set has to coordinate with them. The PE verifies that the specified windows, doors, roofing systems, and cladding carry valid approvals for the design pressures, that the approvals' installation requirements — fastening schedules, anchorage, substrates — are reflected in the structural details, and that the approved pressures meet or exceed what the wind analysis demands. A beautiful window schedule with approvals rated below the site's design pressure is a correction; worse, it is a building that cannot legally be built as drawn. I check approvals against pressures before the seal goes on.
What foundation conditions does Broward County's ground create?
South Florida ground is sand, limestone, and a high water table — the geotechnical report drives everything. The PE verifies the foundation design against the report: bearing conditions, settlement expectations, and deep-foundation requirements where the near-surface soils cannot carry the loads. In the HVHZ the foundation review also covers uplift resistance, because the wind loads trying to lift the building have to be delivered into ground that can hold them — holdowns and anchorage are only as good as the foundation they attach to. Flood-zone foundations get coordinated with the flood maps where applicable. The sealed foundation sheets reference the report they were designed from.
