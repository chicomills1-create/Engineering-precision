How Is Knoxville Building Design Shaped by the Tennessee River and TVA Dams?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Knoxville flood design starts with FEMA maps that set the base flood elevation, then elevates finished floors above it with flood-resistant construction below and elevation certificates documenting compliance. TVA dams moderate river flows but do not remove floodplain regulation. City projects permit through Knoxville Plans Review and Inspections; county projects go through Knox County Codes Enforcement.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

FEMA Maps, Base Flood Elevation, and Freeboard

The flood insurance rate map is the legal geography of flood risk. Its zones tell the engineer what rules apply: high-risk zones where elevation or floodproofing is mandatory, and shaded or unshaded moderate zones where lenders may still require coverage. The base flood elevation printed on the map is the water surface of the one-percent-annual-chance flood, and every flood design decision hangs from that number. Freeboard, the extra elevation above the base flood elevation that a jurisdiction requires, is where local rules bite. It buys margin against map uncertainty, future development upstream, and events bigger than the mapped flood, and it usually pays for itself in lower flood insurance premiums. The surveyor's elevation certificate is the proof document: it records the building's lowest floor elevation against the base flood elevation so the AHJ and the insurer can verify compliance without re-surveying the project.

TVA Operations, Tributaries, and Stormwater

TVA's reservoir operations shape the river Knoxville sees day to day, holding back flood storage in the wet season and releasing it in a controlled way. But tributary flooding does not wait for the main river: intense storms can flood First Creek, Second Creek, and the smaller streams through Knoxville's neighborhoods while the Tennessee River itself stays in its banks. The floodplain maps capture both, and the engineer checks the site against the tributary panels as carefully as the river panels. Stormwater regulation is the parallel track. Knoxville's and Knox County's stormwater programs require new development to manage peak runoff and water quality, typically with detention basins, underground systems, or green infrastructure sized to local design storms. A site outside the mapped floodplain still needs a full stormwater design, and a site inside it needs both floodplain compliance and stormwater compliance coordinated so the detention design does not conflict with the floodproofing. The civil engineer sequences these early because they set grading, which sets everything else.

What else do project teams ask?

Do TVA dams eliminate flood risk in Knoxville?
No. TVA dams provide significant flood control storage, but they are designed for specific events and operating rules, and tributary streams can flood independently of the main river. FEMA floodplain maps still govern construction near the river and its creeks. Engineers design to the mapped base flood elevation regardless of dam operations upstream.
What is base flood elevation?
Base flood elevation is the computed water surface elevation of the one-percent-annual-chance flood, shown on FEMA flood insurance rate maps. It is the reference height for floodplain construction: lowest floors must be at or above it, with many jurisdictions requiring additional freeboard. A licensed surveyor documents the building's elevation against it on an elevation certificate.
What is the difference between dry and wet floodproofing?
Dry floodproofing seals a nonresidential building so floodwater cannot enter, using reinforced walls, sealed openings, and closures designed for water pressure. Wet floodproofing allows water to enter and exit sacrificial areas like parking garages through vents, equalizing pressure so walls are not damaged. Residential buildings generally must be elevated rather than dry-floodproofed.
Who handles permits for a Knoxville floodplain project?
Projects inside Knoxville city limits permit through the city's Plans Review and Inspections office, which enforces the city's floodplain ordinance. Projects in unincorporated Knox County go through Knox County Codes Enforcement and Engineering under the county's ordinance. The AHJ decides what is approved, so the engineer confirms which office has the parcel before designing to its requirements.
Does stormwater design apply outside the floodplain?
Yes. Stormwater quantity and quality rules apply to new development across Knoxville and Knox County regardless of floodplain location. The civil engineer designs detention and treatment for the site's runoff, and on floodplain sites coordinates that design with the floodproofing so the two systems do not conflict.
