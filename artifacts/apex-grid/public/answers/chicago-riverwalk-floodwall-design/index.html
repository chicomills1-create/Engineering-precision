How Are Chicago Riverwalk Floodwalls Designed?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Chicago Riverwalk floodwalls are designed by analyzing river flood levels, wave action, and soil conditions, then building reinforced concrete or sheet-pile walls integrated with the walkway, gates, and drainage so the riverfront stays open while the wall holds back design floodwaters.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Flood Forces on the Riverwalk

The Chicago River's flood behavior is engineered already - and that complicates the design. The river's flow is managed by the lock at its mouth and control structures upstream, so flood levels depend on operations as well as rainfall; the engineer studies the regulated system, not just a natural river, to establish the design flood elevation. Wave action from wind and boat wakes adds dynamic force on the wall face, and flood-borne debris - everything from branches to dock sections - impacts the wall and gates with forces the design must absorb. Ice in winter adds its own loading on exposed wall sections, a Great Lakes reality that riverfront designs ignore at their peril. The soil-structure interaction decides the wall type and foundation. Geotechnical investigation along the alignment reveals the riverbank soils - often fill over soft clays in the downtown reach - and the engineer designs foundations that reach reliable bearing, whether that means deep wall embedment, piles, or a widened footing. Seepage under the wall during a flood is analyzed too: water pressure beneath the wall reduces its effective weight and can pipe soil from under the toe, so cutoff walls or seepage collars extend the water's path and keep the foundation stable. Each of these analyses goes into the permit submittals, where the reviewing agencies check the flood elevations and stability calculations against the adopted standards.

Designing a Wall People Walk On

A riverwalk floodwall is public realm first and infrastructure second, which shapes every detail. The wall's river face carries architectural finishes - form liners, stone veneer, or board-formed concrete - detailed so the finish does not compromise the structural section or create maintenance traps where water and deicing salts collect. The top of the wall becomes seating, planting, and walking surface, so the structural design accounts for pedestrian surcharge loads, and railings meet the required heights and loading while preserving river views. Expansion joints are placed to control cracking from thermal movement and settlement, sealed against water intrusion, and detailed so they do not trip pedestrians. Operations and maintenance are designed in, not added later. Closure gates need clear deployment procedures, trained crews, and storage that keeps the gates accessible and undamaged - a gate that cannot be installed before the flood is not flood protection. Pump stations get redundant pumps, backup power, and remote monitoring so a single failure does not flood the walkway from behind. And the entire system gets an inspection and maintenance program: concrete condition surveys, gate exercise schedules, and drain cleaning, because a floodwall's reliability on the day of the flood is determined by the maintenance of the decade before.

What else do project teams ask?

How high are Chicago Riverwalk floodwalls?
Wall heights follow the design flood elevation plus freeboard at each location along the river - there is no single height. The engineer establishes the flood elevation from gage records, watershed analysis, and regulatory floodplain maps for the river's managed system, then sets the wall crest above it. Walkway grades and access points are arranged around those elevations.
What are flood closure gates?
Closure gates seal the openings in a floodwall - stairs, ramps, and access points - before floodwaters arrive. They range from stop logs stacked into slots to hinged swing gates to demountable panels, all designed for the same hydrostatic forces as the wall itself. Gates require storage, deployment procedures, and regular exercise to be reliable when needed.
Why does the design consider water receding quickly?
Rapid drawdown is often the governing stability case. When floodwaters fall fast, the saturated soil behind the wall keeps pushing while the balancing water pressure in front disappears. The engineer checks the wall's sliding and overturning stability for this condition, which can be more severe than the full-flood case.
Which agencies permit riverfront floodwall work in Chicago?
Permitting typically involves the City of Chicago for building and zoning approval, the Metropolitan Water Reclamation District for work affecting the waterway, and federal review for construction in navigable waters. Each has its own application, technical standards, and review timeline. The authority having jurisdiction at each level must approve the work, so the engineer sequences the submittals early in the project.
