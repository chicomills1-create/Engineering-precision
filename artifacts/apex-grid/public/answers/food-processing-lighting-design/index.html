Food Plant Lighting Design: Levels, Shatterproof & Washdown

Food plant lighting serves production, inspection, and sanitation — three users with different needs from the same fixtures. Production needs task-appropriate light levels; inspectors need 50 footcandles at inspection stations and the color quality to see defects; the sanitation crew needs fixtures that survive the nightly hose-down. And over every square foot of exposed product, the fixtures must be shatterproof. Here's how lighting engineers design for all of it.

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran

Levels, color quality, and the inspection task

Light levels start with the tasks: general processing at 30–50 footcandles, inspection and quality stations higher, with FSIS's 50-footcandle standard at inspection stations as the regulatory anchor. Warehouses and dry storage run lower; packaging and labeling — where label accuracy and seal inspection matter — run at the higher end. The lighting calculation lays out fixture locations, mounting heights, and predicted levels on the working plane; it's an engineering deliverable, not a fixture count.

Color quality affects inspection: CRI (color rendering index) and color temperature determine whether a sorter or inspector can see the defect, the discoloration, the foreign material. Specify CRI 80+ and appropriate color temperature for inspection areas — the contractor's default 5000K low-CRI high-bay may meet the footcandle count while failing the actual task. Lighting for inspection is optical engineering, not just illumination.

Shatterproof and washdown-rated: the fixture standard

Shatter protection is non-negotiable over exposed product: shatterproof lenses, coated or sleeved lamps, fixtures designed so that no glass or plastic fragment can reach food if the fixture fails. Both FDA cGMPs and USDA-FSIS require it. The protection is integral to the fixture specification — not a field add-on — and the submittal review verifies it. In USDA plants, the inspector looks up; make sure what's overhead passes.

Washdown rating completes the fixture specification: sealed LED high-bays rated IP65/IP66 or NEMA 4X, with smooth housings that don't harbor and mounting that survives the hose stream. LED dominates new construction — efficient, long-lived (reducing the maintenance traffic over product), instant restrike, and dimmable for areas with varying needs. Fluorescent persists in existing plants but loses on every metric that matters in a washdown environment.

Emergency lighting, controls, and commissioning

Emergency lighting in food plants follows the same washdown discipline as general lighting: sealed, corrosion-resistant emergency fixtures and exit signs in wet areas, because a corroded emergency light that fails during a power event is both a life-safety failure and an inspection finding. Battery units get located where maintenance can reach them without crossing product zones; generator-backed egress lighting gets the same fixture standard.

Controls round out the design: occupancy and daylight harvesting in warehouses and offices, task-appropriate switching in processing (the sanitation crew needs full light; the off-shift needs safe egress levels), and integration with the building management system for monitoring. Commissioning verifies the design — measured light levels against the calculation, emergency system testing, and controls checkout. Lighting is the most visible engineering system in the plant; make it look engineered.

Frequently Asked Questions

What light levels do food plants need?

FSIS applies a 50-footcandle standard at inspection stations; general processing areas typically design to 30–50 footcandles, with higher levels at inspection and quality stations. The lighting calculation documents compliance — the inspector may ask for it.

Is shatter protection required?

Yes — shatterproof lenses, coatings, or sleeves are required over exposed product under both FDA and USDA rules. A broken lamp over a line is a contamination event; the protection is cheap insurance designed into every fixture.

LED or fluorescent in food plants?

Sealed LED high-bays (IP65/66 or NEMA 4X) dominate new food plants: efficient, long-lived, instant-on, and available in washdown-rated housings. The color temperature and CRI affect inspection quality — specify them, don't accept the contractor's default.

Does emergency lighting need washdown rating?

Yes — and it's one of the most common lighting failures. Emergency fixtures in washdown zones need the same sealed, corrosion-resistant construction as the general lighting, or they'll fail when they're needed most.
