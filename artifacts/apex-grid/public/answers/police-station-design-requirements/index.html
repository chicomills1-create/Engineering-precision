What Are the Design Requirements for a Police Station?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

A police station is designed around sally-port vehicle security, climate-controlled evidence storage with chain-of-custody protection, ligature-resistant detention MEP, and emergency power sized to keep dispatch, security, and holding areas operating through any outage.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Sally port and secure vehicle entry

I design the sally port from the vehicle outward: the design transport vehicle sets the dimensions, turning paths, and door sizes, and everything else follows. The doors are interlocked through the security system so the inner and outer doors can never be open simultaneously, with manual override provisions the agency's procedures require. Exhaust ventilation handles vehicle fumes in the enclosed space, and the drainage design handles washdown of the sally port floor — a space that gets dirty in the course of its work and needs to be hosed out. Beyond the sally port, the secure circulation is a planning discipline the MEP design respects: detainee movement, staff movement, and public movement stay separated, and the mechanical, electrical, and plumbing systems are routed and zoned to match. I keep security-system infrastructure — conduit, pathways, and equipment space for access control, intrusion, and video — coordinated in the design from the start, because retrofitting security pathways into a finished secure facility is disruptive and expensive.

Evidence storage: climate and security

The property and evidence areas get their own HVAC zones with the temperature and humidity control the stored materials require, segregated sub-zones for narcotics, firearms, currency, and general property, and dedicated refrigeration or freezer capacity for biological and DNA evidence. I put these zones on emergency power with environmental monitoring and alarming — temperature, humidity, and system-fault alarms that reach the right people — because the cost of an undetected environmental failure is measured in lost cases, not just spoiled material. Security integration is designed alongside the MEP: access-controlled entries with audit trails, intrusion detection, and video coverage coordinated through the electrical and communications design. The layout supports the chain of custody — intake, processing, and storage in a controlled sequence — and I design the lighting, ventilation, and finishes for a working vault: durable, maintainable, and secure. Evidence technicians work in these rooms every day, so the design serves their workflow as well as the evidence.

Holding areas, detention MEP, and emergency power

Holding and detention areas get security-grade everything: ligature-resistant plumbing fixtures and accessories, tamper-resistant diffusers, luminaires, and sprinkler heads, and ventilation designed so airflow cannot be defeated from inside the space. I locate every serviceable component outside the secure envelope — valves, dampers, and controls are accessed from corridors or chases, never from inside an occupied holding area. Finishes are abuse-resistant and the lighting is designed for both security observation and humane conditions. Emergency power ties the whole facility together. The generator plant is sized from the continuity analysis: dispatch, communications, security systems, detention ventilation and lighting, evidence climate control, and data systems — everything the agency needs to keep operating. Fuel storage matches the required runtime, and the distribution design segregates critical loads so a fault in a non-critical area cannot take down dispatch. I also design the grounding and surge protection for the communications and data systems, because a station's radios and 911 equipment are only as reliable as the power feeding them.

What else do project teams ask?

What is a sally port and how is it designed?
A sally port is a secure, enclosed vehicle vestibule at the detention intake: the transport vehicle drives in, the exterior door closes behind it, and only then does the interior door open — so there is never a direct path from the secure interior to the outside. I design it with interlocked doors coordinated with the access-control system, exhaust ventilation for vehicle fumes in the enclosed space, washdown drainage, and video coverage. The sally port is sized for the transport vehicles the agency uses, with turning clearances verified, because a sally port that cannot fit the van is a design failure.
Why does evidence storage need climate control?
Because evidence degrades. Biological and DNA evidence needs refrigeration or freezing; drugs and general property need stable temperature and humidity to preserve their condition for trial; and the chain of custody requires documented, continuous environmental control. I design the property and evidence areas with zoned HVAC — including dedicated refrigeration for biological evidence — on emergency power, with temperature and humidity monitoring and alarming. Losing evidence to an environmental failure can collapse a prosecution, so the design treats climate control as a mission system, not a comfort system.
What makes detention holding MEP different?
Everything must be ligature-resistant, tamper-proof, and maintainable from outside the cell. Plumbing fixtures are designed so they cannot be used for self-harm; diffusers, lights, and sprinkler heads are security-grade and tamper-resistant; ventilation is arranged so it cannot be blocked from inside the holding area; and all serviceable components — valves, controls, access panels — are located outside the secure space so maintenance never requires entering an occupied cell. I also design for the applicable detention standards, which layer requirements on top of the base building code for holding areas.
How is emergency power sized for a police station?
For continuous operations, not just life safety. Dispatch and 911 call-taking, radio and communications systems, security/access control/video, detention-area ventilation and lighting, evidence-area climate control, and the data and server rooms all ride on emergency power. I build the load analysis from the agency's continuity requirements — what must stay up, and for how long — and size generation plus fuel storage to match. The transfer scheme is designed so critical systems see no meaningful interruption, with UPS bridging the generator start for dispatch and communications.
