What Does Texas Windstorm (TWIA) Compliance Require?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Texas Windstorm (TWIA) compliance requires designing coastal structures for hurricane wind and wind-driven rain under the Texas Department of Insurance windstorm program, using approved products and construction methods, documenting the design with certified inspections at key construction stages, and obtaining a Certificate of Compliance (WPI-8) before windstorm insurance coverage can be issued.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

The Continuous Load Path and Tested Products

Every TWIA-compliant structure is engineered as a chain of connections, each link verified. Roof decking is fastened to framing at the spacing and with the fastener type the wind analysis requires - in high-pressure zones at roof edges and corners, that spacing tightens considerably. Framing connects to walls with rated hurricane ties and straps, walls anchor to the foundation with bolts or straps sized for uplift, and the foundation itself is designed for the overturning the wind delivers. The engineer sizes each connection from the ASCE 7 wind pressures for the site's exposure and the building's geometry, then details them so the ductile elements yield before brittle ones fail. Products in the building envelope carry their own compliance burden. Windows, doors, skylights, garage doors, and roof coverings must have current product approvals demonstrating they resist the design wind pressures, including resistance to wind-borne debris where the program requires opening protection. The contractor must install each product per the manufacturer's approved instructions and keep the approval documentation on site for the inspector. Substitutions during construction are common and dangerous to certification: swapping an approved window for an unlisted equivalent voids that opening's compliance, and the inspector will flag it.

Staged Inspections, the WPI-8, and Dual Permitting

The inspection sequence follows construction: foundation and anchor bolt placement before the pour, framing connections and hold-downs before drywall, roof deck attachment before the covering goes on, and opening protection installation as windows and doors are set. Each stage needs sign-off by a TDI-qualified inspector, and the timing is unforgiving - once finishes cover the connections, that stage generally cannot be certified. The WPI-8, the Certificate of Compliance, is issued only when every required stage is documented and the structure matches the approved design. For existing structures, a separate certification path exists for qualifying alterations and re-roofs, with its own inspection requirements. Owners should plan for two permitting tracks from day one. The local authority having jurisdiction - the city building department or the county - issues the building permit, performs code inspections, and grants the certificate of occupancy under its own timeline and portal. The TDI windstorm program independently certifies the structure for insurance eligibility. The tracks run in parallel and neither controls the other: a city final inspection does not produce a WPI-8, and a WPI-8 does not substitute for the city's approvals. Starting both early, with the engineer coordinating the structural design to satisfy both sets of requirements at once, is how coastal projects avoid expensive rework.

What else do project teams ask?

What is the WPI-8?
The WPI-8 is the Certificate of Compliance issued under the Texas Department of Insurance windstorm program. It certifies that a structure was designed and built to the program's windstorm requirements, verified through staged inspections. TWIA cannot issue wind and hail coverage without it, which makes it effectively mandatory for insurable coastal construction in the designated catastrophe area.
Which areas require TWIA compliance?
The designated catastrophe area covers the 14 Texas coastal counties plus parts of Harris County. Within that area, property owners seeking windstorm and hail coverage through TWIA need certified construction. Even owners using private wind coverage benefit from the same engineering, since the design and inspection discipline produces a more storm-resistant building regardless of the insurer.
Can an existing coastal home get windstorm certification?
There are certification paths for existing structures, typically tied to qualifying alterations such as a full re-roof or structural retrofit. The requirements focus on what can be verified: roof deck attachment, opening protection, and accessible connections. An engineer evaluates the structure, designs the upgrades, and the work goes through the program's inspection stages. Not every older home can be certified economically - the inspection may reveal connection deficiencies that are expensive to correct behind finished walls.
Do I need both a city permit and windstorm certification?
Yes, they are separate. The city or county building department issues the building permit and certificate of occupancy under the building code, with its own submittal process and inspectors. The TDI windstorm program separately certifies the structure for TWIA insurance eligibility. Both authorities review against their own requirements and each decides its own approvals - one does not substitute for the other.
