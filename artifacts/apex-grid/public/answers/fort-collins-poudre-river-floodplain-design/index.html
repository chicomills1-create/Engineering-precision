How Is Development Designed for the Poudre River Floodplain in Fort Collins?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Poudre River floodplain development follows FEMA maps plus Fort Collins' strict local floodplain rules: lowest floors elevated above the base flood elevation with freeboard, zero-rise certification for any floodway work, compensatory storage for fill, and a floodplain use permit that must clear before the building permit.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Floodway vs. Floodplain Fringe: What Can Be Built Where

The floodway is the no-build heart of the system. It carries the deepest, fastest water, and federal rules adopted locally prohibit any development that would raise the base flood elevation, even by a fraction of a foot. Bridges, trails, and utility crossings can cross it only with engineering that proves no rise, and buildings are effectively excluded. The floodway boundary is drawn on the FEMA maps, and Fort Collins enforces it strictly because encroachment there raises flood levels for everyone upstream. The floodplain fringe allows building under conditions designed to keep people and property above the water. Residential lowest floors go above the base flood elevation plus freeboard, the safety margin the city requires above the mapped flood level. Enclosures below the elevated floor are limited to parking, access, and storage, built with flood-resistant materials and fitted with openings that let water in and out to equalize pressure. Fill in the fringe must be balanced with compensatory excavation in many cases, so the project does not displace floodwater onto neighbors. Every one of these conditions is verified in the field and documented before a certificate of occupancy issues.

The Fort Collins Floodplain Review Path

Floodplain review in Fort Collins starts before the building permit application, and it follows its own track. The owner or engineer confirms the flood zone from the effective FEMA Flood Insurance Rate Map, then prepares the floodplain use permit submittal: site plans showing flood zone boundaries, proposed finished-floor elevations, foundation details, and, where required, a hydrologic and hydraulic analysis. The city reviews the submittal against both FEMA minimums and its stricter local standards, and no building permit issues until the floodplain review clears. Construction brings its own documentation. An elevation certificate is typically required when the lowest floor is set and again at completion, proving the building was actually built above the required elevation. For projects that alter the floodplain, a Conditional Letter of Map Revision goes to FEMA before construction and a final Letter of Map Revision after, formally updating the maps. Lenders, insurers, and future buyers all rely on these documents, so the paperwork is as much a part of the project as the concrete.

What else do project teams ask?

What is the difference between the floodway and the flood fringe?
The floodway is the channel and adjacent land needed to pass floodwater; development there is effectively prohibited unless it proves zero rise in flood levels. The flood fringe is the rest of the mapped floodplain, where building is allowed if lowest floors are elevated above the base flood elevation plus freeboard and other conditions are met. The FEMA maps draw the line between them.
What does no-rise certification mean?
It is an engineering certification, backed by hydraulic modeling, that a proposed project in the floodway will not raise the base flood elevation at any point. Even tiny increases are prohibited because they compound upstream. A licensed professional prepares the analysis and stamps the certification for the floodplain review.
What is an elevation certificate?
It is a FEMA form documenting a building's elevations relative to the base flood elevation, including the lowest floor and mechanical equipment. Fort Collins typically requires one when the lowest floor is constructed and another at completion. Insurers use it to rate flood insurance, and the city uses it to verify the building met its elevation conditions.
Can you build in the floodplain at all?
Yes, in the flood fringe, with permits and conditions: elevated lowest floors, flood-resistant materials below, proper openings in enclosures, and compensatory storage where fill is placed. In the floodway, new buildings are effectively excluded. A floodplain use permit from the city is required before the building permit, and the AHJ decides what is approved.
Why are Fort Collins floodplain rules so strict?
The city rewrote its approach after the July 1997 Spring Creek flash flood, which killed five people and caused devastating damage, and the 2013 Front Range floods reinforced the lesson. Fort Collins adopted local standards that exceed national minimums in several areas. The rules reflect a deliberate policy: the Poudre and its tributaries will flood again, and buildings must be designed for it.
