Structural PE Stamp for Denver County, CO Building Permits

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Denver's consolidated city-county government permits through Community Planning & Development, and structural sets require Colorado-licensed PE review-and-seal — design to the Denver Building Code amendments (wind, expansive soils, snow/seismic), submit electronically, and seal only after a complete engineering review.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Designing to the Denver Building Code

The Denver Building Code's amendments are not cosmetic — they change structural design decisions. Wind load provisions reflect Front Range exposure, foundation requirements address the expansive soils found across much of the metro, and snow and seismic criteria are tuned to local conditions rather than national defaults. I start every Denver structural project by pulling the current adopted edition and the full amendment package from CPD, then build the calculation set around it. Designing to the base IBC and hoping the amendments do not matter is a strategy that fails at first review, every time.

What our structural review-and-seal covers

Our Colorado-licensed PEs take responsible charge of every Denver structural set we seal: complete drawing review against the Denver Building Code and amendments, verification or performance of gravity and lateral calculations, foundation design checked against the geotechnical report, and coordination with architectural and MEP disciplines. Every required revision is marked and made before the seal goes on. Denver's reviewers read calculations, so our review reads them first — the sealed set that reaches CPD is engineering a licensed professional has verified, which is exactly what the seal certifies.

Denver structural submittal checklist

A Denver structural permit submittal is ready when these items are resolved. CPD's review rewards complete, calculation-backed sets. • Parcel confirmed inside Denver proper (consolidated city-county) vs. adjacent counties • Current Denver Building Code edition and all amendments identified and designed to • Structural calculations covering gravity, lateral, and foundations, checked against geotech • Colorado PE seal, signature, and date on every structural sheet, portal-ready digital format • Correction-response discipline planned: resubmittals checked against reviewer comments line by line

What else do project teams ask?

Is Denver a city or a county for building permits?
Both — Denver is a consolidated city and county, so the Department of Community Planning & Development is the single AHJ. There is no separate Denver County building department to navigate. I still verify parcel jurisdiction on every project, because parcels near the county line can sit in adjacent jurisdictions (Adams, Arapahoe, or Jefferson County), but for sites inside Denver proper, CPD is the reviewer, the portal, and the inspector. One amendment package, one submittal path — the engineering quality of the set is what determines review speed.
Does Denver require a Colorado PE seal on structural drawings?
Yes — structural engineering documents for Denver permits must be sealed by a Colorado-licensed Professional Engineer in responsible charge of the work, with seal, signature, and date on the structural sheets. The seal certifies the engineer reviewed the design and takes professional responsibility for it, and Denver's reviewers treat it as a substantive certification: they check the calculations. Our Colorado PEs perform the full review — calculations verified against the Denver Building Code, revisions marked — before anything is sealed.
What Denver amendments affect structural design?
The Denver Building Code adopts the IBC with city amendments that address Front Range conditions: wind provisions, expansive-soil foundation requirements, and snow and seismic criteria tuned to the local hazard profile. The amendments are published by CPD, and I confirm the current adopted edition and amendment package at the start of every project. Structural calculations must reflect the package in force on the submittal date — Denver reviewers check calculations against current amendments, and a set designed to an outdated package earns a correction list on the first cycle.
How does Denver's electronic plan review handle sealed structural sets?
Denver runs its permitting through an electronic portal, and digitally sealed structural PDFs are the standard submittal format — the digital seal must carry a verifiable signature tied to the licensee. Reviewers issue correction lists electronically and check resubmittals against the original comments, so correction responses need to be complete and clearly marked. I prepare sealed sets as clean, portal-ready PDFs and confirm CPD's current digital-seal formatting requirements before uploading, because an intake rejection for formatting is the most avoidable delay in an otherwise well-engineered submittal.
