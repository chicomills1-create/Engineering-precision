Structural PE Stamp in King County, WA: Unincorporated Review and Seismic Design

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

A structural PE stamp in unincorporated King County means a Washington PE has taken responsible charge — verifying seismic design, wet-climate detailing, and slope foundations — for permits reviewed by the county Permits Division, separate from the City of Seattle.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Seismic design under responsible charge

Our King County structural review starts with the seismic parameters: the seismic design category from the adopted code, the site class from the geotechnical investigation, and liquefaction assessment where the site demands it. We verify the lateral system's selection and detailing for the region's seismic demands, trace the complete load path — diaphragms, collectors, vertical elements, foundations — and check that connections are designed for seismic forces. Foundations are reviewed for seismic performance in the area's soils, with the geotech report's recommendations implemented in the drawings. The calculation package states every parameter explicitly. This is the engineering the Washington PE's seal certifies: a seismic design that has been checked, corrected, and confirmed before it reaches the Permits Division.

Water, slopes, and the sealed package

The wet-climate review verifies drainage, waterproofing, and moisture-protection detailing throughout the structural set — foundations drained, below-grade walls waterproofed, exterior detailing that sheds King County's rain. Sloped sites get the geotechnical implementation check: foundations, retaining, setbacks, and drainage per the investigation. We confirm drawings match calculations, note special inspections where the code requires them, and designate delegated-design items as deferred submittals. Snow is addressed where elevation requires it. The Permits Division submittal is organized for the correction cycle with indexed calculations and clear revision responses. The Washington PE seals when the seismic, water, and slope engineering is verified — complete and coordinated.

Unincorporated King County structural submittal checklist

A county structural package is ready when the AHJ is confirmed and the Northwest's demands are engineered. • Jurisdiction confirmed as unincorporated King County (not Seattle or another city) from parcel records • Seismic design verified: parameters, site class, lateral system detailing, liquefaction review • Complete load path traced with drawings matching calculations • Wet-climate detailing: drainage, waterproofing, and moisture protection designed throughout • Slope foundations, retaining, and drainage implementing the geotechnical report • Special inspections noted; deferred-design items designated as deferred submittals • Washington PE seals only after responsible-charge review and verified revisions

What else do project teams ask?

Do unincorporated King County projects permit through Seattle?
No. Unincorporated King County areas permit through the county's Permits Division — a separate AHJ from the City of Seattle's building department, with its own submittal process, checklists, and reviewers. Seattle's jurisdiction covers only land inside city limits. Confirm the parcel's jurisdiction from county records before design: the structural engineering review we perform before sealing is the same, but the package must be organized for the AHJ that will actually check it.
How does seismic risk shape King County structural design?
The Puget Sound region's seismicity — including the Cascadia subduction zone hazard — makes seismic design a primary structural consideration. Our review verifies the seismic design category and parameters, the lateral-force-resisting system's selection and detailing, diaphragm and collector continuity, and foundation design for seismic forces in local soils. Liquefaction potential in susceptible areas gets geotechnical-driven review. County reviewers practice in a seismic jurisdiction and read the seismic design with expert attention; our pre-seal review is calibrated to the same standard.
What wet-climate issues does the structural review address?
King County's rainfall makes moisture management a structural durability issue: our review checks drainage detailing that keeps water away from foundations, waterproofing coordination at below-grade walls, flashing and cladding details that shed water, and wood-framing moisture protection where applicable. Pressure-treated and decay-resistant selections are verified where exposure demands them. Reviewers see moisture-driven structural damage regularly in the Northwest, and a sealed package that shows water was designed for — not just hoped away — earns their confidence.
What about sloped sites in unincorporated King County?
Slopes add geotechnical and structural demands: our review verifies foundation systems designed for slope conditions per the geotechnical investigation, retaining structures engineered for lateral earth pressures plus seismic increments, and drainage provisions that protect slope stability. We check that setbacks, foundation depths, and drainage from the geotech report were implemented in the structural drawings. The county scrutinizes hillside structural submittals, and the package that visibly implements its geotechnical findings is the package that moves.
