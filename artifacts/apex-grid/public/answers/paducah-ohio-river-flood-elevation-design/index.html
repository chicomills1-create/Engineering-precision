How Is Flood Elevation Design Done Along the Ohio River in Paducah?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Paducah flood elevation design uses FEMA maps to set the base flood elevation, elevates finished floors above it with local freeboard, and documents compliance with elevation certificates. The 1937-era floodwall protects the core but does not remove floodplain regulation. City parcels permit through Paducah; county parcels go through McCracken County.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

The 1937 Flood, the Floodwall, and What Protection Means

The 1937 Ohio River flood remains the defining event for Paducah engineering: weeks of record flooding that inundated the city and led directly to the floodwall, levee, gate, and pump station system built in its aftermath. Understanding that history matters because the system was designed around a specific level of protection with specific operating procedures. Gates must be installed, pumps must run, and interior drainage must be managed for the protected area to stay dry. For the design engineer, the floodwall means two things. First, floodplain regulations still apply: the maps, the base flood elevation, and the ordinance requirements do not vanish behind the wall, and insurance and lending treat mapped zones the same. Second, the wall creates a new design condition, the interior flood: with gates closed and the river high, stormwater inside the protected area ponds until pumps remove it. The civil engineer designs grading, detention, and building elevations for that ponding condition as well as the riverine flood, because the building must survive the water the wall holds out and the water the wall holds in.

Elevation, Floodproofing, and the Permit Path

Elevation is the preferred compliance path wherever the building type allows it. The design sets the lowest floor, including basements, above the base flood elevation plus freeboard, keeps enclosures below that level sacrificial and properly vented, and places every piece of service equipment above the flood line. The elevation certificate is the compliance record: the surveyor's certified elevations against the mapped base flood elevation, filed with the permit and used to rate flood insurance. Dry floodproofing serves nonresidential buildings where elevation is impractical. The engineer designs the walls and closures for the full water loads, specifies the closure installation procedure and its triggers, and certifies the design as the regulations require. Wet floodproofing suits parking and storage enclosures that are allowed to flood. On the permit side, the engineer first pins down the AHJ: City of Paducah offices for city parcels, McCracken County for unincorporated land, each enforcing its own floodplain ordinance. Confirming the current effective FIRM panel and any local freeboard requirement happens before the first floor elevation is set, not after.

What else do project teams ask?

Does Paducah's floodwall eliminate the need for flood design?
No. The floodwall protects against riverine flooding up to its design level when gates are closed and pumps operate, but FEMA floodplain maps, insurance requirements, and local floodplain ordinances still apply behind it. Buildings must still meet elevation or floodproofing requirements, and the interior ponding that occurs when gates are closed is itself a design condition.
What was the 1937 Ohio River flood?
The 1937 flood was a record Ohio River flood that inundated Paducah and cities up and down the river, prompting the federal flood protection projects including Paducah's floodwall and levee system. It remains the reference event for the region's flood protection infrastructure and a reminder that the design basis has limits.
What is freeboard in flood design?
Freeboard is additional elevation above the base flood elevation that a local ordinance requires, providing margin against map uncertainty, future development, and larger-than-mapped events. It is set by the jurisdiction, not the federal minimum, and it typically reduces flood insurance premiums enough to pay for itself.
What is an elevation certificate?
An elevation certificate is a form completed by a licensed surveyor documenting a building's elevations relative to the base flood elevation shown on the FEMA maps. The AHJ uses it to verify floodplain compliance at permit closeout, and insurers use it to rate flood insurance premiums.
Who permits a floodplain project in the Paducah area?
Parcels inside Paducah city limits go through the city's engineering and inspections offices under the city's floodplain ordinance; unincorporated McCracken County parcels go through the county under its ordinance. The AHJ decides what is approved, so the engineer confirms the parcel's jurisdiction and the current effective maps before designing.
