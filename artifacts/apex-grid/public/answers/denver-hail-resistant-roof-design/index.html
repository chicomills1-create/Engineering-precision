How Do You Design a Hail-Resistant Roof in Denver?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

A hail-resistant Denver roof uses UL 2218 Class 4 impact-rated roofing as a complete assembly: upgraded underlayment, enhanced wind-rated fastening, impact-rated hip and ridge products, and protected penetrations and skylights. Non-structural reroofs permit quickly through Denver CPD, and many insurers discount premiums for Class 4 roofs.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Class 4 Assemblies and How They Survive Hail

The UL 2218 test is blunt: steel balls dropped from specified heights onto the roofing, with Class 4 surviving the largest impacts without cracking through. But the assembly is what faces the storm. The deck must be sound and properly nailed, since a shingle cannot survive impact over a spongy or under-fastened deck. Underlayment is the second line of defense: synthetic underlayments and self-adhered membranes at vulnerable areas keep water out even if hail fractures the outer layer. Fastening follows the manufacturer's enhanced patterns for high-wind regions, with nails placed in the reinforced zone, because a shingle that lifts in the storm's wind is a shingle that fails. Hips, ridges, and edges get special attention because they take the hardest hits. Purpose-made impact-rated hip and ridge products outperform field-cut shingles, and metal edge details resist the tearing that starts blow-offs. Penetrations are weak points: plastic vents crack, so impact-rated or metal vents are specified, and skylights get impact-rated glazing or protective screens. Gutters and downspouts will dent in severe hail regardless; heavy-gauge metal and secure fastening keep them functional even when cosmetically marked.

Insurance, Reroof Permits, and Maintenance

The insurance math favors Class 4 in hail alley. Many carriers offer meaningful premium discounts for UL 2218 Class 4 roofs, and some Front Range insurers effectively require impact-resistant roofing for new policies or renewals in the highest-risk areas. Owners should confirm the discount and any product requirements with their carrier before selecting materials, since the paperwork, product approvals and installation certificates, is what unlocks the savings. Keep every document: the shingle's Class 4 listing, the contractor's invoice specifying the product, and photos of the installation. Denver's reroof permit path is one of the simpler interactions with CPD. Non-structural reroofing, tear-off and replacement with similar-weight materials, qualifies for a quick permit through e-permits without plan review. Structural work, redecking, changing to heavier tile or slate, or modifying framing, triggers standard building review with engineered drawings. Maintenance is the long game: inspect after every significant hailstorm, keep gutters clear so impact-damaged granule loss does not compound into drainage problems, and replace the roof when functional damage accumulates rather than waiting for leaks.

What else do project teams ask?

What does Class 4 impact resistance mean?
It is the highest rating under UL 2218, the standard test for shingle impact resistance. Steel balls are dropped onto the roofing from set heights to simulate hailstone strikes, and Class 4 products survive the largest impacts without fracturing through the material. The rating applies to the tested assembly, so installation must follow the manufacturer's instructions.
Do impact-resistant shingles lower insurance premiums?
Often, yes. Many insurers operating in Colorado offer premium discounts for UL 2218 Class 4 roofs, and some make impact-resistant roofing a condition of coverage in hail-prone areas. Confirm the discount amount and documentation requirements with your carrier before you buy, and keep the product approvals and installation records.
Does Denver require a permit to reroof?
Non-structural reroofing qualifies for a quick permit through CPD's e-permits portal, issued without plan review. Structural changes, such as redecking, switching to heavier materials, or altering the roof structure, require standard building review with engineered drawings. The AHJ decides what is approved.
Is metal roofing better than Class 4 shingles in hail country?
Both are strong choices with different trade-offs. Standing-seam metal lasts decades and sheds hail well, but large hailstones can dent the panels cosmetically. Class 4 asphalt or synthetic shingles resist functional damage at a lower installed cost but have shorter service lives. The right choice depends on budget, aesthetics, and how much cosmetic denting the owner will tolerate.
Should I inspect my roof after every hailstorm?
Yes. Functional hail damage, fractured mats and granule loss, shortens roof life and can void manufacturer warranties even before leaks appear. A professional inspection documents the damage for insurance purposes, and claims have filing deadlines. Catching damage early also lets you plan the reroof on your schedule instead of after the next storm.
