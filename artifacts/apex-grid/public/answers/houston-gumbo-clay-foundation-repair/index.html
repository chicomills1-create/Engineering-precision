How Are Houston Gumbo Clay Foundations Repaired?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Houston gumbo clay foundations are repaired by first stabilizing moisture around the foundation, then underpinning settled areas with steel or concrete piers driven to stable bearing strata, lifting the slab or grade beam back toward level, and correcting drainage so the expansive clay stops cycling.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Diagnosing the Failure Before Designing the Fix

Every foundation repair starts as an investigation. The engineer takes precise floor-elevation readings across the building to draw a settlement contour map, which reveals whether the foundation is heaving, settling, or doing both in different areas. Exterior observations — the stair-step cracks in brick veneer, separations at window frames, gaps under the slab edge — are correlated with the elevation data. A plumbing hydrostatic test checks for under-slab leaks, because in Houston's gumbo a single leaking line can saturate and soften the clay under one portion of the building while the rest stays firm. The engineer also reviews what was built originally. Many distressed Houston foundations are thin post-tension slabs or lightly reinforced conventional slabs from eras when expansive-soil design was less rigorous, sometimes with no moisture barrier and minimal steel. Knowing the original construction tells the engineer what the repair must compensate for: a slab that was never stiff enough for gumbo clay needs more than releveling — it needs the underpinning to carry the loads the slab cannot. The diagnostic report becomes the repair plan's foundation, documenting cause, extent, and the specific remedy for each area.

Underpinning Methods and the Lift

Steel push piers are the most common underpinning for Houston residential and light commercial work. Sections of steel pipe are hydraulically driven to refusal or to a specified bearing stratum, using the building's own weight as reaction, then a bracket transfers the foundation load to the pier. Helical piers — steel shafts with screw-like plates — are torqued into the ground to a specified torque that correlates with capacity, and they work well where driving vibrations or access constraints rule out push piers. The engineer selects the system from the soils data and the structure's loads, and specifies the required depth or torque criteria rather than leaving it to the installer. The lift itself is controlled and incremental. Jacks at each pier raise the foundation in small stages, with elevation readings taken continuously so no area is lifted faster than its neighbors. The engineer's plan sets a target recovery — often most of the settlement, not all of it — because forcing a building fully back to its original plane can crack drywall, rack door frames, and overstress connections that adapted to the settled position over years. After the lift, the piers are locked off, brackets are secured, and the engineer records final elevations. The repair drawings also show the drainage and plumbing corrections, because the piers solve the support problem while the water work solves the cause.

What else do project teams ask?

What is Houston gumbo clay?
Gumbo is the local name for the highly plastic, highly expansive clay soils underlying much of the Houston area. They swell dramatically when wet and shrink when dry, with some of the highest shrink-swell potential in the state. Foundations on gumbo must be designed — or repaired — for large, uneven soil movement driven almost entirely by moisture changes.
What is foundation underpinning?
Underpinning extends a foundation's support down to stable soil or rock below the problem zone. In Houston repairs, steel push piers or helical piers are installed through the active gumbo clay to a competent bearing stratum, then connected to the existing foundation with brackets. The building's load transfers to the piers, bypassing the moving clay, and jacks can lift settled areas back toward level.
Should a settled foundation always be lifted back to perfectly level?
Not necessarily. The engineer's lift plan typically targets most of the settlement rather than a perfect plane, because forcing a long-settled building fully back can crack finishes and overstress the frame. The goal is a stable, functional foundation on reliable support — level enough that doors, floors, and drainage work — verified by before-and-after elevation surveys.
How is foundation repair permitted in Houston versus Harris County?
Within Houston city limits, foundation repair with engineered plans goes through the city's permitting center for structural review. In unincorporated Harris County, the county's permitting office handles residential foundation repairs under its own submittal requirements. Either way the authority having jurisdiction approves the work, and engineered plans with pier layouts and lift criteria are typically required.
