How Is Evidence Storage Designed for Courthouse Security?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Engineers design a dedicated access-controlled evidence room with logged credential entry, CCTV, intrusion alarming, separately zoned climate control with monitoring, and fire suppression matched to the contents.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Chain of Custody and Access Control

Chain of custody is a legal standard, and the building systems exist to make it provable. The evidence room has one controlled entry — no secondary doors, no shared corridors — with credential readers that log every entry and exit by identity and timestamp. The design separates the intake transaction from the storage: a pass-through counter or intake vestibule lets officers deposit items without entering the storage area, and each deposit is logged against the item's tag. Inside, storage is organized by case with lockable compartments or cages for high-sensitivity items, and the lighting design provides the even, high-clarity illumination needed for inventory and inspection. CCTV covers the room, the intake point, and the approach corridor, with cameras positioned to document every handoff. The access control system enforces role separation — the people who can enter are not necessarily the people who can open every compartment — and the audit trail is retained as long as the jurisdiction requires, which for evidence can be decades.

Climate, Fire Protection, and Monitoring

Evidence degrades, and degradation destroys cases. The engineer designs a dedicated HVAC zone for the evidence room with temperature and humidity control suited to mixed contents — documents, electronics, biological material, and narcotics each have their vulnerabilities, so the design targets stable, moderate conditions with tight alarming on excursion. Refrigeration for biological evidence gets its own monitored units on emergency power, with temperature logging that survives a power event. Fire protection is selected for the inventory: standard sprinkler protection for general storage, with clean-agent or pre-action systems where water would destroy the evidence it is meant to save. Detection is early-warning — air-sampling or very-early smoke detection in high-value rooms — because minutes matter when the contents are irreplaceable. All of it — temperature, humidity, refrigeration, detection, suppression status — reports to the building management system with alarming to the facilities team and, for critical excursions, to the evidence custodian directly.

Evidence Storage Design Checklist

A complete evidence storage design makes custody provable and contents preservable: • Dedicated evidence room with single controlled entry and solid construction • Credential access with per-person logging and role-separated permissions • Intake counter or vestibule separating deposits from the storage area • CCTV covering the room, intake, and approach with long retention • Dedicated HVAC zone with temperature/humidity monitoring and alarming • Monitored refrigeration for biological evidence on emergency power • Fire suppression and early-warning detection matched to the room's contents

What else do project teams ask?

What is chain of custody and why does the building matter?
Chain of custody is the documented, unbroken record of who handled a piece of evidence from collection to courtroom. If the defense can show a gap — an unlogged access, an unsecured room — the evidence can be challenged or excluded. The building supports custody with controlled single-entry access, per-person logging, CCTV of every handoff, and environmental monitoring that proves proper storage. The room's systems are, in effect, witnesses to the evidence's integrity.
What climate conditions does evidence storage need?
Stable, moderate temperature and humidity with tight control and alarming — the enemies are heat, moisture, and fluctuation, which degrade documents, electronics, and biological material. Refrigerated evidence needs monitored cold storage on emergency power with logging that survives outages. The engineer zones the evidence room independently from the rest of the courthouse so its conditions never depend on the comfort settings of adjacent offices.
How is fire protection handled for evidence rooms?
By matching the suppression to the contents. General evidence storage typically gets sprinkler protection per code, but rooms holding documents, electronics, or other water-sensitive material may use clean-agent or pre-action systems that keep water out of the room until a real fire is confirmed. Early-warning detection — air-sampling systems in high-value rooms — buys the time that makes the difference between a scare and a loss.
Who should have access to the evidence room?
As few people as possible, with permissions matched to roles: evidence custodians have full access, intake officers can use the deposit counter but not the storage area, and auditors get supervised or logged access. The access control system enforces these distinctions and logs everything. The design principle is that no single person should be able to enter, remove, and alter evidence without the system recording each step.
