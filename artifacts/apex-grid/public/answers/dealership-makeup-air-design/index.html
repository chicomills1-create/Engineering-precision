How Is Makeup Air Designed for Dealership Service Bays?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Engineers size makeup air units to match total shop exhaust volume, temper the supply for design-day heating, distribute it across work zones, and interlock it with exhaust so the building holds its pressure cascade.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Air Balance and Unit Sizing

The air balance is an accounting exercise with real consequences. The engineer lists every exhaust stream — general shop exhaust, tailpipe capture reels, welding and paint-prep exhaust, restroom and parts exhaust — at its design flow, then sizes makeup air to replace the total, holding the shop slightly negative to the showroom and offices but never starving. The calculation is documented as the balance schedule so future exhaust additions trigger a makeup recheck. Unit selection follows the load: direct gas-fired units where gas is available and the application suits, indirect-fired or hydronic where combustion products in the airstream are a concern, electric where gas is absent. The engineer sizes heating for the design-day temperature difference at full airflow — the worst credible hour — and verifies turndown so the units also behave on mild days.

Distribution, Tempering, and Exhaust Interlocks

Delivering the air well matters as much as heating it. The engineer designs low, distributed supply — ducted drops or fabric duct along the shop — so tempered air washes across technician work zones and migrates toward exhaust points, rather than short-circuiting from a single grille to the nearest exhaust fan. Discharge temperature control holds the supply in the comfort range regardless of outdoor conditions. Interlocks make the system foolproof: makeup air units start with the exhaust systems they serve and prove operation before the shop goes to work. The control sequence includes morning warm-up, occupied and unoccupied modes, and freeze protection that shuts the units down safely rather than letting coils freeze. Commissioning measures the actual balance and adjusts sheaves, dampers, and controls to the documented intent.

Dealership Makeup Air Design Checklist

A complete makeup air package balances the shop honestly: • Air balance schedule totaling every exhaust stream at design flow • Makeup air units sized to match exhaust with the intended pressure cascade • Heating sized for design-day tempering with discharge temperature control • Low, distributed supply washing work zones toward exhaust points • Interlocks proving makeup air with exhaust before shop operation • Freeze protection, warm-up, and occupied/unoccupied sequences • Commissioning verification of balance with doors open and closed

What else do project teams ask?

What happens if a service shop has exhaust but no makeup air?
The building goes negative and everything suffers: overhead doors become hard to open, exhaust fans cannot move their rated airflow because there is no air to move, backdrafting can pull flue gases from water heaters and furnaces into the space, and cold outdoor air infiltrates through every crack in winter. Makeup air is not optional equipment — it is the other half of the exhaust system, and the engineer sizes the two together as one design.
How is makeup air heated in winter?
Through heating sections in the makeup air units — most commonly direct gas-fired burners, with indirect-fired, hydronic, or electric heat where the application requires it. The engineer sizes the heating for the design-day outdoor temperature at full airflow and controls discharge temperature so the supply stays in the comfort range. On a cold morning with the shop exhaust at full operation, the makeup air system is moving and heating thousands of cubic feet per minute — the heating load is substantial and designed, not incidental.
Where should makeup air be delivered in a service shop?
Low and distributed across the work zones, flowing toward the exhaust points. High, single-point supply short-circuits to the nearest exhaust and leaves technicians in stagnant zones; low distributed supply — through duct drops or fabric duct — washes tempered air across the breathing zone and carries contaminants toward capture. The engineer lays out the distribution against the bay and lift layout so supply and exhaust work as a planned airstream, not two independent systems.
Should makeup air run when the exhaust is off?
No — the two are interlocked as a system. Running makeup air without exhaust pressurizes the shop and pushes fumes and odors into the showroom and offices; running exhaust without makeup starves the building negative. The control sequence starts them together, proves operation of each, and shuts them down together, with unoccupied setback for both. The interlock is drawn in the control schematics and proven at commissioning, not left as a field decision.
