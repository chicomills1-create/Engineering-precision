How Do Design-Build Teams Coordinate Permits With Sealed Drawings?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Design-build permit coordination means planning the PE's phased review-and-seal sequence and the AHJ's phased submittals together at project setup — foundations first, then building, then MEP/delegated packages — tracked on a visible permit dashboard with correction float built into every phase.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Building the phased permit plan

I start the permit plan with the AHJ, not with the design. Before the phases are drawn, I confirm what the jurisdiction accepts: phased submittal policy, deferred submittal policy for delegated components, digital seal acceptance, intake checklists per phase, review timelines, and how corrections are processed per phase. This confirmation happens in the pre-submittal meeting or in direct correspondence with the plans examiners — and I get it in writing, because phased strategies live or die on the details (one jurisdiction's "foundation permit" includes the underground MEP; another's does not). Then the phases get designed around the construction sequence and the PE's review capacity. The typical stack: site and utilities, foundations and underground, the full building (structure and architecture), MEP systems, and delegated/deferred components — but the actual stack follows the project's logic, not a template. Each phase gets a PE review milestone with a genuine review behind it, a seal date, a submittal date, and a review-plus-corrections window. I build the plan backward from the construction activities: when does the field need foundations released, when the superstructure, when the MEP rough-in — and the engineering and AHJ timelines stack up behind those dates with float. A permit plan without float is a wish.

Running the permit dashboard

The dashboard tracks every phased package through the same pipeline: PE review in progress, sealed, submitted to AHJ, under review, corrections received, corrections with PE, re-sealed, resubmitted, approved. Each stage has an owner and a date, and the whole team — design-build PM, PE, superintendent, owner — sees the same status. I update it weekly at minimum, and daily when phases are in correction rounds, because permit status decays fast and stale status is worse than no status. The dashboard's real value shows in the two moments that define phased permitting. First, when a correction round lands: the dashboard shows exactly which phase is affected, who owns the response, and how the correction ripples into later phases still in PE review — so the response is managed, not panicked. Second, when construction is waiting on a release: the dashboard shows whether the wait is PE review, AHJ review, or corrections, and who needs to act. Design-build lives on overlapping design and construction; the dashboard is the instrument panel that keeps the overlap productive instead of chaotic. I have seen it turn permit coordination from the project's biggest anxiety into its most boring meeting — which is exactly what you want.

Design-build permit coordination checklist

Plan the phases with the AHJ first, then engineer and build inside the plan. • Confirm the AHJ's phased and deferred submittal policies in writing before the phase plan is drawn — intake checklists per phase • Build the phase stack from the construction sequence backward: each construction release traced to its permit phase and PE seal date • Give every phase a genuine PE review milestone — phased seals carry the same professional weight as full-set seals • Budget one full correction round per phase, and keep later phases' PE reviews open until earlier phases clear plan check • Run the visible permit dashboard: every package tracked PE-review → sealed → submitted → corrections → re-sealed → approved, with owners and dates

What else do project teams ask?

What does a phased permit plan actually contain?
The phase breakdown (e.g., site/utilities, foundations, full building, MEP rough-in, delegated components), what each phased submittal includes, the PE review milestone and seal date for each phase, the AHJ submittal date, the expected review duration plus one correction round, and the construction activity each phase releases. I also include the AHJ's written phased-submittal policy or the reviewer's confirmation, the digital seal requirements per phase, and the contingency: what happens if a phase's corrections require redesign. The plan is a schedule document, not a narrative — dates, owners, and dependencies — and it lives on the project dashboard where everyone sees it.
How do plan-check corrections work across phased permits?
Each phase gets its own correction cycle, handled the same way: the comments go to the responsible PE, the PE revises and re-verifies, the corrected sheets are clouded and re-sealed, and the complete response is resubmitted. The phasing risk is cascade — a foundation-phase correction that changes the structural concept ripples into the building phase still under PE review. I manage this by keeping the PE's review of later phases open until earlier phases clear plan check, so ripple effects get absorbed in review rather than in resubmittals. It costs some review float; it saves correction cycles. Budget one full correction round per phase and you will rarely be surprised.
Can construction start before all phases are permitted?
Construction can start on the phases that are permitted — that is the entire point of phasing — but only on those phases, and only within the conditions the AHJ sets. The foundation permit releases excavation and foundations; it does not release the superstructure. AHJs enforce this boundary, and building beyond the permitted phase risks stop-work orders that erase every day phasing saved. I make the release boundaries explicit in the permit dashboard: each construction activity traced to the permit phase that authorizes it. The field superintendent should be able to answer "which permit covers this work" for anything happening on site.
What is the PE's role in AHJ meetings during design-build permitting?
The PE attends (or briefs for) the pre-submittal meeting and any significant correction-resolution meetings, because the engineering questions get answered fastest by the engineer in responsible charge. I prepare for these meetings with the review record behind me — the calculations, the code analysis, the revision history — so the reviewer's technical questions get technical answers on the spot rather than generating another written round. The design-build team's PM handles the process and scheduling; the PE handles the engineering substance. Teams that send only the PM to technical meetings add a full review cycle to every engineering question.
