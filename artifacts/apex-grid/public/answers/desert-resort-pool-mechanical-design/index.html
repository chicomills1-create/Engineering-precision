How Is Resort Pool Mechanical Design Engineered in the Desert?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Desert resort pool mechanical design sizes filtration and automated chemical treatment for peak bather loads and health-code turnover, selects heating for the real operating season, treats evaporation control as the primary water and energy saver, and engineers indoor pool halls as dedicated dehumidification projects — permitted through the health department and the building department together.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Filtration, Turnover, and Chemical Automation

The engineer starts with the health code's turnover requirement and the pool's volume, which together set the recirculation flow rate — the single number the rest of the mechanical room is built around. Filters are selected for that flow with a fouling margin, pumps with variable-speed drives deliver it efficiently across the day's bather curve, and the piping is sized for velocities that neither waste pump energy nor scour the pool finishes. Main-drain and skimmer hydraulics are balanced so the pool surface actually skims at all operating conditions, and entrapment protection — compliant drain covers, and vacuum-release or vented systems where required — is designed to the federal and state standards the health reviewer will check. Chemical control is fully automated at resort scale. ORP and pH controllers modulate chlorine and acid feed against the live bather load, UV or ozone units provide supplemental oxidation that keeps chloramines — the compounds behind the classic indoor-pool smell and eye irritation — under control, and the chemical room is designed with code-required ventilation, containment, and separation. The engineer sizes chemical storage for the delivery interval the owner negotiates with the supplier, so the resort never runs dry on a holiday weekend.

Evaporation Control and Indoor Hall Dehumidification

The evaporation budget drives the water-feature decisions. The engineer calculates the pool's evaporation at design conditions and shows the owner what covers, wind screening, and operating schedules save in water, heat, and chemicals — the numbers usually justify the measures several times over. Automatic covers are specified for the hours the pool is closed; where covers are impractical for a resort's 24-hour aesthetic, the design leans harder on wind screening and on heating and chemical systems sized for the uncovered loss. Makeup water comes from the softened supply with a backflow-protected connection, and the engineer meters it so the owner can see the real consumption. The indoor pool hall gets its own psychrometric design. The engineer sets the hall air temperature a few degrees above the water temperature — the standard strategy to limit evaporation — and sizes the dehumidification unit for the full evaporation load at peak occupancy plus the outdoor-air ventilation the code requires. Supply air is washed down the glazing and the exterior walls to keep surface temperatures above the dew point, the structure and fasteners are specified for the corrosive environment, and the vapor retarder placement is detailed with the architect so moisture never condenses inside the wall assembly. Adjacent spaces — lobbies, restaurants, guest corridors — are held positive to the pool hall so chloramines migrate nowhere.

Desert Resort Pool Mechanical Checklist

Use this checklist before the pool mechanical drawings are issued: • Recirculation flow rate set from the health code turnover requirement and pool volume • Filters, variable-speed pumps, and piping sized for the turnover with efficient part-load operation • Automated ORP/pH chemical control with UV or ozone supplemental disinfection • Chemical storage room designed with code ventilation, containment, and separation • Heating selected for the real operating season: heat pumps, solar thermal, and gas for spa recovery • Evaporation budget prepared; covers, wind screening, and softened makeup water specified • Indoor halls: dedicated dehumidification sized for peak evaporation, corrosion-resistant construction • Health-department submittal (turnover, disinfection, entrapment, barriers) prepared alongside building permit

What else do project teams ask?

How much water does a desert resort pool lose to evaporation?
Far more than most owners expect — an uncovered pool in 110-degree heat with low humidity and wind can lose a quarter inch or more of water depth per day, which over a large resort pool is thousands of gallons daily, carrying away heat and chemicals with it. The engineer prepares an evaporation budget during design so the owner sees the number, then designs covers, wind screening, and operating schedules against it. Evaporation control is usually the highest-return investment in the pool's mechanical design.
Why do indoor pools need special dehumidification?
Because 82-degree pool water evaporates continuously into the hall air, and that moisture condenses on any cool surface — structure, glazing, walls — rotting and corroding the building from the inside. Standard comfort air conditioning cannot remove that much latent load. The engineer designs a dedicated pool-dehumidification unit sized for the peak evaporation rate, holds the hall slightly negative to protect adjacent spaces, and recovers the removed heat back into the pool water.
What does the health department review on a resort pool?
The turnover rate and recirculation design, the filtration and disinfection systems, entrapment protection on drains and suction outlets, pool barriers and alarms, chemical storage safety, and water quality monitoring. In the Phoenix metro, Maricopa County Environmental Services reviews public and semi-public pools; the building department separately reviews the structure, electrical, and plumbing. The engineer prepares both submittals in parallel so the reviews do not stack end to end.
Heat pump or gas heater for a desert resort pool?
Usually both, for different jobs. Heat pumps efficiently hold the pool at temperature — ideal in a climate with warm air to harvest — and solar thermal supplements where roof or site area allows. Gas heaters earn their place on the spa, where guests expect fast recovery after heavy use that a heat pump cannot deliver quickly. The engineer sizes each to its duty and lets the controls schedule them independently.
