How Are Denver Midcentury Ranches Structurally Remodeled?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Denver midcentury ranches are structurally remodeled by mapping the existing load paths - typically wood trusses or rafters bearing on interior walls - then sizing beams and posts to open up walls, verifying foundations for added loads, and detailing connections so the low-pitched roof diaphragm stays intact through the remodel.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Opening Up Walls Without Breaking the Load Path

Every wall proposed for removal gets a verdict: bearing or non-bearing, lateral or not, and what it carries. The engineer traces roof loads from the rafters or trusses through the wall, through the floor system, to the foundation - documenting the path that the new beam must replace. Openings get sized beams with engineered connections at each end: proper bearing length, hangers or welded and bolted connections for steel, and posts that land on adequate footings. The design also handles the temporary condition, because the house has to stand while the wall comes out - shoring and sequencing are part of the drawings, not left to the contractor's judgment. Trusses deserve special caution. A truss is an engineered system where every web member matters; cutting a web to open a ceiling or move a wall invalidates the design unless a licensed engineer redesigns it. The survey identifies trusses early so the floor plan works around them or budgets for the redesign. Where rafters bear on the removed wall, the new beam picks up each rafter with proper hangers, and the ceiling diaphragm is reconnected across the opening so the roof's lateral stability survives the remodel.

Foundations, Pop-Tops, and Second Stories

New point loads need new footings. The engineer probes the existing foundation to confirm its width, depth, and condition, then designs pad footings under each new post - sized for the soil bearing capacity and detailed to avoid undermining the existing foundation during excavation. Where the ranch sits on expansive soils, the footing design accounts for movement so the new work does not crack away from the old. Underpinning is specified when the existing foundation itself is inadequate for the remodeled loads. Pop-tops are evaluated as new structures on old foundations. The engineer checks whether the existing footings, foundation walls, and first-floor framing can carry the added story's gravity and lateral loads - often they cannot without reinforcement or a new independent foundation for the addition. The design coordinates the structural work with Denver's zoning limits on height and bulk plane, because a structurally feasible pop-top that violates zoning is a wasted design. Energy code compliance for the new envelope is integrated from the start rather than bolted on at permit time.

What else do project teams ask?

Can I remove any interior wall in a midcentury ranch?
No. Many interior walls in a ranch are bearing walls carrying the roof, and some participate in the lateral system. The engineer surveys the framing, traces each wall's loads to the foundation, and designs the beam, posts, and footings to replace exactly what the removed wall did. Walls come out only with their replacement structure designed.
Why are roof trusses a problem in ranch remodels?
Trusses are engineered as complete systems - cutting or altering a web member without a redesign compromises the whole truss. Unlike conventional rafters, trusses cannot be casually modified to open ceilings or relocate bearing points. The engineer identifies trusses in the survey and either designs around them or provides the stamped redesign the alteration requires.
Will my ranch's foundation handle a second story?
Often not without help. Ranch foundations were sized for one light story, and a pop-top roughly doubles the gravity load while adding wind and seismic demand. The engineer evaluates the existing footings and walls against the new loads and designs reinforcement, underpinning, or an independent foundation where they fall short.
How do Denver permits handle ranch remodels and pop-tops?
Inside Denver, residential remodels and additions go through the city's development review with structural, zoning, and energy checks on one coordinated set - and pop-tops must satisfy height, bulk-plane, and setback limits before structural review matters. Nearby unincorporated areas use the county AHJ with different forms and timelines. The engineer confirms jurisdiction and zoning limits before designing. The AHJ decides approvals.
