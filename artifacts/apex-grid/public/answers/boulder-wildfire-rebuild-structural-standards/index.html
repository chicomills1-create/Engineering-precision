What Structural Standards Guide Boulder Wildfire Rebuilds?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Boulder wildfire rebuilds follow expanded county wildfire mitigation rules, extended into Wildfire Zone 2 after the December 2021 Marshall Fire destroyed 1,000+ structures, plus Colorado's 2026 statewide Wildfire Resiliency Code for WUI areas. The standards target ember intrusion with Class A roof assemblies, ember-resistant vents, ignition-resistant siding and decking, and defensible space.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Hardening Details That Changed Rebuilds

Ember intrusion drives the detailing. Attic, eave, and foundation vents use ember-resistant designs with fine mesh or baffles that block embers while still ventilating; ordinary vents are how most wildfire homes ignite from the inside out. Roofing carries a Class A fire rating as a complete assembly, including underlayment and edge details, not just the shingle or tile, and gutters are noncombustible so they cannot collect and hold burning debris. Siding, decking, and exterior projections use ignition-resistant or noncombustible materials, eaves are boxed in with noncombustible soffits to eliminate open rafter tails where embers lodge, and at least one pane of each window is tempered glass that survives radiant heat without breaking open an entry path. The first five feet around the house get the same rigor as the walls. Combustible mulch, shrubs against the siding, firewood stacks, and wood fences attached to the structure are removed from the zone touching the house, replaced with hardscape, gravel, or irrigated low groundcover. Fences near the house switch to metal, because a burning fence is a fuse that carries fire straight to the siding. These are site-design decisions drawn on the grading and landscape plans, and the structural engineer accounts for the heavier materials, tile roofing or stucco re-siding, in the dead loads the framing must carry.

The Statewide WUI Code and Where It Applies

The Colorado Wildfire Resiliency Code gives the state its first uniform wildfire construction standard, and its reach is broader than the old county-by-county patchwork. The CWRC applies to construction, alteration, and certain changes of use for occupiable structures within mapped wildland-urban interface areas, with requirements scaled to the area's hazard classification. Roofing work can trigger companion requirements for siding, soffits, fascia, and doors, because the code treats the envelope as one ignition-resistant system rather than a menu of options. Mapping determines everything. A parcel's WUI designation and hazard classification set which provisions apply, and local jurisdictions adopt and enforce the code with their own amendments, so two neighboring towns can have meaningfully different requirements. Existing legal structures are generally allowed to continue their use, with the code biting on new construction and covered alterations. For anyone rebuilding or building new near the Front Range foothills or in grassland WUI areas, the first engineering step is confirming the parcel's WUI status and the local AHJ's amendments before the envelope is detailed. The AHJ decides what is approved at plan check and final inspection.

What else do project teams ask?

When was the Marshall Fire and how destructive was it?
The Marshall Fire started on December 30, 2021, in Boulder County. Driven by extreme winds, it destroyed more than 1,000 structures in Superior, Louisville, and unincorporated areas, with damages exceeding one billion dollars. It remains the most destructive wildfire in Colorado history and the event that forced a rethink of grassland wildfire risk.
Why didn't Boulder County's wildfire codes prevent the Marshall Fire losses?
The county's wildfire construction rules applied only to the forested foothills and mountains in Wildfire Zone 1, outside cities and towns. The Marshall Fire burned through grassy suburban neighborhoods that had never been subject to those requirements. After the fire, the county extended its wildfire mitigation rules into Wildfire Zone 2, and the state developed the CWRC.
What is the Colorado Wildfire Resiliency Code?
The CWRC is Colorado's statewide wildfire construction code for the wildland-urban interface, released in July 2026. It sets ignition-resistant construction requirements for new buildings and certain alterations in mapped WUI areas, scaled to hazard classification. It was modeled on California's wildfire mitigation approach and shaped by research into how the Marshall Fire spread.
Can I reuse my foundation when rebuilding after a fire?
Sometimes, but only after a structural engineer evaluates it for heat damage. Concrete and masonry exposed to intense heat can lose strength even when they look intact, and damaged sections must be removed or replaced. The AHJ decides whether a surviving foundation can be reused, and the evaluation is part of the rebuild's structural submittal.
What makes a vent ember-resistant?
Ember-resistant vents use fine metal mesh, baffles, or tested designs that block wind-driven embers while still allowing attic and crawl-space ventilation. Standard vents are a leading ignition path in wildfires because embers enter the attic and start fires from the inside. Boulder County's post-fire rules require mesh-covered exterior vents for this reason.
