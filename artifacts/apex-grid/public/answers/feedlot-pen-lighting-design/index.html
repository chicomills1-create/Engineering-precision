How Is Feedlot Pen Lighting Designed for Night Operations?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Feedlot lighting is task-focused: LED area lights on poles along alleys, bunks, and working areas for uniform shadow-free coverage, photocell and timer controls with manual override, full-cutoff fixtures for dark-sky compliance, and optional photoperiod systems where the feeding program justifies them.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Lighting Layout and Coverage Targets

The engineer starts with a site plan marked for night work: which alleys the pen riders use, where the feed truck runs, the loadout, the scale, the processing barn, and the waterers. Each gets a target light level matched to its task — working areas brighter, pen interiors dimmer — and the engineer spaces poles to hit those targets uniformly. Poles go outside fences and out of traffic lanes, with breakaway or protected bases where vehicles pass. Uniformity matters more than peak brightness. A layout with bright pools under each pole and dark gaps between them is worse than a dimmer even layout, because the crew's eyes adapt to the bright spots and the gaps become blind. The engineer checks the point-by-point calculation for uniformity ratios and adjusts spacing and mounting height until the coverage is even. At the loadout and processing areas, the engineer adds higher-output fixtures on separate control so those zones can go to full brightness for night work.

Fixtures, Controls, and Dark-Sky Compliance

Full-cutoff LED fixtures are the default: they put light on the working surface and almost none above horizontal, which satisfies most county dark-sky ordinances and keeps neighbors happy. The engineer selects fixtures rated for the environment — dust, moisture, temperature swings — with surge protection on the rural feeders that take lightning hits. Color temperature in the neutral range renders cattle, footing, and equipment naturally for night checks. Controls balance automation with crew needs. Photocells bring lights on at dusk; timers or scheduling drop non-essential zones to minimal levels after the night's work window; manual overrides at the office, processing barn, and loadout let the crew bring any zone to full brightness on demand. The engineer wires photoperiod pens, if any, on dedicated circuits with their own timers and metering. Every control sequence is documented simply, because the night crew needs to run it without an engineering degree.

Feedlot Pen Lighting Design Checklist

Use this checklist before the lighting plan is finalized: • Night-work areas mapped: alleys, bunks, waterers, loadout, scale, processing barn • Light levels set per task, with pen interiors dimmer than working areas • Pole spacing and height modeled for uniform coverage, no dark gaps • Poles placed outside fences and traffic lanes, with protected bases • Full-cutoff LED fixtures selected for the dusty outdoor environment, with surge protection • Photocell plus timer controls with manual override at key working areas • County dark-sky ordinance requirements verified and met • Photoperiod lighting, if used, on dedicated metered circuits with its own timers • Loadout and processing zones on separate control for full-brightness night work

What else do project teams ask?

How much light do feedlot pens need at night?
Working areas — alleys, bunks, loadout, processing — need enough for safe work and clear cattle observation, while pen interiors can stay dim. The engineer sets foot-candle targets per zone rather than flooding the whole yard, because even, task-matched lighting is safer and cheaper than brute-force brightness.
Does extended lighting help cattle gain?
Some feeding programs use extended photoperiod to stimulate intake, and the response can justify the power cost — but it is a program decision, not a default. The engineer designs photoperiod systems to a uniform target level and duration on dedicated metered circuits, so the operator can measure the return. Without a program behind it, extra light is just expense and light pollution.
What are dark-sky rules for feedlots?
Many rural counties limit upward light and spill onto neighboring property through dark-sky or outdoor lighting ordinances. Full-cutoff fixtures, sensible mounting heights, and controls that dim non-essential zones after work hours typically satisfy them. The engineer checks the county ordinance before finalizing the layout, since fixture choice is hard to change after installation.
How are feedlot lights protected from damage?
By placement and ruggedness: poles outside pen fences and traffic lanes, protected bases where vehicles pass, fixtures rated for dust and weather, and surge protection on rural feeders. The engineer keeps fixtures out of cattle reach entirely — a curious thousand-pound animal destroys anything within range.
