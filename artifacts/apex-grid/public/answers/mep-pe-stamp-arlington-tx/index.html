When Do You Need an MEP PE Stamp in Arlington, TX?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

An MEP PE stamp is needed in Arlington for any project with engineered HVAC, plumbing, or electrical work — from ground-up commercial to restaurant and retail tenant improvements — sealed by a Texas-licensed PE after a genuine review under responsible charge.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Projects that trigger sealed MEP drawings

The trigger isn't square footage — it's engineered systems. New commercial buildings of any size need sealed MEP drawings, because the HVAC, plumbing, and electrical designs all require a licensed engineer's certification. Additions that extend or modify existing systems need sealing on the affected disciplines. Tenant improvements need it whenever they touch the systems: reworking air distribution, adding electrical load, altering plumbing, or installing specialty systems like kitchen exhaust. Change-of-occupancy projects frequently trigger it too, because the new use brings new ventilation, plumbing fixture, and electrical demands the old systems weren't designed for. When in doubt, the PE can tell you in a scoping conversation what the city's reviewers will require — that conversation is free compared to a rejected submittal.

What the review covers by occupancy

The PE's verification follows the building's use. Restaurants and food service mean kitchen exhaust and makeup air balance, grease waste detailing, gas distribution, and the HVAC loads those systems create. Retail means rooftop units or split systems verified against real envelope and occupancy loads, plus lighting and power for the sales floor. Office means VAV or similar distribution checked against dense occupancy, ventilation per code, and electrical distribution with IT loads honestly accounted for. Industrial means process ventilation, high-bay heating and cooling, and electrical service sized for equipment the template never imagined. In every case the sequence is the same — verify loads, match equipment, check risers and distribution, reconcile energy compliance, mark revisions, re-check, seal — but the engineering judgment is occupancy-specific, which is why the review has to be real.

Arlington MEP scoping checklist

Before engaging the PE, having these answers ready makes the scoping conversation productive and the fee proposal accurate. The theme is knowing what systems the project actually touches. • Project type and occupancy — new, addition, TI, or change of use • Which MEP systems are new, altered, or untouched • Specialty systems involved: kitchens, grease waste, process exhaust, medical, IT loads • Completeness of the starting drawings and calculations, if any exist • Target permit timeline, so the review can be scheduled realistically • Confirmation the sealing PE is Texas-licensed and the firm shows its F-number

What else do project teams ask?

Does a restaurant tenant improvement in Arlington need sealed MEP drawings?
Almost always, yes. A restaurant TI is one of the most MEP-intensive small projects there is: commercial kitchen exhaust with makeup air, grease waste and interceptors, gas distribution, water heating sized for the operation, and electrical distribution carrying heavy kitchen and HVAC loads. The city's reviewers hold TI work to the same code standard as new construction, and the systems interact — the exhaust drives the makeup air, which drives the HVAC loads, which drives the electrical service. A Texas-licensed PE reviews the whole MEP package for the TI, verifies the engineering, marks revisions, and seals. Small footprint, full professional standard.
What does the MEP PE verify on Arlington hospitality projects?
Hospitality and assembly occupancies get a review tuned to high occupant loads and operational intensity. I verify the HVAC loads with realistic occupancy densities — not the optimistic ones — and check that ventilation meets code for assembly and dining spaces, with the outside-air load fully in the calculations. Kitchen systems get their own pass: exhaust rates, makeup air balance, grease duct construction and clearances, and gas sizing. Electrical review covers the service calculation against the building's real loads, panel schedules in agreement with the riser, and emergency and life-safety systems. These buildings run hard, and the sealed design has to reflect how they'll actually operate, not how the template assumed.
What do PE review and seal services cost for an Arlington MEP project?
Cost follows scope and complexity, not a flat fee. A straightforward TI with a clean starting package costs far less than a ground-up commercial building with central systems — the review hours scale with the number of systems, the coordination complexity, and how much revision the set needs. What I'd budget for is the engineering review itself plus at least one round of responding to the PE's markups; the city review cycles are separate. The expensive version of this engagement is the one that starts late, with an incomplete package and a permit deadline — rush conditions and redesign cost more than a planned review. Engage the PE early with complete drawings and the fee stays proportional to the actual engineering.
How long does the city review take after the MEP set is sealed?
After sealing, the timeline belongs to the city's plan review cycles, which run on published schedules plus correction rounds. I budget at least one correction round on top of the published review times for any commercial MEP submittal — even clean sets occasionally draw a reviewer question. What compresses the timeline is the quality of the sealed package: loads verified, equipment matched, risers and panels in agreement, energy forms reconciled. A PE who knows Arlington's correction patterns front-loads the details reviewers flag, so the sealed set goes in once and the permit keeps moving instead of cycling on avoidable items.
