What does CCRC design require?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

A continuing care retirement community is three projects in one: independent living (residential), assisted living (board and care), and skilled nursing (health-care occupancy) — each with its own code regime, MEP standards, and licensing. The engineering challenge is the campus: shared utilities, phased construction around occupied buildings, and boundaries between occupancies that satisfy both the AHJ and the licensing agencies.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Three occupancies, one campus

Each CCRC component carries its own IBC occupancy, NFPA 101 chapter, and licensing category — and the boundaries between them must be designed as separations, not lines on a site plan. The health center's health-care occupancy cannot bleed requirements into the independent living village, and the memory care perimeter must work within the assisted living building. Early code analysis maps every boundary before schematic design, because moving them later means redesigning MEP systems.

Shared infrastructure strategy

Central utility plants serve the campus efficiently but create single points of failure for a population that cannot tolerate outages — redundancy and emergency power for the plant are designed in. Kitchens often centralize production with finishing kitchens in each venue, which concentrates the grease-exhaust, gas, and plumbing scope. Phasing plans must keep occupied buildings fully serviced while new phases tie in: temporary utilities, construction separation, and infection-control barriers during health-center-adjacent work.

The regulatory stack

Beyond building codes, CCRCs face state continuing-care statutes — financial disclosure, entrance-fee regulation, and reserve requirements — plus the licensing regimes for each care level and, for the nursing component, CMS. The engineering team's deliverables feed all of these: the pro forma needs real utility and systems costs, and the licensing surveys will test the physical plant. Engage the state's CCRC regulator early alongside the building and health departments.

What else do project teams ask?

What contract types do CCRCs use?
Type A (lifecare, extensive contract), Type B (modified), and Type C (fee-for-service) — the contract sets the financial promise, which sets the care capacity the campus must physically deliver. Engineering follows the promise.
Can a CCRC be built in phases?
Yes, and usually is — independent living first, care buildings as the population ages. The master plan must reserve sites, utility capacity, and licensing pathways for later phases from day one.
Who regulates CCRCs?
The building department for construction, the health/licensing agencies for each care level, CMS for the nursing component, and usually a state insurance or aging department for the continuing-care contract itself.
