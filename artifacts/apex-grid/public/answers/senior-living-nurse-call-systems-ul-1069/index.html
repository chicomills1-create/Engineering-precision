What are the nurse call system requirements?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Nurse call in senior living follows UL 1069: listed equipment providing reliable two-way communication between residents and staff, emergency signaling from resident rooms and baths, and supervised circuits that report faults. Assisted living needs comprehensive coverage; nursing adds code-response capability. The system integrates with access control, wander management, and the fire alarm — coordination the electrical design must document.

What UL 1069 covers

UL 1069 is the safety standard for hospital and nursing signaling equipment: it tests reliability, supervision, audibility, and failure behavior. Specifying listed equipment matters for AHJ acceptance, insurer confidence, and liability — an unlisted improvised system is indefensible after an incident. The standard applies to the equipment; the design applies it to the building through device layouts, coverage verification, and integration sequences.

Coverage and device strategy

Resident rooms, bathrooms, common areas, and outdoor courtyards each need appropriate devices — bathrooms get waterproof pull cords reachable from the floor, because falls happen there. Corridor dome lights give staff at-a-glance location; consoles at nurse stations manage prioritization. Wireless pendants extend coverage beyond fixed devices but need battery-management and supervision discipline.

Integration points

Nurse call touches the fire alarm (alarm events display at nurse consoles), access control and wander management (elopement alarms route to the same staff), and the emergency power system (the system must work in an outage). Each integration is a documented sequence of operations with defined failure behavior — the specifications carry this, not just the drawings.

What else do project teams ask?

Is nurse call required by code?
Requirements come from the licensing category and the facility's program rather than a single building-code line — but licensed assisted living and nursing facilities are universally expected to have it, and surveyors check it.
Wired or wireless nurse call?
Wired is the reliability baseline for new construction; wireless adds flexibility for renovations and pendants. Either way the system needs supervision, emergency power, and documented coverage.
How does nurse call differ in memory care?
Memory care adds wander-management integration — elopement alarms route through the same staff notification the nurse call uses — and devices must be usable by cognitively impaired residents, which favors simple pull cords over complex stations.
