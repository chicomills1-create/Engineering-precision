How Are Mississippi Gulf Coast Casinos Structurally Engineered?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Mississippi Gulf Coast casinos are structurally engineered by designing for hurricane wind pressures under the IBC and ASCE 7, elevating occupied floors above the design flood elevation, founding structures on deep piles through loose coastal soils, and designing large-span gaming floors for vibration and heavy live loads — permitted by the city or Harrison County.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Wind Engineering for the Resort Envelope

The engineer starts with the site's wind climate: the design wind speed, the exposure category reflecting the open coastal terrain, and the building's risk category. The main wind force-resisting system — the shear walls or frames that keep the building standing — is designed for the full lateral loads with drift limits that protect the brittle finishes. But on a casino resort, the components-and-cladding design is nearly as critical: the vast glass facades, entrance canopies, porte-cocheres, and rooftop signage each see localized pressures far higher than the average, and each connection is engineered for its tributary pressure. The roof gets special attention because it is the most common hurricane failure point. The engineer designs the roof deck attachment for uplift, specifies the membrane and edge-metal systems for the wind rating the project requires, and details parapets and rooftop equipment — HVAC units, screens, penthouses — with their own anchorage for wind loads. Missiles are a design consideration too: the engineer specifies impact-resistant glazing or shutters for openings in the most exposed areas, and designs the backup systems — emergency generators, their fuel, and their enclosures — to survive the same storm the building is designed for.

Flood Elevation and Deep Foundations

Occupied floors are set above the design flood elevation with freeboard, and everything below is designed to get wet without structural damage. The engineer designs the below-flood structure for hydrostatic and hydrodynamic loads, buoyancy, and debris impact; specifies flood-resistant materials for all construction below the flood line; and locates breakaway walls where enclosures must fail safely rather than transfer flood forces to the frame. Mechanical, electrical, and plumbing systems — the building's life support — are elevated or floodproofed, because a casino that survives the wind but loses its building systems is still out of business. Foundations go deep through the coastal soil profile. The geotechnical investigation typically shows loose sands and soft clays overlying denser bearing strata, and the engineer designs driven piles or drilled shafts to reach that bearing layer. Pile design accounts for downdrag from consolidating soils, loss of lateral support from scour during surge, and the corrosive chemistry of saltwater-saturated soils — concrete mixes and reinforcement protection are specified accordingly. The engineer also checks global stability: a resort podium on piles in liquefiable or scourable soils needs the lateral system and the foundation to work as one, which the analysis verifies.

Gaming Floors, Towers, and Permitting

The gaming floor's long spans are engineered for both strength and feel. The engineer selects the floor system — composite steel, post-tensioned concrete, or a hybrid — for the heavy live loads, then runs vibration analyses for foot traffic and rhythmic activity, stiffening the framing until the floor meets the acceptance criteria. Column layouts are coordinated with the gaming operations team early, because moving a column after the floor is designed means redesigning the spans around it. Transfer girders carry tower columns down through the podium where the grids do not align, and these massive elements are designed for the concentrated loads with careful attention to shear and deflection. Permitting a casino is a multi-agency effort. The host city's building department reviews the structural, architectural, and MEP design under the adopted building code; the floodplain administrator reviews the elevation, floodproofing, and any compensatory measures; the fire marshal reviews egress, fire protection, and assembly occupancies. In unincorporated areas, the county performs these reviews with its own submittal portal and timelines. Gaming regulatory approvals run on a separate track but can impose design requirements — surveillance sightlines, secure count rooms, controlled access — that the engineer and architect coordinate into the structure. Every authority decides its own approval.

What else do project teams ask?

Why are Gulf Coast casinos built elevated?
After catastrophic hurricanes, Mississippi required casino gaming areas and critical spaces to sit above the design flood elevation. Elevating keeps storm surge out of the occupied spaces and the building's systems. Everything below the flood line is designed to survive flooding — breakaway walls, flood-resistant materials, and foundations engineered for scour.
What makes a casino gaming floor structurally difficult?
The combination of long column-free spans, heavy live loads from crowds and equipment, and strict vibration limits. Guests notice a bouncy floor immediately, so the engineer designs the framing stiffer than strength alone would require, verifying vibration performance analytically. Column locations are coordinated with gaming operations because the layout drives the span design.
How do the wind requirements affect the building's appearance?
They shape it more than most guests realize: the glass curtain wall, entrance canopies, signage, and roofing are all engineered for high localized wind pressures, with impact-resistant glazing or shutters in exposed areas. The structural frame — shear walls or braced frames — is sized for the full hurricane lateral loads. Good design integrates all of this invisibly into the architecture.
Who reviews a casino's structural design on the Mississippi Gulf Coast?
The host city's building department — Biloxi, Gulfport, and neighboring cities each enforce the state building code — or the county for unincorporated sites, plus the floodplain administrator for elevation and floodproofing review and the fire marshal for life safety. Gaming regulators review on a separate track. Submittal portals, timelines, and inspection scheduling differ between city and county jurisdictions.
