OKC vs. Unincorporated Oklahoma County: Which Permit Path Applies?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Oklahoma City projects permit through the city's Development Services Department; unincorporated Oklahoma County projects permit through the Oklahoma County Planning Commission, which has its own checklist including deed records, easements, perc tests, and a sealed stormwater plan for one-acre-plus disturbances. The parcel location, not its proximity to downtown, determines the AHJ.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

The County Submittal: What Oklahoma County Asks For

The Oklahoma County Planning Commission's residential permit packet is a paper trail of ownership and access. The deed with book and page number proves legal ownership; easement copies prove legal access; the site plan must show setbacks from the centerline of section-line roads, which are the county's arterial grid, plus side and rear setbacks, all existing and proposed structures, driveway location, and a north arrow. The percolation and soil test from the Oklahoma Department of Environmental Quality addresses wastewater where municipal sewer does not reach. For larger disturbances, the stormwater site development plan sealed by a licensed civil engineer, with the notice of intent on file with the state, brings the project under the county's stormwater management regulations. The county's subdivision regulations add teeth for developers: no lot may be sold before the final plat is approved by the Planning Commission and the Board of County Commissioners and filed with the Registrar of Deeds, no building permit issues for a structure on a lot created in violation of the regulations, and the county has no obligation to issue a certificate of occupancy for such parcels. The County Engineer administers the regulations and handles enforcement, and violations are misdemeanors under the statute. These are not suggestions; they are the conditions on which a rural project lives or dies.

The City Submittal: How OKC's Path Differs

Inside city limits, the engineering team works to Oklahoma City's municipal standards. Zoning compliance, platting through the city's planning process, the city's drainage and stormwater design standards, and municipal utility extension policies all apply, and the review runs through Development Services with the city's own plan-check correction cycles. Commercial projects face the full commercial submittal — civil site plans, architectural and structural sets, MEP design, landscape plans — reviewed by city staff against the adopted building codes and local amendments. The practical difference is process density. The city path typically involves more reviewers, more specialized submittals, and longer coordination with utility providers, while the county path is leaner but absolute on its own checklist — miss the deed book and page or the perc test and the application does not move. Either way, the project should confirm the AHJ in writing before design begins, because a set drawn for city drainage standards does not automatically satisfy the county's stormwater regulations, and resubmittal to the other government costs months. The AHJ decides what is approved, so the engineer designs to the AHJ that actually holds the parcel.

What else do project teams ask?

How do I know if my project is in Oklahoma City or unincorporated Oklahoma County?
Check the parcel against the city's official boundary or zoning map, or ask the county assessor's office. Proximity to downtown means nothing — the city boundary is irregular, and county pockets sit inside the metro. If the parcel is outside all municipal boundaries, it is unincorporated county and permits through the Oklahoma County Planning Commission.
What does Oklahoma County require for a residential building permit?
The county's published checklist includes the filed deed with book and page number, copies of access easements, a perc and soil test from the state environmental quality department, a dimensioned site plan with setbacks from the section-line road, a floor plan, and for one-acre-plus disturbances a stormwater site development plan sealed by a licensed civil engineer with a notice of intent filed with the state.
How long is an Oklahoma County building permit valid?
Per the county's published requirements, construction must begin within six months of permit approval and be completed within two years of approval, or a new permit must be obtained. Plan the construction schedule around that window before pulling the permit.
Can a county project be denied a certificate of occupancy?
Yes. The county's subdivision regulations state the county has no obligation to issue a certificate of occupancy for a parcel created or a building constructed in violation of the regulations, and no building permit may issue for a structure on a lot subdivided or sold in violation. Plat compliance comes before construction, not after.
