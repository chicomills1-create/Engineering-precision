How Does Floodplain Development Work at Table Rock Lake?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Table Rock Lake floodplain development is controlled by the Corps of Engineers' pool elevations, 915 conservation, 917 seasonal, 931 flood pool, 936 surcharge and flowage easement, with no habitable structures or contour changes allowed in the easement without Corps approval. Above the easement, the county floodplain administrator governs finished-floor elevations and floodproofing, with the county AHJ varying by shoreline mile across the lake's five counties.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

The Pool Elevations and What They Restrict

The four elevations are worth memorizing because every shoreline decision references them. The conservation pool near 915 feet is the lake's normal operating level and the datum for shoreline measurements like dock spacing. The seasonal pool to 917 feet reflects summer operations. The top of the flood pool at 931 feet is the level the reservoir is designed to reach during flood control operations, and any structure, wall, or utility below it must be designed for submergence. The 936-foot level is both the top of the surcharge pool and the general upper limit of the federal flowage easement, and it is the line where the most restrictive rules apply: the government's perpetual right to flood, prohibitions on habitable structures and waste systems, and a ban on changing land contours without Corps approval. For the design team, these elevations translate into vertical zoning. Docks, ramps, and shoreline stabilization permitted through the Corps' shoreline program occupy the water's edge under agency authorization. Retaining walls and bank work must be designed for the full pool range and permitted through the shoreline process, with the structural design handling submergence, wave action, and saturated backfill at high pool. Upland structures go above the easement and above the locally required flood elevation, with the lowest finished floor documented by elevation certificate. Utilities are routed and detailed for the flood environment: electrical and mechanical equipment goes above the flood level, and any infrastructure below it is designed for submergence or protected accordingly. The survey is the foundation of all of it, because every elevation requirement is enforced against surveyed heights, not estimates.

County Floodplain Rules and the Permit Sequence

Below the federal layer, the county floodplain ordinance does the day-to-day regulatory work. The county floodplain administrator reviews development permits for parcels in the mapped floodplain, checking finished-floor elevations against the base flood elevation, reviewing floodproofing designs for non-residential structures, and requiring elevation certificates as the proof of compliance. Substantial improvements to existing structures trigger the same elevation requirements as new construction, which matters around a lake with decades of older cabins and homes built before current standards. The ordinance also governs fill in the floodplain, since filling the floodplain displaces flood storage and raises water on neighboring properties, so grading plans near the shore face compensatory storage or fill restrictions. Sequencing the approvals is a project-management discipline. The Corps shoreline authorization leads for any work within the federal interest, because no county permit can authorize what the Corps prohibits. The county floodplain development permit follows with the elevation and floodproofing documentation. Building, grading, and trade permits layer on through the county or city AHJ for the parcel, and environmental permits cover stream and wetland impacts. Pre-application meetings with the Corps project office and the county floodplain administrator are the cheapest hours on the project: they confirm which authorizations a given scope actually needs, what the current shoreline management requirements are, and what the realistic review timelines look like. The agencies decide what is approved, and a submittal that arrives with surveyed elevations, a coherent flood strategy, and complete documentation moves while an incomplete one waits.

What else do project teams ask?

What is a flowage easement at Table Rock Lake?
A flowage easement is the federal government's perpetual right to temporarily flood private land during reservoir operations, and at Table Rock it generally extends to elevation 936 feet. It carries explicit restrictions: no structures for human habitation, no waste disposal systems, no changes to land contours, and Corps approval required for construction of any type within the easement. It is a federal interest that runs with the land regardless of who owns it.
Can I build a house below elevation 936 at Table Rock Lake?
Not a habitable structure within the flowage easement, where federal restrictions prohibit it. Eligible pre-existing structures affected by the federal boundary above 936 feet may be addressed through the Corps' boundary line resolution process. New residential construction belongs above the easement and above the locally required flood elevation, with the county floodplain administrator and the Corps each reviewing their respective requirements. The agencies decide what is approved for the specific parcel.
Who issues floodplain permits around Table Rock Lake?
The county floodplain administrator for the county where the parcel sits, which varies because the lake spans Stone, Taney, and Barry counties in Missouri and Boone and Carroll counties in Arkansas. The Corps of Engineers separately authorizes shoreline work through its shoreline management program. A project near the water typically needs both, plus building and grading permits from the county or city AHJ, sequenced with the Corps authorization first.
What is an elevation certificate?
An elevation certificate is a surveyed document recording the elevations of a building's lowest floor, adjacent grade, and related floodplain features, prepared by a licensed surveyor. Floodplain administrators use it to verify that the structure meets the required elevation above the base flood elevation, and it is also the basis for flood insurance rating. Around Table Rock Lake it is the standard proof of floodplain compliance for new construction and substantial improvements.
