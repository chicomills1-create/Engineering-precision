How Are Plano Corporate Campuses Structurally Designed?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Plano corporate campuses are structurally designed with steel or concrete frames on deep foundations suited to North Texas expansive clays, long-span floor systems for flexible office layouts, curtain-wall facades engineered for wind loads, redundant mechanical and electrical systems for continuous operations, and site and utility coordination with the City of Plano's development review process.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Long-Span Floors and Vibration Control

The composite steel floor system is the workhorse of the Plano corporate campus: steel beams with shear studs locked into the concrete slab above, acting as a single structural unit. The engineer sizes beams for the strength demand, then checks the two serviceability criteria that actually govern office design - deflection under live load and floor vibration from footfall. A floor strong enough but too lively will generate endless tenant complaints, so the design targets natural frequencies and damping levels that keep walking-induced vibration imperceptible. Where post-tensioned concrete is used instead, the engineer designs the tendon layout for the long spans and details the slab edges for the facade loads. Flexibility is engineered in, not added later. The structural grid is coordinated with the tenant improvement allowance: core zones carry the heavy, permanent loads - restrooms, stairs, elevators, mechanical shafts - while the open office areas stay light and reconfigurable. Future-proofing details include spare electrical and structural capacity at the roof for equipment additions, knock-out panels in shear walls where future openings may be needed, and floor loading allowances that anticipate denser workstation layouts. When a tenant later wants a interconnecting stair between floors, the engineer who planned for it can cut the opening without a structural crisis.

Foundations, Facades, and Phased Permitting

Drilled piers are the standard answer to North Texas clay for campus-scale buildings. The geotechnical investigation sets pier depths, diameters, and allowable capacities, and the structural engineer lays out the pier grid to match the column loads - heavier at the braced frames and cores where lateral forces concentrate. Grade beams span between piers to support the exterior walls, and the ground-floor slab is designed per the geotechnical recommendations, which may call for a structural slab spanning pier to pier where the clays are most active. Waterproofing and drainage at the foundation keep the expansive soils around the building at stable moisture. The facade is a structural system in its own right. Curtain-wall mullions span floor to floor carrying wind loads into the slab edges, with connections detailed for the building's thermal movement and interstory drift in wind events. Precast panels hang from or bear on the frame with connections that allow adjustment during erection and movement in service. Permitting a campus through the City of Plano means navigating site plan approval, phased building permits, and separate permits for parking structures and central plants - each with the city's submittal standards and review schedules. The AHJ reviews every phase on its merits and decides.

What else do project teams ask?

Why do corporate office floors need vibration analysis?
Because strength alone does not make a comfortable office. Long-span floors can bounce perceptibly under footfall even when they are far from any strength limit, and that liveliness distracts workers and can disturb sensitive equipment. The engineer checks the floor's natural frequency and damping during design and adjusts beam sizes, spans, or damping to keep vibration imperceptible.
What foundation type works in Plano's clay soils?
Drilled piers socketed below the active clay zone are the standard for campus-scale buildings. The piers bypass the expansive surface soils and bear in stable strata, while grade beams and structural slabs handle the building's perimeter and ground floor. The geotechnical report sets the depths and capacities; the structural engineer turns those into the foundation design.
How are corporate campuses built in phases without structural problems?
Each phase is designed as a structurally independent building with its own lateral system and foundations, so one phase never depends on a future phase for stability. The engineer coordinates shared elements - parking structures, utility tunnels, central plants - and details expansion joints and future connections where phases will eventually meet.
Who reviews corporate campus construction in Plano?
The City of Plano, through its development review and building permit process - site plans, building permits for each phase, and separate permits for structures like parking garages and central utility plants. Each follows the city's submittal requirements and review timelines. The authority having jurisdiction evaluates every submittal and decides the approval; phased projects should expect multiple concurrent reviews.
