How Should Gymnasium Ventilation Be Designed?

A competition gym swings from empty on a Tuesday to 2,000 spectators on a Friday night — and the ventilation design has to serve both without wasting energy or warping the wood floor. ASHRAE 62.1 sets the outdoor-air baseline for the occupancy, but the design work is in humidity control, distribution, and turndown.

The concise answer

Ventilate to ASHRAE 62.1 assembly rates, control humidity to protect the playing surface (wood floors move with moisture — typically target 35–50% RH in the gym), distribute air without creating drafts across the court, and use demand-controlled ventilation with variable-speed equipment so the empty-weekday gym sips energy. The floor manufacturer's humidity warranty usually has opinions about your HVAC design; read it before selecting equipment.

Humidity and the wood floor

Maple competition floors are humidity instruments: too dry and they shrink and gap, too humid and they cup and buckle. The HVAC design must hold the gym in the floor's happy range year-round, which in humid climates means dedicated dehumidification capacity — not just cooling — and in dry climates means watching winter humidification needs. Slab moisture and envelope vapor detailing matter as much as the air handler.

Distribution and noise

Gym air distribution must reach the occupied zone without blasting the court — low-velocity displacement or carefully placed high outlets, never a direct throw across the playing surface. And the system must be quiet: a gym where the air handler roars over the announcer fails its primary function. Equipment sound ratings, duct silencers, and vibration isolation are part of the ventilation design, not extras.

Frequently asked questions

What humidity should a gym maintain?
Typically 35–50% RH to protect wood competition floors — check the floor manufacturer's warranty, which usually specifies the range. Concrete or synthetic floors are more forgiving, but occupant comfort still argues for control.
How is gym ventilation controlled for varying occupancy?
Demand-controlled ventilation using CO2 sensors, scheduled around the school's event calendar, with variable-speed fans. The system ramps for Friday-night crowds and sets back for empty weekdays.
Why does gym air movement matter for play?
Drafts across the court affect ball flight in volleyball and basketball and athlete comfort everywhere. Distribution should deliver air to the occupied zone at low velocity, never a direct high-velocity throw across the playing surface.
Do school gyms need air conditioning?
In most U.S. climates, yes for competition gyms: internal gains from occupants and lighting plus envelope loads overwhelm ventilation-only cooling, and humidity control for wood floors requires mechanical dehumidification anyway.
