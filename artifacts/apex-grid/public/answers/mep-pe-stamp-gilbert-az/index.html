What Does a PE Check Before Sealing MEP Drawings in Gilbert, AZ?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Before sealing MEP drawings in Gilbert, the PE verifies HVAC loads and equipment against desert design conditions, checks plumbing risers and fixture counts for code compliance, confirms the electrical distribution agrees end to end, and reconciles energy compliance with the sealed design.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

The pre-seal verification pass

The PE's verification runs discipline by discipline, and each has its own failure pattern. Mechanical starts with the load calculations: design conditions appropriate for Gilbert's summers, realistic internal gains, ventilation per code with the outside-air load fully accounted for. Then equipment: selections matched to the loads at peak and part-load, with controls sequences that actually deliver the design intent. Plumbing runs from the fixture layouts through the risers — counts per occupancy, pipe sizing and slopes, venting, water heating, and specialty systems like grease waste detailed to code. Electrical runs from the service calculation through the riser to the panel schedules: loads, breaker and feeder sizing, fault current, and overcurrent coordination all in agreement. The energy compliance forms get reconciled last, against the finished design. Every discrepancy becomes a marked revision, and the PE re-checks the corrections before sealing.

Energy compliance in an Arizona jurisdiction

Gilbert enforces the adopted energy code at plan check, and the compliance documentation is where fast-tracked projects most often stumble. The forms have to describe the building that's actually on the drawings — not the building from two design iterations ago. I check the envelope inputs against the architectural drawings, the lighting power against the lighting plans and controls, and the mechanical efficiencies against the equipment schedules. In a cooling-dominated climate like Gilbert's, the mechanical side carries the most weight: equipment efficiency, duct design, and controls sequencing are where the real energy performance lives, and the compliance model has to reflect the selections the PE is sealing. When the model and the drawings tell the same story, plan check moves on to more interesting questions.

Gilbert MEP pre-seal checklist

An MEP set is ready for the PE's verification pass — and then for the town — when these items are resolved. Internal coordination is the theme, because that is where Gilbert corrections come from. • HVAC load calculations with Gilbert design conditions and ventilation loads fully accounted for • Equipment schedules matching the mechanical plans at peak and part-load performance • Plumbing risers showing every fixture on the plans, with code-compliant sizing and venting • Electrical riser, panel schedules, and service calculation in full agreement • Energy compliance documentation reconciled against the sealed design, not an earlier iteration • MEP layouts coordinated with structural framing and architectural backgrounds

What else do project teams ask?

What are the most common MEP corrections on Gilbert projects?
The greatest hits are coordination failures, not engineering mysteries. Equipment schedules that don't match the mechanical plans — a unit shown at one capacity on the plan and another on the schedule. Panel schedules whose calculated loads don't agree with the electrical riser. Plumbing risers that don't show every fixture on the floor plans, or show pipe sizing the code won't accept. And energy compliance forms that describe a different building than the drawings do. The PE's pre-seal verification pass is designed to find exactly these: I read the set the way a plan reviewer reads it, so the markups happen on my desk instead of costing a town review cycle.
Does the PE verify the energy compliance model before sealing?
Yes. The energy compliance documentation is part of the sealed design package, and the PE checks that it describes the building on the drawings: envelope values consistent with the architectural set, lighting power densities matching the lighting plans, and mechanical equipment efficiencies matching the schedules. Gilbert enforces the adopted energy code at plan check, and a compliance model that disagrees with the drawings is one of the most avoidable corrections in the business. I reconcile the forms against the sealed sheets before the stamp goes on — fixing it afterward means a resubmittal over paperwork.
Does tenant improvement MEP work in Gilbert still need a sealed set?
Often, yes. TI work that touches the HVAC system, alters plumbing, or modifies the electrical distribution generally needs engineered drawings, and the town's reviewers apply the same standards to a TI as to new construction — the code doesn't grade on project size. A restaurant TI with a new kitchen exhaust and grease waste system, or an office TI that reworks the air distribution and adds electrical load, will need a PE's review and seal on the MEP sheets. The review-and-seal sequence is the same: real review, verified calculations where the code requires them, marked revisions, then the seal. Small project, same professional standard.
How do I avoid a second MEP review cycle with the town?
Submit a complete, coordinated, sealed set the first time. The projects that sail through town review are the ones where the PE already ran the gauntlet: loads verified, equipment matched, risers and panels in agreement, energy forms reconciled, and every correction from the PE's own markup cycle addressed and clouded. What earns a second cycle is almost always incompleteness — a missing riser diagram, a schedule that contradicts the plan, a compliance form for a different design. The PE's verification pass exists to make the first submittal the one that counts. Budget one correction round in the schedule anyway, because even clean sets occasionally draw a reviewer question.
