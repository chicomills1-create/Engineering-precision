Do EV Charger Installations Need a PE Stamp?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Commercial and multi-charger EV installations typically need a PE stamp covering the electrical load analysis, panel and service capacity, and — for canopy chargers — the structural support. Single residential Level 2 chargers with existing panel space usually permit without engineered documents.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

The electrical review: loads, panels, service

The load analysis is the foundation of the electrical review. I build the calculation from the building's existing loads — using either the code's calculation methods or documented demand data where the code permits — then add the EV charging as continuous load with the code's sizing factor. That total gets checked against the panel's bus rating, the feeder conductors, and the service equipment. Where the numbers don't fit, the design step is honest about the options: load management systems that cap simultaneous charging demand, panel replacement, or full service upsizing, each with its cost and timeline implications laid out. Load management deserves attention because it's the value-engineering lever in commercial EV projects. A listed energy management system can allow more chargers on an existing service by capping the simultaneous draw, and the code recognizes it — but the design has to show the control architecture, the capped demand used in the calculations, and the failure-mode behavior. I verify the load management design as carefully as a service upgrade, because a system that sheds load unpredictably is a different kind of failure.

Structural scope and California's Title 24 layer

Where chargers mount to structures — canopies, parking structures, building walls — the structural review verifies the added loads and designs the supports. Canopy structures get the full treatment: foundations for overturning and uplift, columns and beams for gravity plus wind and seismic, and charger mounting details with the equipment weights confirmed from cut sheets. Wall-mounted chargers on existing structures need the attachment verified into the actual wall construction. None of this is exotic engineering, but all of it has to be in the sealed set for the AHJ to approve the installation. In California, the Title 24 EV infrastructure requirements ride on top of the base electrical design. I confirm the count and type of EV spaces required for the occupancy and scope, verify the design provides the raceways, capacity, and equipment each category needs, and reconcile the compliance forms with the drawings before sealing. AHJs in California check this at plan review, and a set that undercounts the EV-ready spaces earns a correction that ripples through the panel schedule.

EV charger PE review checklist

An EV charging submittal is ready for the PE's seal when the electrical math, the service reality, and any structural scope are all resolved. Utility coordination starts in parallel, not after. • Load analysis with EV charging as continuous load, checked against panel bus, feeders, and service capacity • Panel schedules and one-line diagram showing the charging distribution clearly • Service adequate or upgrade designed; utility application opened with the PE's load numbers • Load management architecture documented and verified where used to avoid service upsizing • Structural design for canopies, wall mounts, and equipment pads — foundations, wind, seismic • Title 24 EV infrastructure compliance documented and reconciled for California projects

What else do project teams ask?

Why can't I just add a breaker for an EV charger?
Because the code sizes EV charging as a continuous load, which changes the math. A charger's rated current gets multiplied for conductor and overcurrent sizing, and that larger number has to fit within the panel's bus rating, the feeder's ampacity, and the service's calculated load. On a panel that's already near capacity — common in older commercial buildings — the 'simple breaker addition' becomes a panel upgrade or a service upsizing. The PE's load analysis is what determines which one you're facing before you've bought equipment. Skipping the analysis and discovering the shortfall at final inspection is the expensive path.
What does Title 24 require for EV charging in California?
California's energy code requires EV charging infrastructure — a mix of EV-capable, EV-ready, and EVSE-installed spaces depending on the building type and scope — for new construction and certain alterations. The electrical design has to provide the raceways, panel capacity, and in some cases the installed chargers the code section requires, and the compliance forms documenting it are part of the permit set. I verify the EV infrastructure design against the current Title 24 requirements for the project type, because the requirements have expanded over recent code cycles and older assumptions undercount. The PE's sealed set includes the compliance documentation reconciled with the drawings.
Do canopy EV chargers need structural engineering?
Yes — a charging canopy is a structure, and it gets engineered like one. The PE designs or verifies the columns, beams, foundations, and the charger mounting for gravity loads, wind, and seismic per the site's criteria. Open canopies see significant wind uplift, and the foundations have to handle overturning, which rules out the shallow post bases people imagine. Vehicle impact protection — bollards or barriers — is typically an AHJ requirement the design must show. The structural calculations and drawings for the canopy are sealed alongside the electrical set.
How does utility coordination affect the EV charger timeline?
More than the permit does, in many cases. Load additions large enough to need engineered documents are usually large enough to trigger the utility's review: service upgrade applications, transformer capacity checks, and sometimes new service lateral construction. Utility timelines run on the utility's schedule — weeks to months — and they run in parallel with, not after, the permit. I advise owners to open the utility application the same week the engineering starts, because a sealed permit set means nothing if the transformer can't serve the load. The PE's load analysis gives the utility the numbers their application asks for.
