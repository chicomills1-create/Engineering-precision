Is Permitting Different in Nashville vs. Surrounding Counties?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Nashville-Davidson County's consolidated government routes structural permits through one authority, Metro Codes, with a single portal and reviewer pool. Surrounding counties each run their own building departments, and satellite cities inside Davidson County keep their own permits too. The parcel address, not the mailing address, determines the AHJ, which decides what is approved.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Inside Davidson County: One Government, a Few Exceptions

The 1963 consolidation merged Nashville's city and Davidson County's county governments, which is why a warehouse in Antioch and a tower downtown both permit through Metro Codes. The practical benefit is consistency: the same structural plan reviewer standards, the same amendment package, and one online permitting portal for the whole county. For engineers, that means one set of submittal habits covers the largest share of Middle Tennessee commercial work. The exceptions are the satellite cities. An addition in Forest Hills or a new build in Belle Meade needs that city's building permit, and the city's reviewers apply their own standards on top of county-level zoning and stormwater rules. Goodlettsville straddles the Davidson-Sumner line, so the parcel's county side decides which county's rules layer under the city permit. These edge cases are exactly where projects stall, because the owner assumed Metro Codes was the only stop. A parcel lookup with the county assessor and a call to the municipal clerk resolve the question in an afternoon.

Outside the County Line: Every County Is Its Own AHJ

In Williamson County, an unincorporated site permits through the county's building codes department, while a site inside Franklin city limits goes to Franklin's own department with different reviewers and expectations. Rutherford County mirrors the split between the county department and the City of Murfreesboro's Building and Codes Department. Sumner and Wilson counties run the same county-versus-city pattern around Hendersonville, Gallatin, Lebanon, and Mount Juliet. Each department publishes its own submittal checklists, fee schedules, and inspection scheduling, and none of them is required to match its neighbor. The engineering scope adjusts to the AHJ. Some county departments lean on third-party review agencies for structural plan check, which changes the correction format and response process. Stormwater, floodplain, and fire district rules can differ sharply between an incorporated city and the unincorporated county around it, affecting grading, detention, and access design. Because the AHJ decides what is approved, the engineer builds the permit strategy around the specific department: its portal, its amendment package, its current review timelines, and the correction items its reviewers are actually writing this quarter.

What else do project teams ask?

What is a consolidated government and how does it affect permits?
A consolidated government merges city and county functions, so Nashville-Davidson County has one building department, one zoning code, and one permit portal for the whole county. The effect is a single, consistent structural permit path instead of separate city and county processes. Satellite cities inside the county are the main exception, keeping their own building permits.
Do I need a Metro Nashville permit if my project is in Belle Meade or Forest Hills?
Those incorporated satellite cities issue their own building permits through their own departments, so the city permit is the primary structural approval. Metro-level requirements such as zoning or stormwater may still apply depending on the project. Confirm with the city's building official and Metro Codes before assuming either office is the only stop.
How do Williamson County and City of Franklin permits differ?
Unincorporated Williamson County projects go through the county's building codes department, while projects inside Franklin go to the city's own department. Each has its own reviewers, submittal standards, fee schedule, and inspection process. A project on the city-county boundary must be sited precisely, because the parcel location decides the AHJ.
Can one set of sealed drawings be submitted in multiple counties?
The sealed structural design generally transfers, but each AHJ applies its own amendment package, submittal format, and supporting-documentation requirements. Geotechnical reports, special inspection programs, and energy compliance forms often need jurisdiction-specific adjustments. The engineer adapts the permit package to each department rather than resubmitting an identical set.
Who decides whether my permit is approved?
The authority having jurisdiction for the parcel decides what is approved, and each department's reviewers apply their own amendment package and standards. Timelines vary with workload, so confirm current processing times with the actual reviewing department before committing to a construction schedule.
