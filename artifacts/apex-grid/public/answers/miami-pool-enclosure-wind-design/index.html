How Are Miami Pool Enclosures Designed for Wind?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Miami pool enclosures are engineered as aluminum-framed structures designed for the Florida Building Code's high-velocity hurricane zone wind loads, with corrosion-resistant fasteners for the salt-air climate and footings sized for uplift, usually permitted as a separate structure through the City of Miami or Miami-Dade County with product-approval documentation.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Wind Loads on Screen Enclosures in the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone

The high-velocity hurricane zone designation means Miami-Dade enclosures are designed for higher wind speeds and stricter detailing than most of Florida. Wind pressure on a screen enclosure acts on the full projected area of the screen, so a large enclosure accumulates thousands of pounds of lateral and uplift force that must resolve through the frame into the footings. The engineer calculates pressures for the enclosure's height, exposure, and location, then sizes each beam and column for the tributary area it supports, checking both strength and deflection so the frame does not flex enough to tear the screen or pop connections. Connections are where enclosures historically fail. The engineer designs the beam-to-column and column-to-footing connections for the combined uplift and lateral loads, specifies through-bolts or engineered brackets rather than relying on screws alone, and details the screen attachment with spline and fastener schedules that develop the mesh strength. Where the enclosure attaches to the house, the connection must engage the house's structural framing, not just fascia or trim, and the engineer verifies the house framing can take the added load.

Corrosion, Footings, and Getting the Permit

In Miami's salt air, an enclosure's lifespan is set by its corrosion detailing. The engineer specifies stainless steel or properly coated fasteners, requires isolation between aluminum and any dissimilar metal, and selects aluminum alloys and finishes rated for marine exposure. Fastener schedules call out the grade and coating explicitly, because a corroded connection loses capacity silently until a storm tests it. Periodic washing and inspection are part of the owner's maintenance, but the engineering has to give the structure a fighting chance first. Footings for enclosures are small but heavily loaded in uplift. The engineer sizes spread footings or piles for the net uplift after subtracting the structure's light self-weight, checks overturning, and details anchor bolts with proper embedment and edge distances in the sandy, high-water-table soils. The permit package includes the structural calculations, product approvals or site-specific design, the footing plan, and connection details, submitted through the city or county portal depending on jurisdiction. Inspectors verify footing depth, anchor bolt placement, and member sizes in the field before the screen goes on.

What else do project teams ask?

What is the high-velocity hurricane zone?
It is the Florida Building Code designation for Miami-Dade and Broward counties, where hurricane risk is highest and the code imposes its strictest wind design and product approval requirements. Structures there, including pool enclosures, must meet higher wind pressures and use approved products or site-specific engineering. The designation exists because South Florida's hurricane exposure exceeds the rest of the state's.
Does a pool enclosure need its own permit?
Yes. A pool enclosure is permitted as a separate structure from the house, with its own structural calculations, product approvals, and footing inspections. It cannot be covered under the original house permit. The enclosure permit also triggers zoning review for setbacks and lot coverage, which is why the engineer or contractor confirms the enclosure fits the lot's zoning envelope before designing it.
Why do screen enclosures collapse in hurricanes?
Most failures trace to connections, not members: undersized or corroded fasteners, columns anchored to inadequate footings, or attachments to house fascia instead of structural framing. Uplift peels the frame from its footings when footings are too small or anchor bolts too shallow, and racking collapses the frame when bracing or moment connections are missing. An engineered enclosure with proper connections, footings, and corrosion detailing is designed specifically to survive the code's design wind event.
Is permitting different in the City of Miami versus unincorporated Miami-Dade?
Yes. The City of Miami's building department reviews permits inside city limits, while Miami-Dade County's regulatory department handles unincorporated areas. Both enforce the Florida Building Code's high-velocity hurricane zone provisions, so the engineering is equivalent, but the submittal portals, fees, review timelines, and inspection scheduling differ. The authority having jurisdiction decides approval in either case.
