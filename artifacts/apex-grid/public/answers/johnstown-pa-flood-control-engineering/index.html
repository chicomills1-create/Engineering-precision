How Does Flood-Control Engineering Protect Johnstown PA?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Johnstown PA is protected by flood-control engineering that confines rivers in engineered channels, builds levees and floodwalls along vulnerable reaches, maintains dams and debris basins upstream, models flood levels to size every structure, and coordinates with the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers and the City of Johnstown on maintenance and permitting.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Channel Design and Flood Modeling

Flood-control design begins with hydrology: how much water arrives, and how fast. The engineer builds rainfall-runoff models of the watershed using gage data, soil characteristics, and land-use mapping, then checks the model against the historic floods--in Johnstown, that record is brutally well documented. The design flood is selected per the applicable standards for the protection level sought, and the hydraulic model routes that flow through the proposed channels, computing water-surface elevations at every bridge, bend, and constriction. If the model shows a bridge deck going underwater or a bend overtopping, the design changes until the profile works. Channel detailing turns the model into something that survives real floods. Linings resist the velocities the model predicts; grade-control sills prevent the channel bed from degrading and undermining the walls; access ramps let maintenance equipment reach the channel for debris removal. The engineer designs for sediment and debris explicitly--Johnstown's steep tributaries deliver both in quantity--with debris basins and trash racks at critical points. After construction, the as-built channel is surveyed and the model is updated, because a channel that differs from the model protects to a different standard than the one on paper.

Levees, Floodwalls, and Closure Operations

Levee design is geotechnical engineering with flood consequences. The embankment section is sized for stability under full flood loading, with seepage analysis checking that water does not pipe through or under the levee--the classic failure mode. Cutoff trenches, relief wells, or seepage berms control underseepage where foundation soils are permeable. The levee crown carries an inspection road, slopes are armored against wave and current erosion, and every utility penetration through the levee gets a designed antiseep detail, because an unsealed pipe through a levee is a built-in failure point. Floodwalls serve where levees cannot fit. These reinforced concrete walls are designed for hydrostatic and hydrodynamic loads, debris impact, and the scour that flood flows carve at their base--often on deep foundations where soils are poor. Closure structures at crossings are engineered systems in their own right: stoplog slots, gate frames, and storage for the closure pieces, with an operations plan naming who installs them, on what trigger, and how long installation takes. The engineer runs tabletop exercises of the closure sequence with the city, because flood protection that depends on human action must be rehearsed before the water rises.

What else do project teams ask?

What floods shaped Johnstown's flood-control system?
Three catastrophic floods: 1889, when the South Fork Dam failed and killed over 2,200 people; 1936, the St. Patrick's Day flood that inundated the downtown; and 1977, when heavy rains overwhelmed the watershed again. Each disaster drove a new generation of flood-control construction--the channelization and dam projects that protect the city today were engineered directly from those events.
What is the difference between a levee and a floodwall?
A levee is an engineered earthen embankment with specified soils, compaction, and seepage controls, used where there is room for its wide footprint. A floodwall is a reinforced concrete structure used in tight urban reaches where no embankment fits. Both are designed for the design flood plus freeboard, and both need closure structures at road and rail crossings.
Who maintains Johnstown's flood-control works?
Responsibility is layered: the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers maintains the major federal channel and dam works, the City of Johnstown handles local drainage, closure operations, and coordination, and the state oversees dam safety. Private development in the floodplain is reviewed by the local floodplain administrator. The authority having jurisdiction decides approvals for any work affecting the flood-control system or the floodplain.
Can you build in Johnstown's floodplain?
Yes, but under floodplain construction requirements: structures must be elevated or floodproofed to the regulatory flood elevation, with engineered foundations, flood-resistant materials below that level, and proper anchorage. The local floodplain administrator reviews every project, and the engineer designs to the flood maps and ordinance in effect--not to the last flood's high-water mark.
