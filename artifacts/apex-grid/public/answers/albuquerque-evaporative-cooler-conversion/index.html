How Do Albuquerque Evaporative-Cooler Conversions Work?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Albuquerque evaporative-cooler conversions work by replacing the swamp cooler with refrigerated air: the engineer calculates the home's cooling load, designs ductwork or adapts existing ducts, sizes a right-fit condenser and air handler, upgrades the electrical service as needed, and seals the old roof or window openings.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Sizing the System to the House

The load calculation treats the house as it is, not as the cooler assumed it was. The engineer measures or verifies insulation levels, window areas and orientations, air leakage, and internal gains, then computes the design heating and cooling load for Albuquerque's design temperatures. That number - not the cooler's tonnage, not a square-foot rule of thumb - selects the equipment. In a well-shaded adobe or a leaky 1960s ranch, the right size can differ dramatically from what a contractor might guess, and the calculation is the document that justifies it. Duct design follows the room-by-room loads. Supply runs are sized for the airflow and the static pressure the selected air handler can deliver, with short, straight, sealed runs preferred over the long flex duct sags that strangle performance. Return air gets a designed path - grilles, chases, or ducted returns - because a system that cannot get air back to the equipment cannot deliver comfort no matter how well the supplies are sized. The design includes a commissioning checklist: measured airflow per room, verified against the design, before the contractor leaves.

Electrical, Envelope, and the Old Cooler

The electrical check is arithmetic the AHJ will verify. The engineer performs a load calculation on the existing service, adds the condenser, air handler, and any electric heat, and determines whether the panel and service conductors suffice or an upgrade is required. Many Albuquerque homes make the jump from 100 to 200 amps in the same project, which also future-proofs for an electric water heater or vehicle charger. The permit set shows the panel schedule, the feeder sizing, and the grounding - the details the electrical reviewer checks. The old cooler leaves a hole, and the envelope repair is engineered, not patched. A roof curb removal gets the roof structure reframed, sheathed, dried-in, and flashed to match the existing roofing; a window unit opening gets reframed and finished to match the wall. The engineer specifies the repair so the thermal envelope and the weather barrier are continuous again - air sealing and insulation at the repair are part of the energy performance the conversion promises. Skipping this step carefully is how conversions develop the roof leaks owners blame on the new equipment.

What else do project teams ask?

Is refrigerated air better than evaporative cooling in Albuquerque?
It depends on priorities. Evaporative coolers cost less to run in dry heat but lose effectiveness during monsoon humidity, require open windows that admit smoke and dust, and need seasonal maintenance. Refrigerated air holds setpoint in any humidity, filters the air, and dehumidifies - at higher equipment and operating cost. The engineer's load calculation and cost comparison let the owner decide on numbers rather than assumptions.
Can the existing ductwork be reused?
Most cooler homes have no ductwork to reuse - just a central discharge grille. Where ducts exist, the engineer evaluates their size, sealing, and insulation against the new system's airflow requirements. Undersized or leaky ducts get replaced or supplemented; the design verifies delivered airflow room by room rather than assuming the old distribution works.
Will I need an electrical panel upgrade?
Often. A house wired around a swamp cooler and gas heat may have a 100-amp service with no spare capacity for a condenser, air handler, and electric backup heat. The engineer runs the service load calculation and specifies the upgrade when the math requires it - the electrical reviewer checks that math, so it is done properly in the permit set.
Where do cooler-conversion permits go in Albuquerque?
Inside city limits, mechanical and electrical permits run through the City of Albuquerque's building safety division. In unincorporated Bernalillo County, the county is the AHJ with its own permit forms and inspection scheduling. Either way the submittal includes the load calculation, equipment selection, and electrical math - the AHJ decides approvals.
