What Does Edmond Require for Stormwater Detention?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Edmond requires engineer-designed detention built before site runoff changes, sized for the 100-year storm with freeboard to the top of the berm. Developments disturbing one acre or more also need the state's OKR10 construction permit with a stormwater pollution prevention plan. The city reviews the drainage design at plan check and inspects controls during construction.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Detention Design: The 100-Year Standard and the Details

Detention design in Edmond is a routing calculation, not a rule of thumb. The engineer models the pre- and post-development hydrographs, sizes the pond volume and outlet structure so post-development peak discharges do not exceed the allowed rates, and checks the 100-year water surface against the top of berm with the required freeboard. Side slopes, low-flow channels, outlet protection, and emergency spillways are detailed on the plans; access for maintenance — typically a wide, limited-grade accessway to the pond bottom — is a plan-check item because a pond the city or owner cannot reach will not be maintained. Easements for storm sewers and detention appear on the plat so the facility's legal footprint survives lot sales. Water quality rides alongside quantity. Edmond's stormwater program requires erosion and sediment controls built to the city's BMP standards: inlet protection, stabilized construction entrances, silt fence and check dams where the plans call for them, and concrete washout management. When the detention pond serves as a sediment basin during construction, outlet controls keep sediment from leaving the site. The operations and maintenance guide prepared for the facility documents who maintains it, what the maintenance is, and how often it happens — because a detention pond is infrastructure with a service life, not a hole that is finished when the grass grows in.

Permits, Plan Check, and Construction Inspection

The permit sequence starts before grading. For one acre or more of disturbance, the developer files the notice of intent with the state environmental quality department, obtains OKR10 coverage and the authorization letter, prepares the site-specific stormwater pollution prevention plan, and secures the city's land disturbance permit where applicable — all before equipment mobilizes. The civil construction plans carry the drainage design: contours, spot elevations, structures, storage basins, impervious areas, BMPs, work limits, and FEMA floodplain delineations, with profiles and details the reviewer can build from. During construction, Edmond's stormwater quality inspectors check that the permitted controls are installed and working, with heightened field presence after rainfall events. Common violations include missing or unmaintained inlet protection, absent flume protection on active sites, and detention used as a sediment basin without outlet controls. Enforcement runs from notices of noncompliance up through summons, so the superintendent's weekly inspections and rain-event responses are not paperwork — they are the compliance record. At project closeout, the as-built detention system must match the permitted design; the AHJ's acceptance, not the contractor's demobilization, is what releases the site.

What else do project teams ask?

When must detention be built on an Edmond development?
Before any change in the site's stormwater runoff. Edmond's stormwater code requires the detention system to be constructed prior to the development altering runoff, so the pond and outlet are functional before impervious cover changes the hydrograph.
What storm does Edmond design detention for?
Detention and retention facilities are designed for the 100-year storm with freeboard to the top of the berm, per the city's engineering design manual. Enclosed storm drains, ditches, channels, and culverts each have their own design frequencies and freeboard criteria in the same manual.
What state permits does an Edmond project need for stormwater?
Disturbances of one acre or more need coverage under the state's OKR10 construction general permit from the Oklahoma Department of Environmental Quality, with a signed site-specific stormwater pollution prevention plan, notice of intent, and authorization letter before work begins — plus the city's land disturbance permit where applicable.
Who maintains a detention pond after construction?
The operations and maintenance guide prepared for the facility assigns responsibility — typically the property owner or owners' association — and documents the maintenance tasks and schedule. Access easements and a maintenance accessway are platted so the responsible party can actually reach the pond.
