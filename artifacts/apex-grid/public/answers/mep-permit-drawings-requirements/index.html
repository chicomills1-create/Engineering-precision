MEP Permit Drawings: What's Required for Plan Approval

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran · Updated 2026-09-15

MEP permit drawings document the proposed mechanical, electrical, and plumbing work for plan review. A complete package may include plans, schedules, risers, single-line diagrams, details, calculations, equipment data, controls, energy forms, code notes, and professional seals where required. The authority having jurisdiction determines the exact checklist and approval decision.

What must MEP permit drawings show?

A reviewer should be able to understand what is being installed, removed, relocated, connected, and controlled. I recommend coordinated plans supported by schedules, diagrams, details, and notes. A generic floor plan or collection of equipment cut sheets rarely establishes capacity, routing, clearances, controls, utility interfaces, and code compliance. Demolition, new work, and phased work should be distinguished clearly.

  • HVAC equipment, ducts, piping, ventilation, controls, and schedules
  • Electrical service, panels, feeders, circuits, lighting, power, and one-lines
  • Plumbing fixtures, piping, risers, vents, cleanouts, and connections
  • Demolition, new work, phasing, legends, abbreviations, and general notes

Which calculations and coordination support MEP drawings?

The drawings must agree with the technical record behind them. Depending on the project, that can include HVAC loads, outside-air calculations, electrical demand, fault-current or voltage-drop information, plumbing sizing, energy reports, equipment schedules, and utility data. I encourage an interdisciplinary check for structure, ceilings, rated assemblies, access, fire protection, civil utilities, and architectural dimensions before submission.

  • Heating and cooling loads, ventilation, airflow, and equipment capacity
  • Electrical demand, feeders, overcurrent protection, and available fault data
  • Water, drainage, vent, gas, fixture-unit, and service calculations
  • Energy forms, equipment data, specifications, and discipline coordination

How does the plan-approval process work?

A technically sound design can still be returned for missing signatures, file conventions, calculations, forms, or local amendments. I ask the team to confirm the AHJ, permit type, adopted codes, utility rules, submission portal, and review checklist. Every correction should be logged, assigned, answered, and incorporated into affected drawings and calculations. Permit-ready means prepared for a defined submission; it never means approval is guaranteed.

  • Identify codes, amendments, permit type, forms, and submission rules
  • Confirm seals, signatures, sheet formats, file names, and supporting documents
  • Log comments by discipline, sheet, responsible party, and resolution
  • Synchronize revisions across plans, schedules, calculations, narratives, and forms

What else do project teams ask?

Do MEP permit drawings always need a PE seal?
Requirements depend on discipline, project, jurisdiction, exemptions, and professional rules. The responsible licensed professionals determine what must be signed or sealed.
Are calculations submitted with MEP drawings?
Often, but not universally. The AHJ and project scope determine which calculations, forms, reports, and supporting documents accompany the drawings.
Can equipment cut sheets replace MEP drawings?
No. Product data does not establish project loads, routing, capacity, utilities, controls, code requirements, clearances, or interdisciplinary coordination.
How should MEP plan-check comments be handled?
Preserve each comment, assign responsibility, revise every affected document, explain the response, and check consistency before resubmittal.
Do complete MEP drawings guarantee plan approval?
No. A coordinated package can reduce avoidable omissions, but the authority controls review, interpretation, corrections, timing, and approval.
