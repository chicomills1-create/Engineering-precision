City of Boulder or Boulder County: Which Permitting Path Applies?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Inside Boulder city limits, projects permit through the city's Planning and Development Services with strict energy code and design review; in unincorporated Boulder County they go through Community Planning and Permitting under the Land Use Code, with Site Plan Review, wildfire mitigation in mapped zones, and county grading standards. Confirm jurisdiction via assessor records before designing.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Two Review Systems Side by Side

The City of Boulder runs a consolidated review: planning, building, and inspection staff work under one department, and the submittal moves through zoning compliance, building plan review, and specialty reviews such as energy and historic preservation. The city's energy code is the headline difference: it is among the strictest local codes in the country, and projects budget for the envelope, mechanical, and documentation work it demands from the start. Design review adds a discretionary layer for visible projects, where boards weigh compatibility and community character alongside code compliance. Boulder County's Community Planning and Permitting runs the parallel universe for unincorporated land. Site Plan Review is the county's signature process for new homes, examining the house in its landscape: setbacks, height, grading, driveway access, and wildfire mitigation. The Land Use Code carries the wildfire provisions that hardened post-Marshall Fire construction, and grading review focuses on the foothill realities of steep slopes and drainage. Timelines, fees, and submittal portals are entirely separate from the city's, and county reviewers bring deep familiarity with mountain and foothill construction that shapes their corrections.

Overlays and Edge Cases: WUI, Septic, and County Islands

Overlays add requirements on top of the base review. In the county's mapped Wildfire Zones, the wildfire mitigation provisions require ignition-resistant construction, ember-resistant vents, Class A roofing, and defensible space, reviewed as part of the building permit. Foothill parcels outside sewer service use on-site wastewater treatment systems, which carry their own county review and soil testing. Floodplain, geologic hazard, and conservation overlays can each add studies and conditions to a project that looked simple on the zoning map. County islands are the classic trap. An unincorporated parcel surrounded by city neighborhoods, often with a Boulder mailing address, is county jurisdiction: county permits, county wildfire rules, county timelines. Annexation flips the parcel to city rules, sometimes mid-project, which is why the jurisdiction question is settled before design begins, not during plan check. When in doubt, the assessor's taxing-jurisdiction record and a call to the respective planning counter give a definitive answer in minutes.

What else do project teams ask?

How do I tell whether a property is in the City of Boulder or unincorporated Boulder County?
Check the Boulder County assessor's parcel record for the taxing jurisdiction, and compare the location against the city's limit maps. A Boulder mailing address does not prove city jurisdiction; many county addresses say Boulder. When the records are ambiguous, call the city or county planning counter before designing.
What is Boulder County Site Plan Review?
It is the county's land-use review for new homes and major additions in unincorporated areas, conducted under the Land Use Code. Reviewers evaluate siting, height, setbacks, grading, access, and wildfire mitigation alongside the building plans. It runs separately from the building permit and must clear before construction.
Does the city or county have stricter energy rules?
The City of Boulder's energy code is among the most demanding local codes in the country, requiring high-performance envelopes and pushing electrification. Boulder County has its own green building standards for unincorporated areas. Both exceed the state baseline, and projects budget for energy compliance from schematic design.
What wildfire rules apply in unincorporated Boulder County?
The Land Use Code's wildfire mitigation provisions apply in the mapped Wildfire Zones, which were extended after the Marshall Fire. They require ignition-resistant construction details, ember-resistant vents, Class A roofing, noncombustible groundcover near the foundation, and defensible space. The county reviews these as part of the building permit.
What is a county island?
A county island is an unincorporated parcel surrounded by incorporated city land. It remains under county jurisdiction, county permits, county land-use rules, and county timelines, until it is annexed into the city. Islands are common around Boulder's edges, and their owners sometimes discover their jurisdiction only when they apply for a permit.
