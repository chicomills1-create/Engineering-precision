What Does a PE Verify Before Sealing MEP Drawings in Fort Lauderdale, FL?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Before sealing MEP drawings in Fort Lauderdale, the PE verifies humidity-driven HVAC loads and equipment, HVHZ wind anchorage for all outdoor equipment, flood elevation compliance, and the full plumbing and electrical distribution.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

The pre-seal verification pass

The PE's verification runs discipline by discipline, with the HVHZ and flood checks woven through. Mechanical starts with the loads: Broward County design conditions, sensible and latent broken out, ventilation per code engineered as a dehumidification load. Equipment gets matched to both capacities, and then every piece of outdoor equipment gets its anchorage verified — curbs, stands, and fasteners against the HVHZ wind pressures for its location, with the details and capacities on the drawings. Elevations get checked against the flood provisions. Plumbing runs from fixture layouts through the risers, with backflow and flood-condition detailing where it applies. Electrical runs from the service calculation through the riser to the panels, with flood-elevated equipment reflected throughout. Energy compliance gets reconciled last, against the finished design. Every discrepancy becomes a marked revision, and the PE re-checks the corrections before sealing.

Anchorage, elevation, and corrosion

Three coastal realities get their own verification track in Fort Lauderdale. Anchorage: the PE confirms the wind anchorage for outdoor MEP equipment is engineered, not assumed — the calculations behind the curb and fastener details get checked the way structural connections get checked, because in the HVHZ they are structural connections. Elevation: mechanical and electrical equipment locations get verified against the design flood elevation, with anything below it either relocated or detailed per the flood provisions. Corrosion: the specifications get checked for coastal-appropriate equipment coatings, fasteners, and supports, because salt air degrades the wrong selections within years. The sealed MEP set describes systems that survive the coast they sit on — anchored, elevated, and specified for the environment — and the PE's verification is what makes that true.

Fort Lauderdale MEP pre-seal checklist

An MEP set is ready for the PE's verification pass — and then for Development Services — when these items are resolved. Coastal hardening is the theme: wind, water, and humidity, all engineered. • HVAC loads with latent broken out and a verified dehumidification strategy • Outdoor equipment anchorage detailed for HVHZ wind pressures with calculated capacities • Mechanical and electrical equipment elevated per the flood provisions • Plumbing risers, backflow provisions, and fixture counts coordinated and code-compliant • Electrical riser, panel schedules, and service calculation in full agreement • Energy compliance documentation reconciled against the sealed design

What else do project teams ask?

How does the HVHZ affect MEP equipment and the PE's review?
In the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone, wind isn't just a structural concern — every piece of outdoor MEP equipment has to stay attached in a major hurricane. The PE verifies that rooftop units, condensers, exhaust fans, and similar equipment carry anchorage details designed for the HVHZ wind pressures at their location and height, checking curbs, stands, straps, and fasteners against the calculated loads rather than accepting catalog defaults. Equipment screens and louvers get the same treatment. The sealed drawings show the anchorage with capacities, because the city's reviewers check MEP wind anchorage the same way they check structural connections. A condenser that becomes a projectile in a hurricane is a life-safety failure, and the review treats it as one.
What does the PE check on humidity control for Fort Lauderdale buildings?
The latent load gets the same priority here as in any Gulf coast market — arguably more, given Fort Lauderdale's density of hospitality and multifamily buildings where moisture problems become mold problems fast. I verify the load calculations broke out sensible and latent separately, check that ventilation air is engineered as the dehumidification load it is, and confirm the selected equipment's latent capacity covers the calculated latent load at both peak and part-load conditions. Dedicated outside air systems, energy recovery, and controls sequences that hold humidity at part-load get verified as a strategy, not just as equipment. The sealed drawings need to show a building that stays dry in August, not just cool.
Can a PE stamp be rushed for a Fort Lauderdale permit deadline?
The honest answer is that the review takes the time it takes — responsible charge doesn't have an express lane, and HVHZ verification in particular can't be hurried past. What can move fast is everything around the review: engaging the PE early, delivering a complete and coordinated package on day one, and responding to the PE's markups within days instead of weeks. I offer expedited scheduling where the review gets priority calendar time, but the review itself stays complete — verified loads, anchorage details, flood elevations, marked revisions, re-checked corrections, then the seal. Anyone promising a same-day seal on an unseen MEP set is selling something Florida's licensing rules prohibit.
What do PE review and seal services cost for a Fort Lauderdale MEP project?
Cost follows scope and complexity, not a flat fee. HVHZ anchorage verification, flood elevation coordination, and humidity-driven mechanical design all add genuine engineering hours beyond a standard commercial review — the fee reflects the work the seal represents. A straightforward TI with a clean package costs far less than a ground-up hospitality or multifamily building. What I'd budget for is the engineering review plus at least one round of responding to the PE's markups; the city review cycles are separate. The expensive version starts late with an incomplete package and a permit deadline. Engage the PE early with complete drawings and the fee stays proportional to the actual engineering.
