Structural PE Stamp for Fulton County, GA Building Permits

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Structural sets for unincorporated Fulton County require Georgia-licensed PE review-and-seal — confirm the site is county territory (not an Atlanta-metro city) first, design to the county's adopted IBC plus Georgia amendments, and submit a fully reviewed, calculation-backed set.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Navigating the metro Atlanta jurisdictional map

Fulton County's unincorporated areas are some of the trickiest jurisdiction in the Southeast to pin down, because the county's territory is fragmented around more than a dozen cities. I treat jurisdiction verification as a formal due-diligence step: assessor records, the county's own jurisdiction maps, and — where the boundary is genuinely ambiguous — a direct confirmation from Planning & Community Services. This is a five-minute check that prevents a month-long re-submittal. The engineering follows the jurisdiction, not the mailing address, and the structural set has to satisfy whichever AHJ actually governs the site.

What our structural review-and-seal covers

Our Georgia-licensed PEs take responsible charge of every Fulton County structural set we seal: complete drawing review against the adopted code and amendments, verification or performance of gravity and lateral calculations, foundation design checked against the geotechnical report, and coordination with architectural and MEP disciplines. Every required revision gets marked and made before the seal goes on. The sealed set that reaches the county reviewer is engineering a licensed professional has reviewed and stands behind — which is exactly what the seal certifies, and exactly what a thorough plan reviewer is looking for.

Fulton County structural submittal checklist

A Fulton County structural permit submittal is ready when these items are resolved. The county's review is detailed, and completeness at intake is the fastest route through it. • Jurisdiction confirmed via Fulton County assessor records — unincorporated county vs. metro city verified • Current adopted IBC edition, Georgia amendments, and county requirements identified • Structural calculations covering gravity, lateral, and foundations, checked against the geotech report • Georgia PE seal, signature, and date on every structural sheet • Architectural and MEP coordination verified so the disciplines agree before county review

What else do project teams ask?

How do I know if my site is in unincorporated Fulton County?
Check the Fulton County Board of Assessors' parcel records — the jurisdiction field shows whether a city claims the parcel. Metro Atlanta's boundaries are genuinely confusing: unincorporated county land wraps around and between cities, and some addresses that read as "Atlanta" are county territory while some that read as county are inside a city. I verify jurisdiction on the assessor's records for every project before engineering starts. Submitting a county-designed set to a city building department (or the reverse) means re-review under a different amendment package, which costs weeks.
Does Fulton County require a Georgia PE seal on structural drawings?
Yes — structural engineering documents submitted for permit in unincorporated Fulton County must be sealed by a Georgia-licensed Professional Engineer in responsible charge of the work. The PE's seal, signature, and date go on the structural sheets, and the seal certifies that the engineer reviewed the design and takes professional responsibility for it. An out-of-state seal does not satisfy the county. Our Georgia-licensed PEs perform the full review — calculations verified, revisions marked — before anything gets sealed.
What Georgia amendments affect structural design in Fulton County?
Georgia adopts the International Building Code with state amendments, and Fulton County layers its own requirements on top for unincorporated areas. The amendments can touch structural provisions — design loads, wind and seismic criteria, foundation and soils requirements — and the county's plan reviewer checks the calculations against the package in force at submittal. I confirm the current adopted edition and every applicable amendment before the first calculation is run, because designing to last year's package and discovering the change at first review is one of the most avoidable delays in the process.
What soil conditions affect Fulton County structural design?
North Georgia soils — including the expansive clays and variable rock depths common in Fulton County — drive foundation design decisions on many projects. Our structural review checks that the foundation system matches the geotechnical reality: the geotechnical report's recommendations have to be reflected in the drawings, not just filed with them. Where no geotechnical investigation exists for a project that needs one, we flag it during review rather than sealing a foundation design on assumptions. The county's reviewer will ask the same question, and "we assumed" is not an answer that survives plan check.
