“Stamp Only” vs. Review-and-Seal: What the Difference Means

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

True "stamp only" — sealing without review — violates responsible-charge rules in every state (TX 137.33, CA B&P 6735, FL 61G15, AZ Board rules); legitimate firms provide review-and-seal instead: full PE review, verified engineering, required revisions, then the seal.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

What the state rules actually require

The responsible-charge requirement is not a suggestion and it is not unique to one state. Texas TBPELS Rule 137.33, California Business & Professions Code 6735, Florida's 61G15 rules, and Arizona's Board of Technical Registration rules all require the sealing PE to have performed or directly supervised the work — and for drawings originated elsewhere, direct supervision means the full review sequence: complete review, independent verification, marked revisions, confirmed corrections, then the seal. Boards enforce this. A PE who seals without review risks reprimand, fines, suspension, or revocation, and the sealed documents can be challenged by any party that relied on them. The rules exist because the seal is a public-protection instrument, not a commercial product.

What legitimate review-and-seal looks like

Our review-and-seal engagements follow the same sequence on every project. We take in the complete drawing set plus every piece of supporting engineering available — calculations, basis of design, geotechnical and survey data, equipment selections. The sealing PE reviews the set against the applicable codes, independently verifies the critical engineering (recalculating where the basis is unclear), and produces a marked revision list. Revisions are made and re-verified. Then — and only then — the PE seals, signs, and dates the set. The client gets sealed drawings ready for permit, backed by engineering a licensed professional evaluated and stands behind. That is what the search term "stamp only" is really asking for, delivered in the only form the rules allow.

Red flags when shopping for a seal

Not every PE practices to the standard the rules require. Screen for these warning signs before you hire — each one indicates a practice that puts your project at risk. • Same-day or instant sealing promised with no mention of reviewing your drawings • No request for your calculations, basis of design, or supporting engineering data • A flat fee quoted with no questions about the project's size, discipline, or complexity • Sealing offered in a state where the PE is not licensed, or in a discipline outside their competence • Reluctance to describe the review process or put the review-and-seal scope in writing

What else do project teams ask?

I just need a stamp on already-finished drawings. Is that possible?
The sealing part is possible — what is not possible, legitimately, is sealing without review. Our PEs seal already-finished drawings every week, but the engagement is review-and-seal: we review the complete set, verify the engineering and calculations, mark required revisions, confirm corrections, and then seal. For a well-prepared set this is efficient — verification and correction cost far less than redesign. What we will not do is apply a seal to drawings no PE has evaluated. That is not a service limitation; it is the responsible-charge rule in every state we practice in, and it protects your project as much as our license.
Why do some engineers advertise "stamp only" services?
Some do not understand the rules; others understand them and choose to break them for the fee. Either way, the client's position is the same: drawings sealed without responsible-charge review carry a certification the board would not defend, and if the design fails — structurally, mechanically, in any discipline — the liability chain runs through a seal that was never backed by engineering. I advise owners to treat "stamp only" advertising as a screening tool in reverse: firms that offer it have told you how they practice. Legitimate firms say "review-and-seal" and can explain exactly what the review covers.
How much more does review-and-seal cost than a plain stamp?
There is no legitimate "plain stamp" price to compare against, because the service does not legitimately exist. Review-and-seal fees reflect the actual verification work: the size and complexity of the set, the number of disciplines, the completeness of the provided calculations, and the revision cycles needed. A clean, well-documented set from a competent designer reviews quickly and seals for a modest fee; a drawings-only set with no engineering basis costs more because the PE must reconstruct the verification. Either way, you are paying for engineering review that protects the project — not for ink on paper.
What should I ask a PE before hiring them to seal my drawings?
Ask four questions. One: are you licensed in the state where the project will be permitted? Two: will you review the complete set and verify the calculations before sealing — and can you describe that review? Three: what do you need from me (calculations, basis of design, geotech) to do it? Four: will required revisions be marked and confirmed before the seal goes on? A legitimate PE answers all four directly and without hesitation. Evasion on any of them — especially the review question — is your signal to keep looking.
