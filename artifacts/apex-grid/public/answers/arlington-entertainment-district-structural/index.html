How Is Arlington's Entertainment District Structurally Engineered?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Arlington's entertainment district buildings are structurally engineered for long-span roofs, high crowd loads, and Texas wind and storm exposure, with deep foundations in the local clay soils, steel-framed seating bowls, and coordinated permitting through the City of Arlington for assembly occupancies.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Retractable Roofs and Long-Span Steel

A retractable stadium roof is a machine as much as a structure. The fixed portion is engineered as a conventional long-span roof - deep trusses or arches spanning hundreds of feet - while the moving panels ride on rails or bogies with their own structural framing. The engineer designs for every roof position: fully closed, fully open, and the transient positions in between, because wind pressures and the structure's own dynamics change as the opening geometry changes. Drive mechanisms, brakes, and locking pins get structural backup so a power failure cannot leave a panel unsecured in a storm. Deflection and vibration govern these designs as much as strength. The roof steel is sized so that under full dead plus live load the deflections stay within limits that keep the moving panels aligned with their tracks - a roof that deflects too much jams its own retraction system. Long-span trusses are typically cambered, fabricated with a slight upward curve so they settle to level under load. Erection engineering is its own discipline: the sequence of lifts, temporary bracing, and the moment the structure becomes self-supporting are all analyzed in advance, because a half-erected long-span truss is far more vulnerable than the finished roof.

Crowd Dynamics, Foundations, and City Review

Seating bowls are checked for human-induced vibration using the dynamic load models the industry has developed from measured stadium events. The engineer tunes the bowl's mass and stiffness so its natural frequencies sit clear of the frequencies of rhythmic crowd activity, and details the connections for the fatigue life of decades of events. Cantilevered upper decks get particular attention: their flexibility makes them lively, and the design balances the fan experience of an intimate, steep bowl against the structural demand of keeping it calm. Railings, vomitories, and stairs are engineered for crowd-crush loads at egress points, where the densest loading occurs. Below, drilled piers carry the concentrated column reactions through the expansive upper clays into stable bearing. The lateral system - braced frames or moment frames in the concourse levels - resolves wind forces from the vast roof and facade areas into those foundations. Through all of this, the City of Arlington is the permitting authority for district venues: structural drawings, geotechnical reports, and special-inspection programs go through the city's review, with the fire marshal and accessibility reviewers running parallel tracks. Arlington's reviewers see assembly occupancies routinely, which streamlines the dialogue but does not guarantee any outcome - the AHJ reviews and decides.

What else do project teams ask?

What makes stadium structural engineering different from ordinary buildings?
Scale and dynamics. Stadium roofs span hundreds of feet without interior columns, seating bowls must absorb the synchronized movement of tens of thousands of fans without alarming vibration, and retractable roofs add moving machinery to the structural problem. Design live loads for assembly occupancies are far higher than for offices or retail, and every connection is checked for decades of repeated event cycles.
How do engineers handle crowd-induced vibration?
By tuning the structure's natural frequencies away from the frequencies of rhythmic crowd movement and by designing connections for fatigue. Engineers use dynamic load models based on measured stadium events, then verify the seating bowl, cantilevers, and stairs against them. The goal is a bowl that feels solid under a full, excited crowd - no perceptible bounce or sway.
Why do Arlington venues need deep foundations?
North Texas clay is expansive and compressible near the surface - a poor bearing material for the enormous concentrated loads of stadium columns and long-span roof supports. Drilled piers bypass the troublesome upper soils and socket into stable strata below, giving each column a reliable, settlement-controlled support.
Who permits construction in the entertainment district?
The City of Arlington's building inspection division, which reviews structural, fire-protection, and accessibility submissions for venues and surrounding development in the district. Assembly occupancies get close scrutiny for egress, structural safety, and crowd-related design. Work outside city limits in unincorporated Tarrant County would instead go through the county's permitting process. The authority having jurisdiction decides all approvals.
