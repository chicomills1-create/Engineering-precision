How Do I Get Structural Drawings PE-Sealed in Gilbert, AZ?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Structural drawings get PE-sealed in Gilbert through a review-and-seal engagement: an Arizona-licensed PE takes responsible charge, verifies the calculations, marks required revisions, re-checks the corrections, and seals the set for the Town of Gilbert's Development Services review.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

The Gilbert review sequence

A review-and-seal engagement in Gilbert runs in a predictable sequence. First, the PE receives the complete package — structural drawings, architectural backgrounds, geotechnical report, and any existing calculations — and confirms nothing material is missing, because reviewing half a set is how errors survive. Second, the engineer works through the design: verifying load paths, checking member sizing and connections, confirming the lateral system suits the building's geometry and the site's seismic and wind demands, and checking the foundation design against the geotechnical recommendations. Third, every required revision gets marked and returned; the design team revises, and the PE reviews the corrections. Only when the design is one the engineer will stand behind professionally does the seal go on. Then the sealed set, calculations, and report go to the Town of Gilbert's Development Services as one complete submittal.

Designing for Gilbert's growth corridors

Gilbert's building stock skews new, and the structural patterns follow the growth. Tilt-up concrete dominates the industrial and flex product along the East Valley corridors, which puts the review emphasis on panel design, panel-to-foundation and panel-to-roof connections, and the diaphragm action of the roof system. Commercial and retail buildings bring long-span framing and storefront lateral systems that need complete load paths, not just strong members. And across all of it, the soils: expansive clay that rewards stiffened slabs, careful pier design where deep foundations are used, and moisture detailing that anticipates both irrigation and monsoon runoff. The PE's review is calibrated to these patterns — I spend my time where Gilbert buildings actually fail, which is foundations and connections, not textbook beam sizing.

Gilbert review-and-seal checklist

A Gilbert project is ready to start the review-and-seal engagement when these items are in hand. The theme is completeness before the review, because the review certifies the design, and the design needs its full context. • Arizona-licensed PE engaged during design, not at permit intake • Complete structural set with architectural backgrounds the framing was designed against • Geotechnical report with boring logs and site-specific foundation recommendations • Existing calculations and the project narrative: occupancy, construction type, site constraints • One correction round budgeted into the permit schedule after sealing • Sealed set submitted complete to Development Services — no piecemeal uploads

What else do project teams ask?

How long does review-and-seal take for a Gilbert project?
The engineering review itself typically takes one to three weeks, driven by project size and how complete the starting drawings are — a clean structural set with the geotechnical report in hand moves fast, while a set with an unresolved lateral scheme or missing backgrounds takes longer because the PE has to send it back for coordination first. After sealing, the Town of Gilbert's Development Services review adds its own cycles, so I budget at least one correction round on top of the town's published review times. Engaging the PE during design rather than at intake is the single best schedule protection, because the expensive problems get found while they're still cheap to fix.
What should I send the PE to start a Gilbert review-and-seal?
Send the complete structural drawing set — framing plans, foundation plans, sections, and details — plus the architectural backgrounds the structure was designed against, the geotechnical report with foundation recommendations, and any calculations already prepared. If the project is an addition or a remodel, include the as-built or field-verified dimensions of the existing structure. I also want the project narrative: occupancy, construction type, and anything unusual about the site. A review can only be as good as its inputs, and Arizona's responsible-charge standard means gaps get filled before the seal goes on, not after — so complete inputs up front are what make the one-to-three-week window realistic.
Does Gilbert require a geotechnical report for foundation design?
For most new construction, effectively yes — and even where a report isn't an explicit intake checkbox, no responsible PE will seal a foundation design on assumed soil parameters in the East Valley. Gilbert's expansive soils make the geotechnical report the foundation of the foundation design: boring logs, plasticity data, and the engineer's site-specific recommendations for slab design, pier depths, or whatever system the site calls for. I check the structural foundation design against those recommendations line by line. If your project doesn't have a geotechnical report yet, getting one drilled is step one of the review, not an optional extra.
Why can't a PE just seal my finished drawings the same day?
Because the seal is a professional statement of responsibility, not a notary stamp. Under Arizona's Board of Technical Registration framework, a PE may only seal work they performed or directly supervised — and responsible charge means the engineer actually reviewed the design, verified the calculations, and marked the revisions. Same-day sealing of an unseen set would mean certifying engineering the PE never checked, which is exactly what the rule prohibits. Any engineer willing to seal your drawings without reading them is telling you everything you need to know about the value of that seal. The honest version takes one to three weeks because the review is real.
