How Are Baltimore Formstone Facades Restored?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Baltimore Formstone facades are restored by sounding the stucco skin for delamination, removing loose or hollow sections, repairing the brick substrate and repointing as needed, then patching or reapplying the Formstone finish to match the original pattern - with moisture sources corrected so the repair lasts.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

What Formstone Is and Why It Fails

Formstone was sold as a maintenance-free stone look for Baltimore's brick rowhouses, and thousands of facades still wear it. The system is a thick cement-based coating, reinforced with metal lath, applied directly over the brick and finished to resemble cut stone blocks. When it was applied to sound brick with proper preparation, it can last generations. When it was applied over soft mortar, painted brick, or walls already carrying moisture, the bond was compromised from day one. Failure almost always traces to water. The coating itself is relatively impermeable, which means any water that gets behind it - through a cracked parapet cap, an open window head joint, or rising damp at the base - stays there. Winter freeze-thaw expands that trapped water and shears the coating off the wall, sometimes in dramatic sheets. Corroding lath, differential movement between the stiff coating and the flexible brick wall, and impact damage complete the picture. The engineering survey separates cosmetic weathering, which needs only maintenance, from debonding and substrate decay, which need intervention.

Repair in Kind Versus Full Removal

Repair in kind keeps every square foot of sound Formstone and replaces only what has failed. The engineer specifies the removal limits from the sounding map, the substrate repair, the bonding agent and mix for the patch, and the tooling pattern that matches the original stone courses. Done well, the patches weather in and the facade keeps its historic character. This is the right answer for most facades, and it is the approach preservation reviewers expect to see. Full removal is the bigger undertaking and is specified for a reason, not a preference. Once the coating is off, the brick underneath is fully exposed: some of it will need repointing, some units will need replacement, and the entire wall will need time to dry before it is recoated or left as exposed brick. Removal also changes the wall's moisture behavior for the better - brick that can dry to the exterior lasts longer than brick sealed behind stucco. The engineer details the transition at windows, doors, and cornices either way, because those are the joints where water gets in and where the next failure will start.

What else do project teams ask?

What is Formstone actually made of?
Formstone is a cementitious stucco coating, reinforced with metal lath, troweled over brick and tooled and painted to look like stone masonry. It was patented in Baltimore in the 1930s and applied to thousands of rowhouses. It is not structural - it is a cladding - but at roughly an inch thick it adds meaningful weight, and its bond to the brick determines whether it lasts or delaminates.
Can Formstone be patched, or does it all have to come off?
It can usually be patched. The engineer sounds the facade to map exactly which areas are debonded, and only those areas are removed and redone in kind to match the original pattern. Full removal is reserved for facades where the coating is extensively failed, where it hides serious brick deterioration, or where the owner is deliberately returning the building to exposed brick.
Will removing Formstone damage the brick underneath?
Removal is done carefully, but the brick underneath is often not pristine - that is frequently why the Formstone was applied in the first place. Expect to repoint joints and replace spalled units once the wall is exposed. The engineer assesses the brick as part of the survey so the restoration scope, and its cost, reflects the real condition of the wall rather than an optimistic guess.
Do I need city approval to restore a Formstone facade in Baltimore?
Facade restoration needs a Baltimore City building permit with the scope of work documented. In the city's designated historic districts, the Commission for Historical and Architectural Preservation reviews exterior changes to street-facing facades, and its standards favor repair in kind over removal. The authority having jurisdiction decides approvals, so the engineer confirms whether preservation review applies before the scope is set.
