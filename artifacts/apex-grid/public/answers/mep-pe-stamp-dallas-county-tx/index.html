MEP PE Stamp in Dallas County, TX: County Permitting and North Texas Design

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

An MEP PE stamp in Dallas County means a Texas PE has taken responsible charge under TBPELS Rule 137.33 — verifying extreme-heat HVAC, storm anchorage, freeze protection, and energy-code consistency — with county-level functions through Dallas County Public Works and city projects through their cities.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Mechanical review for North Texas extremes

Our Dallas County mechanical review starts with load calculations built for the region's reality: sustained extreme-heat cooling loads, genuine heating loads for winter cold snaps, and the shoulder-season swings in between. Equipment selection is verified against those loads with documented margins, and we follow the chain through distribution sizing and control sequences. Storm exposure gets dedicated review: rooftop equipment anchorage for severe winds, hail considerations for exposed equipment, and structural coordination for every rooftop unit. Freeze protection — piping, coils, outdoor equipment — is verified with the same rigor as the cooling design. Ventilation follows the adopted mechanical code. The Texas PE seals when the mechanical system is engineered for everything North Texas weather does, verified link by link.

Electrical, plumbing, and coordination

Electrical review verifies service and distribution sizing for the cooling-dominated load, panel schedules matched to the mechanical equipment's actual characteristics, lighting within energy-code allowances, surge and service-entrance provisions for a lightning-active region, and life-safety interfaces. Plumbing review covers fixture counts, water and sanitary sizing, backflow prevention, and freeze protection for exposed piping. The coordination pass reconciles the MEP set with the architectural and structural drawings — equipment locations, risers, working clearances, structural penetrations — before the seal. Under TBPELS Rule 137.33, the sealing engineer verifies all of it; the firm name and registration number on the sealed documents stand behind that verification.

Dallas County MEP submittal checklist

A Dallas County MEP package is ready when the AHJ is confirmed and North Texas conditions are engineered. • AHJ confirmed from parcel records: county Public Works functions vs. the incorporated city • Cooling and heating loads calculated for North Texas extremes, traceable to equipment • Rooftop equipment anchorage verified for severe-wind exposure; hail considered • Freeze protection designed for exposed piping and outdoor equipment • Energy-code compliance forms consistent with final drawings and schedules • Electrical and plumbing coordinated with architectural and structural sets • Sealed documents show firm name and Texas firm registration number per TBPELS requirements

What else do project teams ask?

Who reviews MEP drawings for a Dallas County project?
It depends on the parcel's jurisdiction: Dallas County Public Works handles county-level functions for unincorporated areas, while projects inside Dallas or the county's other cities permit through those cities' building departments. Confirm the AHJ from county parcel records before design — the engineering review we perform is identical, but the submittal portal, checklists, and reviewers follow the jurisdiction. Getting the AHJ right at kickoff prevents the most basic and most wasteful permitting error.
What does TBPELS Rule 137.33 require for MEP sealing?
Rule 137.33 requires the sealing Texas PE to have exercised responsible charge over the MEP engineering — genuine performance, direction, or thorough review of the design, with calculations verified and engineering decisions controlled by the sealing engineer. Firm-sealed documents must show the firm's name and Texas firm registration number. Our process documents responsible charge for each MEP discipline: the review performed, the calculations verified, the revisions required and confirmed. The seal is the certification that this oversight was real.
How do North Texas storms affect MEP design review?
Spring severe weather — high winds, hail, and the region's tornado exposure — makes equipment anchorage and protection a design issue our review treats seriously. We verify rooftop equipment curbs, fastening, and structural coordination for wind forces; check hail-resistant selections where exposure warrants; and confirm that outdoor equipment and distribution can survive the storm season. Electrical systems get surge and service-entrance review appropriate to a lightning-active region. Reviewers notice whether the MEP drawings acknowledge North Texas weather — our sealed set shows that they do.
Does the MEP design need freeze protection in Dallas County?
Yes. North Texas cold snaps are brief but severe enough to burst unprotected piping and damage equipment. Our review verifies freeze protection for piping in unconditioned and exposed locations, outdoor equipment provisions, coil freeze protection, and heating capacity against design winter conditions. Plumbing reviewers check these provisions because freeze failures are among the most common winter insurance claims in the region. The sealed MEP design handles August heat and February ice — both verified before the Texas PE seals.
