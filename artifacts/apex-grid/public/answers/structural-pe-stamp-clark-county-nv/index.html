Structural PE Stamp for Clark County, NV Building Permits

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Structural drawings for Clark County, NV permits require review-and-seal by a Nevada-licensed PE — verify the site is unincorporated county (not a valley city) through the assessor first, design to Clark County's IBC amendments including wind, soils, and seismic requirements, and submit through the county's electronic portal.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Confirming county vs. city jurisdiction

Before any structural engineering begins on a Clark County project, I verify the actual AHJ through the county assessor's parcel records — not the mailing address, not the client's assumption. The Las Vegas Valley is a patchwork: unincorporated county land sits directly adjacent to the cities of Las Vegas, North Las Vegas, and Henderson, and the boundary lines cut through commercial corridors in ways that are invisible from the street. Each jurisdiction enforces its own amendment package and runs its own electronic portal. Getting this wrong means a full re-submittal under a different code package, which is a month or more of lost schedule.

What our structural review-and-seal covers

Our Nevada-licensed PEs treat every Clark County structural set the same way: we take responsible charge, review the complete drawing set against the county's adopted code and amendments, verify or perform the gravity and lateral calculations, and mark every revision the design needs before the seal goes on. That includes checking wind and seismic load paths, foundation design against valley soil conditions, and coordination with the architectural and MEP sets so the structure the county approves is the structure that can actually be built. The sealed set that goes to the county is one a reviewer can approve on its technical merits — not a formality, but engineering a PE stands behind.

Clark County structural submittal checklist

A Clark County structural permit submittal is ready when these items are resolved. The county's reviewers are thorough, and a complete first submittal is the fastest path through. • Jurisdiction confirmed via Clark County assessor records — county vs. valley city verified • Current adopted IBC edition and Clark County amendments identified and designed to • Structural calculations covering gravity, lateral (wind and seismic), and foundations • Nevada PE seal, signature, and date on every structural sheet, portal-ready digital format • Coordination with architectural and MEP sets checked so disciplines agree before review

What else do project teams ask?

Is the Las Vegas Strip in Clark County or the City of Las Vegas?
Most of the Strip is in unincorporated Clark County, which is why the Clark County Building Department permits it — not the City of Las Vegas. This is the single most common jurisdictional confusion in the valley, and submitting to the wrong department costs real schedule: each AHJ has its own portal, fee schedule, and amendment package, and neither one will forward your submittal to the other. I confirm jurisdiction through the Clark County assessor's parcel records for every project before design begins, because a mailing address that says "Las Vegas" proves nothing about which building department governs the site.
Does a Nevada PE have to seal structural drawings for Clark County?
Structural drawings for permit in Clark County must bear the seal of a Nevada-licensed Professional Engineer who takes responsible charge of the design — meaning the PE (or engineers under their direct supervision) performed or verified the engineering, reviewed the complete set, and marked required revisions before sealing. An out-of-state PE's seal does not satisfy the county. Our Nevada-licensed engineers handle the full review-and-seal: we check the calculations against Clark County's adopted code and amendments, coordinate the structural set with architectural and MEP disciplines, and deliver a sealed set the county's reviewers can approve.
What local amendments does Clark County add to the building code?
Clark County adopts the International Building Code with county amendments that touch structural provisions — including requirements shaped by valley conditions like high winds, expansive soils, and seismic design categories. The county publishes its amendments through the Building Department, and I confirm the current adopted edition and amendment package at the start of every project rather than relying on what applied last year. The structural calculations have to reflect the amendments that are in force on the submittal date, because the plan reviewer will check them against the current package, not the one you designed to six months ago.
Can structural review and permitting run through Clark County's online portal?
Yes — the Clark County Building Department runs electronic plan review, and digital submittal is the standard path. Digitally sealed PDFs from a Nevada PE are accepted through the portal as long as the digital seal carries a verifiable signature tied to the licensee. I prepare the sealed structural set as a clean, portal-ready PDF with the PE seal, signature, and date on each sheet, and I confirm the county's current digital-seal requirements before uploading — portal requirements evolve, and a submittal rejected at intake for a formatting issue is the most avoidable delay in the process.
