What Engineering Does a Jewish Community Center Require?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

JCC engineering covers mixed-occupancy code compliance, natatorium structure and dehumidification, long-span gym structure, zoned HVAC for simultaneous diverse uses, commercial kitchens, and security-driven entry and site design.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Pools, gyms, and long spans: the structural core

The JCC's big rooms drive the structural design. Natatoriums need long clear spans over the pool, corrosion-resistant structure — stainless or coated steel, careful concrete detailing — and roof systems that tolerate a warm humid environment for decades. Gymnasiums want 80-foot-plus clear spans, floors engineered for sports surfacing, and wall systems that take ball impacts. Between them sit classrooms and offices on conventional framing. The lateral system threads through it all, and foundations account for the pool excavation and its surcharge on adjacent structure. Vibration matters too: fitness floors with free weights and group-exercise studios need isolation so the preschool below doesn't feel every dropped dumbbell.

Air, water, and energy: the MEP challenge

MEP is where JCCs succeed or fail. The pool hall gets a dedicated dehumidification unit with heat recovery — often the single most expensive piece of equipment in the building — plus stainless ductwork and pool-water heating integrated with the building's hot water plant. Fitness areas need high ventilation and cooling for internal heat gains; the preschool needs gentle, quiet, highly filtered air; event halls need the assembly-occupancy package. Kitchens range from warming kitchens to full commercial depending on catering and camp programs. Electrical must cover it all with emergency power for life safety, and plumbing fixture counts follow the peak simultaneous occupancy across every use. Controls tie it together with scheduling that matches the real operating calendar.

Designing for community life and security

A JCC is a second home for its members — engineer it like one:

  • Welcoming but secure entry sequence: vestibule, check-in, and controlled access to program areas
  • Early childhood wing with its own secure entry, outdoor play, and separation from public areas
  • Senior-friendly details: handrails, seating, gentle lighting, accessible everything
  • Multipurpose event hall with operable partitions, commercial kitchen access, and holiday capacity
  • Site design for constant turnover: drop-offs, stroller parking, and safe pedestrian routes

What else do project teams ask?

What is the hardest system in a JCC?
The natatorium, by a wide margin. Indoor pools demand dedicated dehumidification, corrosion-resistant structure and ductwork, precise air and water temperatures, and chemistry control. Get it wrong and the building literally rots from the inside — I never value-engineer the pool systems.
How do mixed occupancies affect the design?
Assembly, business, educational, and sometimes residential occupancies each bring their own egress, fire protection, and plumbing requirements. Our licensed engineers map every space to its occupancy early and design separations, exits, and systems accordingly.
What security features should be engineered in?
Controlled single points of entry, vestibule interlocks, camera and access-control infrastructure, and site design with vehicle barriers at drop-offs. I coordinate these with the community's security consultant during schematic design, not after.
Can a JCC be built in phases?
Yes — aquatics and fitness first, with classroom and event wings later, is common. The trick is sizing utilities, structure, and the site plan for the full build-out so phase two doesn't require tearing up phase one.
