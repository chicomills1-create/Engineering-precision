What Does a PE Verify Before Sealing MEP Drawings in Anaheim, CA?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Before sealing MEP drawings in Anaheim, the PE verifies HVAC loads and equipment, hospitality and kitchen systems, plumbing risers, and the full electrical distribution, then reconciles Title 24 energy compliance with the sealed design.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

The pre-seal verification pass

The PE's verification runs discipline by discipline, tuned to Anaheim's hospitality-heavy mix. Mechanical starts with the load calculations: Orange County design conditions, realistic occupancy densities for dining and assembly — not the optimistic ones — and ventilation per code with the outside-air load fully accounted for. Kitchen systems get their own pass: exhaust rates, makeup air balance, grease duct construction and clearances, and the HVAC impact of the whole cooking operation. Plumbing runs from fixture layouts through the risers — counts per occupancy, hot water sized for real demand, grease waste detailed to code. Electrical runs from the service calculation through the riser to the panels: loads, protection, and agreement at every level. Title 24 gets reconciled last, against the finished design. Every discrepancy becomes a marked revision, and the PE re-checks the corrections before sealing.

Energy and the resort district

Anaheim's hospitality buildings are energy-intensive by nature — commercial kitchens, high ventilation rates, extensive hot water, and lighting that runs long hours — which makes Title 24 compliance both harder and more valuable here than in a typical office. The PE's review looks for the compliance strategy in the actual design: heat recovery where the code and the operation justify it, lighting controls that match how the spaces really get used, and mechanical selections whose efficiency the compliance model can honestly claim. I check that the model's inputs match the sealed equipment and controls, because a hospitality building's Title 24 story lives or dies on whether the paperwork describes the real systems. When the design and the documentation agree, plan check moves on — and the owner gets a building whose energy performance was designed in, not wished for.

Anaheim MEP pre-seal checklist

An MEP set is ready for the PE's verification pass — and then for the city — when these items are resolved. Hospitality systems and Title 24 agreement are the themes. • HVAC loads with realistic occupancy densities and ventilation fully accounted for • Kitchen exhaust, makeup air, and grease waste designed and checked as a system • Plumbing risers, hot water sizing, and fixture counts matched to the real operation • Electrical service, riser, and panel schedules in full agreement on loads and protection • Title 24 compliance documentation reconciled against the actual sealed design • MEP layouts coordinated with structural framing and architectural backgrounds

What else do project teams ask?

What does the PE check on restaurant and hospitality MEP in Anaheim?
Hospitality MEP gets a review tuned to operational intensity. I verify the kitchen exhaust rates and makeup air balance — the exhaust drives the makeup air, which drives the HVAC loads, so these get checked as a system, not as separate sheets. Grease waste and interceptors get detailed per code, gas distribution gets sized for the actual equipment list, and domestic hot water gets checked against realistic hotel or restaurant demand rather than a template. Electrical review covers the service calculation against the building's real connected loads — kitchens and HVAC are heavy — with panel schedules in agreement with the riser. High-occupancy ventilation for dining and assembly spaces gets verified per code with the outside-air load fully in the math.
How does Title 24 verification work before the seal?
The PE checks that the Title 24 compliance documentation describes the building on the sealed drawings: envelope inputs consistent with the architectural set, lighting power densities and controls matching the lighting plans, and mechanical equipment efficiencies and sequences matching the schedules and specs. Where the model and the drawings disagree, the drawings win and the model gets corrected — the seal certifies the design, not the form. In Anaheim's cooling-dominated climate, the mechanical side carries enormous weight: equipment efficiency, economizer logic, and controls sequences are where real performance lives. I reconcile all of it before the stamp goes on, because the city's plan check reads Title 24 closely and contradictions cycle.
What are the most common MEP corrections on Anaheim projects?
Coordination failures lead the list: equipment schedules that don't match the mechanical plans, panel schedules whose loads don't agree with the electrical riser, and plumbing risers that don't account for every fixture on the floor plans. Title 24 contradictions are next — compliance forms describing an earlier design iteration than the sealed drawings. On hospitality work, kitchen exhaust and makeup air imbalances show up regularly, along with undersized hot water systems. The PE's pre-seal verification pass is designed to find exactly these, reading the set the way a plan reviewer reads it, so the markups happen on the engineer's desk instead of costing a city review cycle.
How does MEP permit comment resolution work with a PE seal?
When the city's reviewers issue corrections on a sealed MEP set, the responses come back through the PE — that's what the seal means. I review each comment, determine whether it needs a design revision, a calculation clarification, or just a clouded pointer to where the information already lives on the sheets, and the resubmittal carries the PE's professional responsibility for the answers. Comments that reveal a real design issue get fixed properly, not argued away. This is one of the quiet values of a genuine review-and-seal: because the PE actually reviewed the design before sealing, the comment responses are fast and authoritative instead of a second round of discovery.
