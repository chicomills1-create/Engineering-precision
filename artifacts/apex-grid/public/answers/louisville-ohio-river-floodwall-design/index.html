How Is the Louisville Ohio River Floodwall System Designed?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

The Louisville Ohio River floodwall system is designed as a levee-and-wall line sized to a target protection level, with concrete floodwalls, closure structures at openings, pump stations for interior drainage, and a maintenance regime overseen with the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

The Line of Protection: Walls, Levees, and Closures

Designing the line starts with the protection elevation: the river stage the system must hold, plus freeboard for waves, uncertainty, and settlement. The engineer runs hydraulic modeling of the design flood against the proposed alignment, checking that the line ties into high ground at both ends with no low gaps. Concrete wall sections are typically inverted-T cantilevers founded below the scour and seepage zone, with waterstops at every joint because a floodwall is a dam and dams leak at their joints first. Where the alignment crosses streets, railroads, and pedestrian paths, closure structures - steel gates, removable stoplog walls, or engineered sandbag closures - seal the openings, each designed for the full hydrostatic load and detailed so a trained crew can close it in hours. Levee reaches get the same rigor in earthwork form. The embankment is built from specified, compacted fill in controlled lifts, with an impervious core or cutoff trench to block seepage and seepage berms on the landward side where the foundation soils are permeable. Riverside slopes are armored against the current and against the rapid drawdown that follows a flood peak - when the river drops fast, saturated levee soils can slough without that protection. Every penetration through the line, from a storm drain to a fiber conduit, is detailed with antiseep collars and gates, because unsealed penetrations are the classic failure point of flood protection systems.

Pump Stations and Interior Drainage

Interior drainage design answers a simple question: when the river is at the top of the wall and the gravity outlets are underwater, where does the city's water go? The engineer sizes pump stations for the coincident event - the design rainfall falling on the protected watershed at the same time the river peaks - using hydrologic modeling of every sub-basin behind the line. Stations get multiple pumps so the loss of one unit does not lose the station, backup power independent of the grid, and intake structures designed to keep running on debris-laden water. Forebay storage - ponds or underground storage that buffer inflow - lets the pumps be sized for sustained flow rather than the instantaneous peak. Operating the system is engineered as carefully as building it. Pump start elevations, gate closure triggers tied to river forecasts, and crew callout sequences are written into an operations manual that the local sponsor drills against. The Corps inspects the system on a recurring cycle, rating levees, walls, closures, and pump stations; deficiencies must be corrected to maintain the system's standing and its treatment on flood maps. For the engineer, this means designing not just for the flood but for the inspection: access to every gate, clearance to exercise every pump, and instrumentation that proves the system is ready between floods.

What else do project teams ask?

What is the difference between a floodwall and a levee?
A floodwall is a concrete or steel structure - essentially a dam wall - used where space is tight, such as along Louisville's riverfront. A levee is an engineered earthen embankment used where there is room for its wide footprint. Both serve the same purpose in a line of protection, and Louisville's system uses each where it fits, with engineered transitions between them.
Why does a floodwall system need pump stations?
Because the wall that keeps the river out also keeps the city's own water in. During a flood, gravity storm outlets are submerged and useless, so rainfall, groundwater, and sanitary flows inside the protected area must be pumped over the line. Without pump stations sized for the design storm, the protected side would flood from the inside while the wall holds the river back.
What are closure structures?
Engineered gates or barriers that seal the openings where streets, railroads, and paths cross the flood protection line. They range from steel swing gates to stoplog walls assembled from stored beams to sandbag closures, each designed for the full flood load and deployable by trained crews on a river-forecast trigger. Their storage, maintenance, and deployment plans are part of the system's design.
Who approves work near the Louisville floodwall?
It is a layered jurisdiction. The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers governs the flood protection system itself - any modification, crossing, or nearby construction affecting the line goes through federal review. The city or county is the authority having jurisdiction for building permits on adjacent land, and the local floodplain administrator enforces the floodplain ordinance. Engineers coordinate all three from the start, because federal review timelines are long and no local permit overrides them.
