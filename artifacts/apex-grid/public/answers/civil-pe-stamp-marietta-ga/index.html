Civil PE Stamp in Marietta, GA: Site Plans, Grading, and Drainage Review

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

A civil PE stamp in Marietta means a licensed Georgia PE has taken responsible charge of grading, drainage, stormwater, and the site plan — verifying the calculations and coordination — before sealing documents reviewed by Marietta's Development Services department.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

The drainage design reviewers expect

Marietta's reviewers read a stormwater design like an auditor: rainfall inputs, runoff coefficients appropriate to Georgia clay soils, time-of-concentration calculations, then pipe, inlet, and detention sizing that follows from those inputs. Our responsible-charge review verifies every link. We check that pre- and post-development runoff are compared correctly, that detention actually controls the required storm events, and that water-quality treatment is designed rather than assumed. The outfall gets attention too — where the water goes after it leaves the site must be legal and physically workable. A drainage design that is internally consistent and tied to the grading plan passes; one with numbers that don't trace to the drawings does not.

Grading, erosion control, and site coordination

The grading plan is where civil design meets the real site. We verify that contours and spot elevations create positive drainage away from every structure, that slopes are buildable and stable, and that retaining walls appear wherever grades demand them — with structural coordination for walls over the height that triggers engineered design. Erosion and sediment control is designed for the construction sequence: silt fence, inlet protection, and stabilized construction entrances that match how the site will actually be built, phased with the grading. Then we coordinate outward: finished-floor elevations against the architectural plans, utility taps and fire lines against the MEP drawings, and accessible parking and routes against the site layout. The civil set has to agree with every other discipline, and our review confirms it before sealing.

Marietta civil submittal checklist

A Marietta civil package is ready for Development Services when these are verified. Civil review rewards completeness more than any other discipline. • Site jurisdiction confirmed as City of Marietta (not Cobb County) via parcel records • Hydrology and hydraulic calculations traceable from rainfall inputs to detention sizing • Grading plan showing positive drainage, buildable slopes, and coordinated finished floors • Erosion and sediment control designed for the actual construction sequence • Floodplain and stream-buffer status checked against FEMA mapping and local rules • Utility connections, driveways, and right-of-way details per city expectations • Civil set coordinated with architectural, structural, and MEP drawings before the PE seals

What else do project teams ask?

What civil work requires a PE stamp in Marietta?
Marietta's Development Services expects sealed civil engineering for site plans involving grading, drainage, stormwater management, and utility design on commercial, multifamily, and substantive residential projects. The seal covers the engineering judgment in the design: hydrology and hydraulic calculations, grading that controls runoff, erosion and sediment control, and utility layouts. Even where the city might accept unsealed plans for very minor work, lenders and owners typically require a sealed civil set. We treat any site work that changes drainage patterns or grades as engineering under responsible charge from the start.
What do Marietta reviewers scrutinize on civil site plans?
Stormwater management first: reviewers check that detention, water-quality treatment, and conveyance are designed to the city's requirements and that the calculations support the structures shown. Grading gets close attention — positive drainage away from buildings, no adverse impact on adjacent properties, and retaining walls where slopes demand them. They also verify erosion-control plans for the construction phase, utility connections, driveway and right-of-way details, and consistency between the civil set and the architectural plans. Our pre-seal review walks this same path so the city's comments find nothing new.
How does Georgia's clay soil affect Marietta civil design?
Georgia's red clay has low permeability, which means rainfall runs off rather than soaking in — so detention volumes are larger and water-quality design matters more than on sandy soils. It also means grading and compaction need care: clay that is poorly compacted settles, and clay that dries shrinks. Our review checks that the stormwater design accounts for the soil's runoff characteristics, that grading plans address compaction and slope stability, and that foundation drainage is coordinated with the structural design. The geotechnical report's recommendations for earthwork get incorporated into the civil plans, not filed separately.
Do Marietta civil permits involve floodplain review?
They can. Portions of Marietta fall within FEMA-mapped floodplains, and stream buffers add local constraints on top of the federal mapping. We check the floodplain status of every site during our review, before the seal goes on — if any work touches the floodplain, the design needs elevation certificates, flood-resistant construction details, and sometimes a floodplain development permit running alongside the building permit. Discovering floodplain involvement at intake can force grading and finished-floor redesign, which is why the check happens in the engineering review phase, not after submittal.
