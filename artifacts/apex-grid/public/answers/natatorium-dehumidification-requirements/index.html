What Are the Dehumidification Requirements for a Natatorium?

ASHRAE Standard 62.1 establishes the ventilation baseline for pool halls, but the requirement that actually sizes the equipment is the pool-water evaporation load. A competition natatorium must hold relative humidity in the 50–60% range while the water surface continuously releases moisture into the air — and the dehumidification system has to win that fight every hour the building is occupied.

The concise answer

Design the dehumidification plant to the evaporation load (a function of water temperature, air temperature, humidity differential, and surface agitation), ventilate to ASHRAE 62.1 minimums, hold the hall at 50–60% RH with air 2–4°F warmer than the water, and detail the envelope — vapor retarders, thermal breaks, corrosion-resistant structure — so moisture can't condense inside the walls. Size for the worst realistic case: a warm therapy or leisure pool with play features evaporates far more than a calm competition pool.

How the load is calculated

Pool evaporation follows the mass-transfer relationship in the ASHRAE Handbook: the rate climbs with the vapor-pressure difference between the water surface and the room air, multiplied by an activity factor. Still water (a covered or calm lap pool) gets the lowest factor; a wave pool or splash-feature leisure pool gets a multiple several times higher. Add occupant latent load, outdoor-air latent load on a humid day, and envelope transmission, and you have the total moisture the system must remove — typically hundreds of pounds of water per hour for a competition facility.

Equipment and energy strategy

Most modern natatoriums use dedicated outdoor-air/dehumidification units with refrigeration-based moisture removal; large facilities may add desiccant wheels for deep dehumidification. The key energy move is heat recovery: the dehumidification process rejects heat that can preheat pool water and domestic hot water, turning the building's biggest load into its hot-water plant. Controls must prevent night-setback humidity drift — letting RH climb overnight to "save energy" is how buildings grow mold inside their walls.

Frequently asked questions

What relative humidity should a natatorium maintain?
50–60% RH is the standard target — low enough to protect the building and keep swimmers comfortable, high enough to limit evaporation and energy waste. Air temperature is typically held 2–4°F above water temperature.
Does ASHRAE 62.1 dictate natatorium dehumidification capacity?
62.1 sets ventilation minimums, but dehumidification capacity is driven by the evaporation load calculation. Ventilation alone can't hold 50–60% RH in most climates — mechanical dehumidification is required.
Why do pool buildings corrode?
Chronic humidity above 60% condenses on cool surfaces inside the envelope, carrying chloramines that attack steel, aluminum, and even concrete reinforcement. Prevention is dehumidification plus a vapor-managed, corrosion-resistant envelope — not paint.
Can heat recovery really heat the pool?
Yes. A refrigeration-based dehumidification unit rejects roughly as much heat as the moisture energy it removes; routed through a heat exchanger, that energy preheats pool water and domestic hot water, often covering a large share of the heating load.
