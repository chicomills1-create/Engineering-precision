How Are Charleston WV Buildings Designed for Flood Resilience?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Charleston WV buildings are designed for flood resilience by elevating occupied floors above the base flood elevation, using wet or dry floodproofing for below-grade areas, specifying flood-resistant materials, and anchoring mechanical and electrical systems so recovery after a flood is fast.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Elevation and Flood-Resistant Foundations

Elevating a building is a structural project, not just a taller foundation. The engineer designs the piers, piles, or stem walls for the combined gravity loads of the building plus flood forces: buoyancy trying to lift it, lateral water pressure pushing it, debris impact striking it, and scour undermining it. Connections from the foundation to the floor framing are detailed for uplift, because a house that floats off its piers is a total loss. Enclosed areas below the elevated floor get flood vents sized to the ordinance so water equalizes instead of collapsing the walls, and everything below the design elevation is built from flood-resistant materials. Freeboard - building above the minimum - is where the engineer earns the fee many times over. Each foot above the base flood elevation cuts expected damage and insurance premiums sharply, and on Charleston's valley floors the extra height often costs little during construction. The design flood elevation is set from the maps plus required and owner-elected freeboard, and the surveyor's elevation certificate proves it after construction. Stairs, decks, and attached garages are detailed to break away or vent as the design requires, so accessory elements never endanger the main structure.

Dry Floodproofing and Hardened Systems

Dry floodproofing turns the building itself into the barrier. The engineer designs walls for the hydrostatic pressure of water standing against them to the design depth - a load that grows fast and punishes any weak point - plus the hydrodynamic force of moving water and the impact of debris. Every opening below the design elevation gets a rated closure: flood shields, gates, or sealed doors, each detailed and specified with its installation hardware stored on site. Backflow preventers keep the sewer from becoming a fountain inside the building, and the engineer certifies the assembly's watertightness because the floodplain administrator and the insurer both require that certification. Hardened building systems are the difference between a cleanup and a reconstruction. Electrical panels, switchgear, and transformers go above the design flood elevation or into rated enclosures; furnaces, boilers, and water heaters are elevated on platforms or replaced with wall-hung units above the line; ductwork below the elevation is eliminated or made flood-resistant. Plumbing fixtures that must stay low get backflow protection. The engineer coordinates these locations with the architect and the MEP designer early, because raising a panel after the walls are framed is expensive and raising it on the drawings is free.

What else do project teams ask?

What is the difference between wet and dry floodproofing?
Dry floodproofing seals the building so water cannot enter - engineered walls, shields on openings, and backflow prevention, certified watertight to the design elevation. Wet floodproofing lets water in and designs everything it touches to survive: flood-resistant materials, elevated systems, and no finished living space below. Homes must be elevated or wet-floodproofed; dry floodproofing is reserved for non-residential buildings under the floodplain rules.
What is a floodway and why does it restrict construction?
The floodway is the channel plus the adjacent land that must stay clear so the base flood can pass without raising water levels. Because any fill or obstruction in the floodway pushes floodwater higher onto everyone else, regulations strictly limit development there - new buildings typically must demonstrate no rise in flood elevations, which is difficult and often means building elsewhere. The engineer checks the floodway boundary before a site is even selected.
How does freeboard affect flood insurance?
Building the lowest floor above the required minimum - freeboard - is the single most effective way to cut flood insurance premiums, because the rating reflects how far the building sits above the expected flood level. Each additional foot reduces both the premium and the expected damage. The elevation certificate documents the finished height, and the insurer prices from it.
Who administers floodplain permits in Charleston?
The City of Charleston administers its floodplain ordinance for sites inside city limits, with a floodplain development permit running alongside the building permit. In unincorporated Kanawha County, the county is the authority having jurisdiction with its own ordinance, application portal, and review timeline. Requirements differ between the two, so the engineer confirms which ordinance governs the site and designs to it from the start.
