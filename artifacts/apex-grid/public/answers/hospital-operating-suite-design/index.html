How Are Hospital Operating Suites Engineered for Safety?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Operating suites are engineered with positive-pressure operating rooms, HEPA-filtered laminar airflow over the sterile field, ceiling booms for gases and power, shadow-free surgical lighting, and one-way clean-to-dirty workflow.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Airflow, Pressurization, and Filtration

The operating room pressure cascade is the backbone of infection control: the OR is positive to the sterile corridor, which is positive to the semi-restricted corridor, which is positive to public areas. Each step in the cascade is small but deliberate, and doors between zones should have minimal undercuts with closers so the relationships hold during traffic. Supply air passes through HEPA filtration before entering the room, and the diffuser array over the table is sized to cover the sterile field plus the surrounding team zone. Low wall returns on opposite walls pull air outward and away from the field. Temperature and humidity are controlled per room because different specialties run different thermal profiles, and the control system should let staff adjust within safe limits without calling facilities.

Booms, Lighting, and Medical Gases

Equipment booms are structural, electrical, and medical-gas infrastructure in one assembly. Each boom needs structural support coordinated with the ceiling structure, normal and critical-branch power, data drops, and medical gas outlets for oxygen, medical air, vacuum, and often waste anesthetic gas disposal. The boom's reach envelope must be mapped against the laminar diffuser and surgical lights so nothing parks in the clean airstream over the table. Surgical lighting is specified for intensity, color rendering, and shadow dilution from multiple light heads. General room lighting uses dimmable, sealed fixtures that can drop to near-dark for endoscopic and image-guided cases. All of it, lights, booms, diffusers, and imaging rails, shares the same ceiling real estate, which is why the reflected ceiling plan of an OR is one of the most coordination-intensive drawings in the hospital.

Operating Suite Engineering Checklist

Use this checklist during design and plan check: • OR positive to sterile core to corridor pressure cascade with door discipline • HEPA-filtered supply through a laminar diffuser array centered over the table • High air change rates per ASHRAE 170 with low wall returns opposite the field • Per-room temperature and humidity control within specialty-specific limits • Ceiling booms with medical gases, critical power, and data mapped clear of airflow • Shadow-free surgical lighting plus dimmable general lighting for imaging cases • One-way workflow: patient entry, sterile core, and separate soiled return path • Scrub sinks outside each OR door within the restricted zone

What else do project teams ask?

Why are operating rooms kept at positive pressure?
Positive pressure pushes air outward when doors open, keeping corridor contaminants from drifting into the sterile field. The OR sits at the top of a pressure cascade, positive to the sterile core and corridors, so every leakage path flows away from the patient rather than toward them.
What is laminar airflow in an operating room?
It is a low-turbulence, downward flow of HEPA-filtered air delivered through a diffuser array over the operating table. The clean air washes over the sterile field and the surgical team, then sweeps outward to low wall returns, carrying airborne contaminants away from the wound instead of letting them settle.
What utilities does a surgical boom carry?
A typical equipment boom carries medical gas outlets for oxygen, medical air, and vacuum plus waste anesthetic gas disposal, normal and critical-branch electrical receptacles, and data connections. The boom articulates so the team can position utilities exactly where each case needs them without floor cords.
How is a hybrid operating room different to engineer?
A hybrid OR adds fixed imaging equipment, such as a C-arm or CT, inside the sterile field. That brings structural loads, radiation shielding, dedicated cooling for the imaging electronics, and larger clearances, all of which must be coordinated with the laminar airflow, booms, and lighting from the start of design.
