How Does Title 24 Apply to California Supermarket Designs?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Under the 2025 California Energy Code / 2025 Standards, effective January 1, 2026, engineers document lighting power density, HVAC controls, and acceptance testing compliance for every supermarket system the code governs.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Lighting Power Density and Controls Compliance

The lighting compliance calculation is a space-by-space accounting: each area's floor square footage times its allowed watts per square foot, summed against the designed lighting power including case lighting. Supermarkets live close to the limit because merchandising demands light, so the engineer squeezes efficiency with high-efficacy LED, daylight dimming that earns its keep, and controls that shut off what is not needed. Mandatory controls are non-negotiable: daylight responsive controls in skylit and sidelit zones, occupancy or vacancy sensors in offices, restrooms, and stockrooms, automatic scheduling for the sales floor, and demand responsive controls that can shed lighting load on utility signal. The design shows every control device on the plans with its sequence, because the acceptance tester will verify each one against the code's test procedures.

HVAC Requirements and Acceptance Testing

Title 24's HVAC rules push supermarkets toward high-efficiency equipment with smart controls: minimum equipment efficiencies above federal baselines, economizers with fault detection and diagnostics that flag stuck dampers and failed sensors, demand-controlled ventilation keyed to occupancy, and optimum-start controls that learn the building's warmup behavior. Acceptance testing is the enforcement mechanism — and the part owners underestimate. A certified acceptance test technician must functionally test the lighting controls, economizers, demand-controlled ventilation, and other covered systems using the code's prescribed procedures, with results documented on the official forms before occupancy. The engineer designs with the test procedures in mind: accessible sensors, trendable points, and sequences clear enough to execute, so acceptance testing confirms compliance instead of discovering redesign.

California Supermarket Title 24 Checklist

A Title 24-compliant supermarket design documents every requirement: • Lighting power density calculations by space type against code allowances • Daylight harvesting, occupancy sensing, and scheduling controls on the plans • HVAC equipment efficiency, economizers, and fault detection per code • Demand-controlled ventilation and optimum-start sequences • Refrigeration-adjacent systems (case lighting, HVAC) in the compliance scope • Acceptance test procedures designed in: accessible, trendable, executable • Completed compliance forms with certified acceptance testing before occupancy

What else do project teams ask?

Which Title 24 code cycle applies to a new California supermarket?
The 2025 California Energy Code / 2025 Standards, effective January 1, 2026, governs new supermarket projects permitted under it. The applicable code is set by the permit application date, so a project submitted before the effective date follows the prior cycle. The engineer confirms the jurisdiction's adoption timeline early — some local amendments add requirements beyond the state code — and designs to the cycle that will actually be enforced at permit.
Does Title 24 regulate supermarket refrigeration directly?
Refrigeration equipment efficiency is primarily governed by federal standards, but Title 24 reaches the systems around it: the lighting inside refrigerated cases counts toward lighting power density, the HVAC protecting the cases must meet the code's efficiency and control rules, and the building envelope requirements affect the whole store. The engineer treats the store as one compliance picture rather than assuming refrigeration sits outside the code.
What is acceptance testing under Title 24?
Acceptance testing is functional testing performed by a certified technician using the code's prescribed procedures — proving that lighting controls dim and switch as designed, economizers modulate and report faults, demand-controlled ventilation responds to occupancy, and demand response sheds load on signal. Results are recorded on official forms submitted before occupancy. It is not optional and not the contractor's startup: it is the code's verification that the efficiency features actually work.
How do local amendments affect supermarket Title 24 compliance?
California cities and counties can adopt local energy ordinances stricter than the state code — reach codes — and many have. These can tighten lighting allowances, require all-electric designs, or add electrification and solar requirements that reshape the MEP design. The engineer checks the specific jurisdiction's amendments at project kickoff, because designing to the state code and discovering a stricter local ordinance at plan check is an expensive surprise.
