How Are Party Walls Engineered in Philadelphia Rowhouses?

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

Party walls in Philadelphia rowhouses are engineered as shared structural masonry walls: the engineer verifies wall thickness, bearing capacity, and fire separation, designs new floor and roof connections so loads transfer without overstressing the shared wall, and details underpinning or needle-beam support where adjacent excavation occurs - reviewed by Philadelphia's Department of Licenses and Inspections.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

How Party Walls Carry Load

In a typical Philadelphia rowhouse block, each party wall is a continuous brick spine running the depth of the houses. Floor joists from both adjoining homes bear in pockets on opposite faces, roof rafters or joists bear at the top, and the wall steps down to footings that may be shared, staggered, or independently built depending on the era of construction. The wall also provides the fire separation between dwellings, which is why the building code treats party walls as fire walls with continuity requirements that renovations must not compromise. When a renovation adds load - a new steel beam for an opened floor plan, a rooftop deck, an added story - the engineer checks the wall's capacity at the bearing points and along its height. Old brick in lime mortar has modest reliable compressive strength, so the design spreads concentrated loads with steel bearing plates, bond beams, or pilasters, and verifies that the wall's slenderness still works with the new loading. Openings cut through a party wall for combined units get lintels or arches sized for the masonry above, detailed so the neighbor's side of the wall is undisturbed.

Underpinning and Adjacent Construction

Underpinning is the engineered answer when adjacent excavation threatens a party wall's foundation. The work proceeds in short segments - typically a few feet at a time, alternating along the wall - so the masonry is never left spanning an unsupported gap. Each new underpin pier extends the foundation to a deeper, competent bearing layer, and the engineer designs the sequence, the pier sizes, and the monitoring: survey points on the wall read before, during, and after excavation to catch movement early. Where full underpinning is not warranted, the engineer may design shoring instead: needle beams passed through the wall above the excavation, carried on posts outside the dig, that temporarily support the masonry while adjacent footings are built. Pre-construction surveys of the neighboring buildings document existing cracks and conditions, protecting both owners if movement claims arise later. Throughout, vibration from demolition and pile driving is controlled and monitored, because old lime-mortar masonry is far more sensitive to shaking than modern construction.

What else do project teams ask?

What exactly is a party wall?
A wall shared by two adjoining buildings that serves a structural purpose for both - in Philadelphia rowhouses, the brick wall between homes that carries floor joists from each side. It is jointly owned in most cases, provides required fire separation between dwellings, and neither owner may alter it in ways that weaken the neighbor's building without proper engineering and, typically, a written agreement.
Who pays when a party wall needs repair?
Generally both owners share costs proportional to benefit or use, often governed by a party wall agreement recorded against the properties. When one owner's project - an excavation, a demolition, a new addition - creates the need for underpinning or shoring, that owner typically bears the engineering and construction cost. Disputes over shared walls are common enough that documenting conditions before work starts protects everyone.
Can I attach a new beam or cut an opening into the party wall?
With an engineer's design, usually yes for bearing connections like beam pockets with proper load spreading. Cutting through the wall to combine units is more involved: it requires a structural design for the opening, fire-separation restoration, waterproofing and acoustic treatment, the neighbor's agreement, and permits. The engineer also verifies the wall still performs as a fire wall after the alteration.
How does permitting differ for rowhouses inside versus outside Philadelphia?
Within the city, the Department of Licenses and Inspections reviews structural drawings and the Historical Commission adds review in designated districts. In surrounding townships in Delaware, Montgomery, or Bucks County, each municipality runs its own building department with its own permit portal, fee schedule, and review timeline. The party-wall engineering follows the same principles everywhere, but submittal requirements and inspection scheduling are set by the local authority having jurisdiction.
