Structural PE Stamp in Atlanta, GA: What the Office of Buildings Actually Reviews

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

A structural PE stamp in Atlanta means a licensed Georgia PE has taken responsible charge — verifying calculations, reviewing drawings against the building code and Georgia's amendments, and marking revisions before sealing — and the sealed package is reviewed by the Office of Buildings through the Accela (ACA) portal.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

The Atlanta structural review path

Everything structural in Atlanta flows through the Office of Buildings' ACA portal: intake, review, correction notices, resubmittal, and approval. The portal tracks each cycle, which is why our submittal discipline is strict — clouded revisions, a written response to every reviewer comment, and a calculation package that matches the current drawing revision. Reviewers apply the building code with Georgia's amendments, and Atlanta's local conditions show up in their comments: foundation systems on expansive red clay, lateral systems for mid-rise and podium buildings going up along the city's growth corridors, and existing-structure evaluations for the constant churn of renovations. We prepare the package the way a reviewer reads it — load path first, foundations second, details last.

What our responsible-charge review catches

The review we perform before a Georgia PE seals an Atlanta structural set is where most projects are actually saved. We check that the lateral system is continuous from roof to foundation with no missing drag struts or collector gaps, that wind and seismic loads were computed with the Georgia-adopted parameters rather than copied from another state's project, and that foundations reflect the geotechnical report — not a generic bearing value. On renovations, we verify existing member capacity and demand field verification of concealed framing before the seal, because Atlanta's older buildings frequently differ from their record drawings. Deferred-design items get explicit designation so nothing falls between the structural set and the fabricator's submittals. This review is the engineering work; the seal is just its signature.

Atlanta structural submittal checklist

An Atlanta structural package is ready for ACA submittal when these items are resolved. Completeness at intake is the fastest thing we can give a project's schedule. • AHJ confirmed: City of Atlanta Office of Buildings (not Fulton County) via parcel jurisdiction check • Calculation package indexed to drawing sheets, referencing the code edition with Georgia amendments • Foundation design tied explicitly to the geotechnical report's recommendations • Lateral system detailed continuously from roof to foundation, wind and seismic parameters documented • Special inspections listed on the drawings where the code requires them • Deferred-design items (connections, trusses, precast, curtain wall) designated as deferred submittals • Existing-structure verification plan for renovations, with field verification before sealing

What else do project teams ask?

Who reviews structural drawings for an Atlanta building permit?
The Office of Buildings, part of Atlanta's Department of City Planning, handles structural plan review, and submittals go through the city's Accela (ACA) online portal. Structural reviewers check the design against the adopted building code with Georgia's state amendments layered on top of the IBC. In our experience, their comments concentrate on lateral-force-resisting systems, foundation design tied to the geotechnical report, and special-inspection callouts. Submitting through ACA means every correction cycle is tracked in the portal, so the drawing set, the calculation package, and the responses all need to stay synchronized across resubmittals.
What does a licensed PE check before sealing structural drawings in Atlanta?
A proper responsible-charge review in Atlanta covers the full load path: gravity framing sized for the actual dead and live loads, a lateral system that handles wind and the Georgia-adopted seismic provisions, and foundations designed to the geotechnical report's bearing and settlement recommendations. We verify that the drawings match the calculations — member sizes, connection callouts, diaphragm details — and that deferred items like steel connections, wood trusses, and precast are explicitly designated as deferred submittals. Renovations get an existing-conditions review: confirming the as-built structure can carry new loads and requiring field verification where conditions are concealed. Only when the review is complete and revisions are incorporated does the PE sign and seal.
How long does structural review take in Atlanta?
First-cycle review timelines in Atlanta vary with the Office of Buildings' workload and the project's complexity, so we plan for several weeks and build at least one correction cycle into every schedule. What compresses the timeline is a complete submittal: drawings, a traceable calculation package, the geotechnical report, and energy and other discipline drawings that don't contradict the structural set. Incomplete packages get correction notices that restart the clock. We submit when the package is genuinely ready — an early submission with missing calculations costs more time than waiting a week to finish them.
Does a Georgia PE stamp cover projects outside Atlanta, like Marietta or Fulton County?
A Georgia PE license is valid statewide, so the same licensed engineer can seal drawings for Marietta, Fulton County, or anywhere else in Georgia — but the AHJ changes. Marietta runs its own Development Services review, and unincorporated Fulton County permits through Planning and Community Services, each with its own checklists and review culture. The structural review we perform before sealing is the same, but the submittal has to be tailored to the jurisdiction receiving it. Confirming the AHJ from the parcel's jurisdiction before design starts avoids submitting to the wrong office.
