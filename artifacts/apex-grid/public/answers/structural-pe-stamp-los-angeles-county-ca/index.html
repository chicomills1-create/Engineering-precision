Structural PE Stamp in Los Angeles County, CA: Unincorporated-Area Review

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

A structural PE stamp in unincorporated LA County means a California PE in responsible charge under B&P Code 6735 — verifying seismic design, detailing, and hillside foundation performance — for permits reviewed by the County Public Works Building and Safety division.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

The county AHJ and the seismic review

Unincorporated LA County's Building and Safety reviewers apply the adopted code in one of the most seismically demanding jurisdictions in the United States, and our submittals are prepared for that audience. The responsible-charge review verifies seismic parameters from the adopted code and the geotechnical site class, the lateral system's selection and high-seismic detailing, and the complete load path from roof to foundation. We check diaphragm and collector continuity, connection design for seismic forces, and foundation performance under seismic demands — including hillside-specific review where the site requires it. The calculation package states every parameter and references the adopted edition explicitly. Precision in the submittal mirrors the precision the county's reviewers bring to the check.

Foundations, hillsides, and the sealed package

Foundation review implements the geotechnical investigation: bearing, settlement, seismic site class effects, and hillside measures — depth, drainage, and retaining coordination — where the report calls for them. Special inspections are noted on the drawings for the seismic and foundation work the code requires. We confirm drawings match calculations throughout, designate delegated-design items as deferred submittals, and organize the package for the county's correction process with indexed calculations and clear revision responses. Existing structures get field verification before the seal, with the lateral system evaluated against current seismic expectations. The California PE seals under B&P 6735 when the review is complete: seismic design verified, hillside demands addressed, and every revision confirmed.

Unincorporated LA County structural submittal checklist

A county structural package is ready when the AHJ is confirmed and the seismic engineering is fully verified. • Jurisdiction confirmed as unincorporated LA County (not the City of Los Angeles) from parcel records • Seismic design verified: parameters, site class, lateral system selection and high-seismic detailing • Complete seismic load path traced: diaphragms, collectors, vertical elements, foundations • Hillside foundation, retaining, and drainage demands addressed per the geotechnical report • Calculations indexed to drawings with the adopted code edition stated • Special inspections noted; deferred-design items designated as deferred submittals • California PE in responsible charge per B&P Code 6735 seals only after review and verified revisions

What else do project teams ask?

Do unincorporated LA County projects permit through the City of Los Angeles?
No. Unincorporated Los Angeles County areas permit through the County's Public Works Building and Safety division — a separate AHJ from the City of Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety, with its own submittal process, checklists, and reviewers. The county covers enormous unincorporated territory, from urbanized pockets to remote hillside and desert communities. Confirm the parcel's jurisdiction from county records before design: the engineering review we perform is the same, but the submittal must be built for the AHJ that will actually review it.
What does B&P Code section 6735 require of the sealing engineer?
Section 6735 addresses the engineer in responsible charge: the California PE who seals the documents must have been in responsible charge of the engineering work — meaning genuine direction, review, and control of the design, with the calculations verified and the engineering decisions made or approved by that engineer. It is California's legal backbone for the principle behind every page we seal: the stamp certifies real engineering oversight. Our process documents responsible charge explicitly — who reviewed what, what was verified, what revisions were required — before the seal is applied.
How does hillside terrain in unincorporated areas change the structural review?
Hillside sites add foundation, retaining, and slope-stability demands on top of the seismic design: our review verifies foundation systems designed for slope conditions per the geotechnical investigation, retaining structures engineered for lateral earth pressures plus seismic increments, and drainage provisions that protect slope stability. We check that the geotechnical report's hillside recommendations — setbacks, foundation depths, drainage — were implemented in the structural drawings. County reviewers scrutinize hillside structural submittals closely, and a package that visibly implements the geotech findings moves while one that waves at them stalls.
What seismic detailing do county reviewers expect to see?
Detailing appropriate to the seismic design category: special moment frames, special shear walls, or other high-seismic systems detailed per the code's seismic provisions — confinement, continuity, collector and diaphragm detailing, and foundation ties where required. Our pre-seal review checks the detailing drawing by drawing, because the seismic performance of a Los Angeles County building lives in its details. Reviewers at Building and Safety practice in a high-seismic jurisdiction daily; they read detailing with expert eyes, and our review is calibrated to match.
