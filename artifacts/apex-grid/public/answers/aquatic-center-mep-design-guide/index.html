What Does Aquatic Center MEP Design Include?

An aquatic center is three engineering projects in one building: a natatorium with its dehumidification plant and corrosion-proof envelope, a pool-plant with filtration and chemical systems, and a public assembly building with surge-capable plumbing and HVAC. The MEP design has to serve all three without letting any of them compromise the others.

The concise answer

Mechanical: dehumidification sized to the evaporation load (competition pool plus any leisure/warm-water bodies, which evaporate more), ventilation to ASHRAE 62.1, supply air washing cold surfaces, and heat recovery that preheats pool and domestic hot water. Plumbing: recirculation and filtration, chemical storage with dedicated exhaust and spill containment, surge tanks, and locker-room plumbing for meet-day peaks. Electrical: service for the dehumidification and filtration plants, pool lighting, surge protection, and life-safety systems — all specified corrosion-resistant for the pool-hall environment.

The corrosion question

Chloramine-laden humid air attacks everything: standard galvanized ductwork, unprotected steel, even some stainless grades. Aquatic-center MEP specifies corrosion-resistant materials throughout the pool hall — coated or stainless ductwork, sealed electrical enclosures, corrosion-rated equipment — and the structural system gets the same treatment. Value-engineering the corrosion resistance out is how buildings get a ten-year envelope instead of a thirty-year one.

Operating cost as a design criterion

Municipal aquatic centers live on operating budgets, so energy design is programming: heat recovery from dehumidification to pool water, high-efficiency filtration with variable-speed pumps, pool covers for overnight evaporation control, and controls that set back without letting humidity drift into the condensing range. The engineer who designs for the utility bill earns the operator's trust for the next project.

Frequently asked questions

What is the biggest MEP cost in an aquatic center?
The dehumidification plant — sized to the evaporation load — plus the pool recirculation and filtration systems. Together they typically exceed the rest of the building's MEP combined.
How does heat recovery work in a natatorium?
The dehumidification process rejects heat; heat exchangers redirect it to pool water heating and domestic hot water, often covering most of the pool heating load. It's standard practice in well-designed facilities.
Why do aquatic centers corrode so fast?
Chloramines in warm humid air attack metals aggressively. Prevention is corrosion-resistant MEP materials, proper dehumidification (less moisture = less corrosion), and envelope detailing that keeps moisture out of wall cavities.
Do leisure pools change the engineering?
Significantly: warmer water, play features, and higher agitation multiply the evaporation load versus a calm competition pool. A center with both needs the dehumidification plant sized for the combined worst case.
