MEP PE Stamp in Nashville, TN: HVAC, Energy Code, and ePermits Review

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran. · Updated 2026-09-15

An MEP PE stamp in Nashville means a licensed Tennessee PE has taken responsible charge — verifying HVAC loads, energy-code consistency, and plumbing/electrical coordination — before sealing drawings reviewed by the Metro Codes Department through the ePermits portal.

I'm Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder of Apex Grid Engineering and a U.S. Air Force veteran. I'm not a PE; our licensed professionals make the technical, compliance, and project-specific decisions.

Mechanical: four-season loads and moisture control

Our Nashville mechanical review runs the full load calculation first — block and zone heating and cooling for the actual building, not a similar one — then follows the chain to equipment selection, distribution layout, and controls. Summer humidity drives dedicated attention to outdoor-air treatment and dehumidification: we verify the design controls moisture at part-load conditions, not just at peak, because Nashville's shoulder seasons are when mold problems start. Winter gets equal rigor: heating capacity for design temperatures, freeze protection on piping, coils, and rooftop equipment, and ventilation that meets code when the building is buttoned up. Rooftop and outdoor equipment is coordinated with structural for weight and wind. Every link verified before the PE seals.

Electrical, plumbing, and the coordination check

Electrical review verifies service and distribution sizing, panel schedules that match the mechanical equipment's actual electrical characteristics, lighting layouts within energy-code allowances, and fire-alarm and life-safety interfaces. Plumbing review covers fixture counts for the occupancy, water and sanitary sizing on calculated demand, and backflow prevention where required. Then the coordination pass that defines responsible charge: risers landing in shafts that exist on the architectural plans, equipment clearances meeting working-space rules, and structural penetrations the framing drawings account for. We check the MEP set against the architectural and structural sets sheet by sheet — the inconsistencies this catches are exactly the ones that generate ePermits correction notices.

Nashville MEP submittal checklist

A Nashville MEP package is ready for Metro Codes when every item below is verified. Completeness is the fastest permit strategy. • Heating and cooling load calculations traceable to equipment schedules • Dehumidification and freeze-protection provisions designed for Nashville's climate • Energy-code compliance forms consistent with final drawings and schedules • Electrical panel schedules matched to mechanical equipment characteristics • Plumbing fixture counts, pipe sizing, and backflow provisions verified • MEP set coordinated with architectural and structural drawings • ePermits submittal organized for tracked corrections with clouded revisions

What else do project teams ask?

What does Metro Codes check on sealed MEP drawings?
Metro Codes' reviewers check code compliance and internal consistency across the mechanical, electrical, and plumbing sets. Their comments typically target HVAC load calculations versus equipment schedules, energy-code compliance forms versus the drawings, plumbing fixture counts and pipe sizing, and electrical service and panel schedules. They also flag coordination failures — ductwork clashing with structure, plumbing risers without shafts, equipment lacking required clearances. Our responsible-charge review pre-checks all of these, because a correction cycle through ePermits costs weeks that a thorough internal review would have saved.
How does Nashville's climate change the mechanical design review?
Nashville needs both serious cooling and real heating, plus humidity control in summer and freeze protection in winter — so the mechanical design gets reviewed as a four-season system. We verify cooling loads and dehumidification for the humid summers, heating capacity and freeze protection for winter cold snaps, and ventilation rates that hold up year-round. Equipment selection has to satisfy the peak of each season, not just the annual average. Reviewers look for this seasonal completeness, and designs that handle only the cooling case draw comments when the heating and freeze-protection provisions are thin.
What energy-code documentation does Nashville require with MEP drawings?
Documented energy-code compliance is expected with the permit package, and the critical requirement is consistency: the compliance forms or model outputs must match the construction documents. We verify the compliance path, check every input — envelope values, equipment efficiencies, lighting power densities — against the drawings and schedules, and make sure the forms reflect the final design iteration. A common failure is forms completed from an early design that no longer matches the sealed drawings; our review catches that mismatch before Metro Codes does.
Who can seal MEP drawings in Tennessee?
A Tennessee-licensed PE sealing MEP work must be competent in the discipline and must have taken responsible charge — meaning they directed the design or personally verified it through genuine review. Firms typically coordinate licensed engineers across mechanical, electrical, and plumbing as the project demands. Sealing a discipline's drawings without real oversight is not responsible charge and not acceptable. Our process documents who holds responsible charge for each system so the seal always reflects actual engineering supervision.
