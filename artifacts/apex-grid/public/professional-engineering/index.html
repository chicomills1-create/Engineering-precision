What Makes Engineering Professional

The term 'Professional Engineer' (PE) has a specific legal meaning — it refers to engineers who have earned state licensure by meeting education, experience, and examination requirements.

  • PE licensure requires passing the Fundamentals of Engineering (FE) exam
  • Four or more years of qualifying experience under a licensed PE
  • Passing the Principles and Practice of Engineering (PE) exam in the relevant discipline
  • Current authorization from the applicable state board for the project jurisdiction
  • Continuing education to maintain active licensure
  • Signing and sealing authority depends on the applicable board rules, project discipline, and AHJ requirements

The PE Stamp and What It Certifies

When a PE stamps an engineering document, they are certifying that the engineering meets applicable codes and standards — and accepting professional, legal, and ethical responsibility for that certification.

  • The PE stamp certifies that the engineering meets applicable codes
  • The PE accepts professional liability for the stamped work
  • Some building departments require a seal from an appropriately authorized professional as part of permit review
  • The responsible professional's current license and authorization must be verified for the project jurisdiction
  • Stamping work the PE did not directly supervise is an ethics violation
  • Any Apex Grid seal is issued only after the responsible individual's license, firm authorization, discipline, and AHJ requirements are verified

Engineering Ethics

Licensed Professional Engineers are bound by codes of ethics that prioritize public safety, honesty, and competence.

  • Engineers hold public safety as the paramount professional obligation
  • Engineers practice only within their area of competence
  • Engineers do not misrepresent their qualifications or work
  • Engineers do not sign work they did not supervise — responsible charge is required
  • Engineers report known violations of engineering safety
  • Responsible charge and any seal or stamp are addressed in the project scope; no professional should sign work outside the applicable rules
