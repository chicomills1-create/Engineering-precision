The Peoria venue market

Central Illinois school-district bonds fund athletic complexes where humidity, tornado exposure, and freeze-thaw all shape the engineering.

Illinois school districts fund athletic facilities through referenda and bonds, with the Chicago suburbs running some of the nation's most aggressive school-building programs — new fieldhouses, natatoriums, and stadium complexes arrive on fixed referendum timelines. Municipal park districts operate enormous indoor recreation and aquatics portfolios, and private ice rinks and indoor-turf facilities are dense across the collar counties.

Electrical design for sports lighting systems

Behind every well-lit field is an electrical design that most spectators never think about: service sizing for lighting loads that can exceed the rest of the building combined, voltage-drop calculations on long pole runs, surge and lightning protection (outdoor sports lighting is a lightning rod by design), and controls that let a parks department run practice lighting at 30% and game lighting at full output from a phone.

We coordinate the lighting design with the structural scope — pole foundations, catwalk and rigging loads for arena systems — and with the utility on service upgrades, because a four-pole LED stadium system can trigger a service-size conversation the owner didn't expect. For broadcast-capable venues, we add the power and dimming infrastructure television requires: flicker-free drivers, vertical-illuminance uniformity, and spare capacity for temporary broadcast compounds.

Stormwater and site work for tournament-grade complexes

Multi-field tournament complexes live or die on drainage: a weekend tournament can't survive fields that hold water, and the local stormwater authority won't approve a site that pushes its problem downstream. We design comprehensive stormwater systems — detention, water-quality treatment, and conveyance sized for the jurisdiction's design storms — integrated with the field grading so the complex drains as a system.

The civil scope extends to access and circulation: tournament traffic patterns, bus and team-vehicle staging, pedestrian separation from vehicles, and lighting and utility corridors that serve future phases. Phased complexes need a civil master plan on day one, so phase three doesn't require tearing up phase one.

Fire protection coordination for venues

Sprinkler, fire alarm, and smoke-control design for assembly venues is a coordination exercise across every discipline: sprinkler zoning that respects the long-span structure and the seating-bowl geometry, fire-alarm notification that reaches every occupied space including the bowl at full crowd noise, and smoke-control sequences tied to the HVAC design for smoke-protected seating.

We coordinate the fire-protection narrative with the code strategy from schematic design — because the AHJ's fire reviewer will ask the hardest questions on the project, and the answers need to be in the documents, not in a meeting.

Drainage design for aquatic and locker areas

Wet areas fail at the drains: pool decks that pond chlorinated water, locker rooms where the floor never dries, trench drains that clog with the debris of a thousand athletes. We design deck and locker-room drainage with slopes that actually move water, trench and area drains sized for the real flows, and cleanout access the maintenance staff can reach without dismantling the building.

Materials matter as much as slopes: chemical-resistant drainage in pool areas, slip-resistant surfacing throughout, and waterproofing details that keep the water in the wet areas instead of migrating into the structure below.

Municipal parks-and-recreation focus

Municipal owners buy venues the community will use daily for decades, so the engineering optimizes for operating cost and durability: high-efficiency HVAC with deep turndown, heat recovery where the program allows it, vandal-resistant plumbing fixtures, and envelopes detailed for the local climate's worst behavior. Capital-program procurement — phased submittals, public-bid documents — is part of the scope, not an afterthought.

University-adjacent athletics focus

In college towns, school-district and municipal venues are judged against the university standard down the road. That means competition-grade gyms with proper humidity control for wood floors, natatoriums held to collegiate dehumidification practice, stadium lighting verified photometrically rather than assumed, and practice facilities engineered for the daily-use abuse that varsity programs dish out.

Frequently asked questions

Does Apex design natatoriums to ASHRAE standards?
Yes. ASHRAE 62.1 sets the ventilation baseline; we size dehumidification to the pool-water evaporation load, hold the hall at 50–60% relative humidity, and detail corrosion-resistant envelopes so the building lasts. Our natatorium dehumidification guide covers the engineering.
What civil work does a tournament sports complex need?
Laser-graded fields with underdrain systems, stormwater detention and treatment per local standards, surge-capable parking and circulation for peak crowds, and phased utility planning so future expansion doesn't tear up phase one. Multi-field complexes are civil-engineering projects with buildings on them.
Can an existing gym be converted to a fieldhouse or practice facility?
Often — with structural verification of the long-span system for new loads, HVAC upgrades for the higher occupancy and exertion levels, and code review of the changed use and occupant load. A feasibility assessment up front prevents the conversion from becoming a reconstruction.
Do you handle the permit process with the local AHJ?
Yes. We build permit packages around a clear code narrative — occupancy, egress, structural criteria, MEP compliance paths — organized the way plan reviewers actually work, plus phased submittals where the jurisdiction allows. For school-district work we add state school-facility review coordination where it applies.
