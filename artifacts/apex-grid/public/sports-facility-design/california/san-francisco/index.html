The San Francisco venue market

California school districts pass local bonds at a remarkable pace, funding new high-school stadiums, aquatic centers, and fieldhouses across the state — each on a fixed DSA-reviewed timeline that rewards engineers fluent in Division of the State Architect submittals. Municipal recreation departments run some of the nation's largest community-center and aquatics programs, and private youth-sports complexes are booming in every major metro.

Retrofitting to LED: what the electrical engineer must verify

LED retrofits of legacy metal-halide sports lighting cut energy use dramatically — but only when the electrical and structural scope is engineered, not assumed. Existing poles must be verified for the new fixtures' weight and wind sail area; existing branch circuits must be checked against LED driver harmonics and inrush; and the control system must be designed for how the venue actually operates — practice, game, and broadcast presets with remote access.

We start every retrofit with a photometric model of the proposed system against IES targets for the venue's level of play, then engineer the electrical distribution, surge protection, and structural verification to match. The result is lighting that meets the standard on the field, controls spill light for the neighbors, and doesn't surprise the owner with a service upgrade mid-project.

Parking, circulation, and the surge crowd

A venue's civil design is tested in twenty minutes: the surge when an event lets out. We design parking capacity, internal circulation, pedestrian paths, and egress routes for the peak occupant load — not the average Tuesday — coordinated with the local traffic authority on signal timing and turn-lane improvements where the venue's traffic study requires them.

Accessible parking and paths, emergency-vehicle access that stays clear during sellouts, and bus and rideshare staging are designed into the site plan from the start. For school venues, the same circulation must handle daily drop-off and pickup without conflicting with the event plan — two traffic systems sharing one site, engineered to never meet.

Assembly occupancy: egress, occupant load, and code strategy

Sports venues are Assembly occupancies, and the code treats them accordingly: occupant-load calculations that drive exit counts and widths, travel-distance limits, smoke-protected seating provisions for large arenas, and accessible seating and egress distributed throughout — not clustered in one corner. The code strategy starts in schematic design, because exit stairs and seating-bowl geometry are nearly impossible to fix late.

We coordinate the life-safety plan across disciplines: the architect's egress geometry, the structural engineer's smoke-protected seating provisions, the electrical engineer's emergency lighting and fire-alarm zoning, and the mechanical engineer's smoke-control sequences. For venues pursuing phased or multi-use programs, the code analysis must cover every configuration the building will actually host.

Indoor turf and fieldhouse ventilation

Indoor turf buildings are some of the hardest-working HVAC loads in sports design: a large clear-span volume, high occupant densities during tournaments, solar gain through any daylighting, and heat that stratifies at the ceiling while players compete at the surface. Ventilation rates follow ASHRAE 62.1 for the occupancy, but the design must also manage temperature uniformity across a 200-foot field and purge the building effectively between events.

We zone fieldhouses for their actual use patterns — full-field tournaments, half-field practices, and empty weekdays — with variable-speed air handling and demand-controlled ventilation that scales energy use to occupancy. High-bay destratification, either through the HVAC design or supplemental fans, keeps the playing surface from sitting in a cold pool while the ceiling bakes.

Venue modernization and retrofit focus

Renovating an aging gym, pool, or stadium starts with verification, not assumptions: structural assessment of the long-span system for new scoreboard, lighting, and rigging loads; HVAC evaluation against current ventilation and humidity-control needs; electrical capacity checks for LED conversions and event power; and envelope investigation for the moisture problems decades of deferred maintenance hide. A good retrofit engineer finds the building's real condition before drawing the first detail.

Esports and multi-use arena flexibility focus

The modern venue program increasingly includes esports and convertible event space alongside traditional sports — which changes the engineering: dense power and data infrastructure at floor level, theatrical lighting and production power to complement sports lighting, and HVAC zoning flexible enough for a 500-person tournament or a 5,000-person concert. Designing the floor boxes, rigging capacity, and acoustic separation for conversion from day one costs little and future-proofs the building's revenue.

Frequently asked questions

What civil work does a tournament sports complex need?
Laser-graded fields with underdrain systems, stormwater detention and treatment per local standards, surge-capable parking and circulation for peak crowds, and phased utility planning so future expansion doesn't tear up phase one. Multi-field complexes are civil-engineering projects with buildings on them.
Can an existing gym be converted to a fieldhouse or practice facility?
Often — with structural verification of the long-span system for new loads, HVAC upgrades for the higher occupancy and exertion levels, and code review of the changed use and occupant load. A feasibility assessment up front prevents the conversion from becoming a reconstruction.
Do you handle the permit process with the local AHJ?
Yes. We build permit packages around a clear code narrative — occupancy, egress, structural criteria, MEP compliance paths — organized the way plan reviewers actually work, plus phased submittals where the jurisdiction allows. For school-district work we add state school-facility review coordination where it applies.
What makes Apex different for sports venues?
Single-team accountability across structural, MEP, and civil; real technical depth in the hard venue systems (natatorium dehumidification, long-span structures, sports lighting, ice-rink refrigeration); and delivery built for bond-funded timelines — fast-track phasing, early procurement, and permit-ready documents.
