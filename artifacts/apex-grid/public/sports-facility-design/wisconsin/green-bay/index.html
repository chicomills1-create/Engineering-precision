The state's hockey and basketball cultures sustain exceptional rink and gym investment, municipal recreation departments run strong community-center portfolios, and the Green Bay and Milwaukee venue districts set the technical bar.

Lighting controls: the system behind the fixtures

The fixtures get the attention, but the control system determines whether the venue's lighting is an asset or a complaint line. We design zone-based controls with presets the operator actually uses — practice, game, broadcast, event, and security — plus astronomical time-clock scheduling, remote access for the parks or athletics director, and auto-shutoff that kills the "lights left on all night" utility surprise.

For broadcast-capable venues, the controls add flicker-free dimming curves and DMX integration for event production; for community venues, simplicity wins — a wall station and a phone app, not a lighting console that needs a technician. Every design includes the spill-light documentation the permit and the neighbors require.

Grading and earthwork for stadium sites

Stadium and arena sites move serious dirt: balancing cut and fill across a bowl excavation, retaining systems where the site is tight, and subgrade preparation that gives the structure a uniform bearing surface. We design the earthwork with the geotechnical report open — over-excavating and replacing problem soils, chemically stabilizing expansive clay, and dewatering plans that keep the excavation workable through the wet season.

For hillside or constrained urban sites, the civil design integrates retaining walls, terraced grading, and construction access that doesn't shut down the surrounding streets. The goal is a site that drains away from the building forever, not just on opening day.

Permitting assembly venues with the AHJ

Permitting a sports venue means permitting the hardest building type in the code: high occupant loads, long spans, complex MEP, and public assembly — every reviewer looks closely. We build the permit package around a clear code narrative: occupancy classifications, construction type, occupant-load and egress diagrams, structural criteria, and MEP compliance paths, organized the way plan reviewers actually work.

For school-district and municipal projects, we add the jurisdiction's own layer — state school-facility review where it applies, local amendments, and the bond-funded schedule that makes review-cycle time a project risk. A permit package engineered for the reviewer's desk clears faster than one engineered for the design team's convenience.

Year-round rink operations and energy

A community rink that operates year-round is an energy story: the refrigeration plant runs through summer, the dehumidification fights humid outdoor air, and the building still needs to be comfortable for spectators. The design answers with heat recovery at every opportunity — rink-plant waste heat to snow-melt pits, domestic hot water, and space heating — plus envelope and controls that minimize the loads the plant has to fight.

We model the annual energy profile honestly, so the municipality or operator sees the real operating budget before the bond vote — not after the first summer utility bill.

Community recreation-center engineering focus

Community centers stack loud and quiet uses in one building — courts beside meeting rooms, pools beside lobbies — so acoustic separation gets engineered alongside the MEP: partition STC ratings, floor-ceiling IIC ratings, and HVAC that doesn't carry noise between rooms. Gym humidity control protects wood floors, and durable, hose-down-capable finishes keep maintenance budgets sane under daily public use.

Tournament sports-complex engineering focus

Outdoor tournament complexes are civil-engineering projects with buildings on them: laser-graded fields with underdrain systems, synthetic-turf base sections where specified, stormwater detention and treatment per local standards, and parking and circulation designed for the twenty-minute surge when events let out. A phased civil master plan keeps future expansion from tearing up phase one.

Frequently asked questions

Can an existing gym be converted to a fieldhouse or practice facility?
Often — with structural verification of the long-span system for new loads, HVAC upgrades for the higher occupancy and exertion levels, and code review of the changed use and occupant load. A feasibility assessment up front prevents the conversion from becoming a reconstruction.
Do you handle the permit process with the local AHJ?
Yes. We build permit packages around a clear code narrative — occupancy, egress, structural criteria, MEP compliance paths — organized the way plan reviewers actually work, plus phased submittals where the jurisdiction allows. For school-district work we add state school-facility review coordination where it applies.
What makes Apex different for sports venues?
Single-team accountability across structural, MEP, and civil; real technical depth in the hard venue systems (natatorium dehumidification, long-span structures, sports lighting, ice-rink refrigeration); and delivery built for bond-funded timelines — fast-track phasing, early procurement, and permit-ready documents.
What engineering disciplines does a Green Bay sports facility need?
A complete venue needs structural (long-span roofs, grandstands, pool vessels), mechanical (arena HVAC, natatorium dehumidification, ice-rink refrigeration), electrical (sports lighting to IES levels, event and broadcast power), plumbing (surge-capable fixtures, aquatic plants), and civil (grading, stormwater, surge parking) — coordinated under one accountable team so the seams between disciplines never become change orders.
