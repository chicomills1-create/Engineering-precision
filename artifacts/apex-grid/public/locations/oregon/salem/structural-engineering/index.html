Structural Engineering for Salem Projects

Salem structural design uses the 2022 OSSC and site-specific seismic, wind, rain, and geotechnical inputs. Cascadia and crustal seismic hazards make lateral design and, on susceptible sites, liquefaction review important; wet soils, floodplain elevation, and corrosion exposure also influence foundations and detailing.

Plan Review Process

Salem routes commercial building applications through its permit services and coordinates building, planning, fire, and public-works review. Land-use, tree, floodplain, right-of-way, and utility approvals may be separate; confirm the applicable review path before submittal.

Local Amendments

Oregon's specialty codes apply statewide and include Oregon amendments; Salem adds land-use, tree, stormwater, and administrative requirements. Do not substitute the base IBC or an older Oregon energy edition for the effective state specialty code.

Utility Coordination

Portland General Electric provides electric service; the City of Salem provides water, sewer, and stormwater services in the city. Confirm PGE service capacity and city connection, frontage, and stormwater requirements early.

Salem Market Context

As Oregon's capital, Salem has state and institutional, healthcare, education, multifamily, food and agriculture, and public-sector construction. Wet-weather site logistics, stormwater, seismic retrofit, and wildfire-smoke resilience are recurring design considerations.

Salem FAQs

Which code edition does Salem use?
The 2022 Oregon Structural Specialty Code is based on the 2021 IBC with Oregon amendments. Commercial energy compliance uses the effective 2023 Oregon Energy Efficiency Specialty Code; confirm transition dates for the permit.
Who provides Salem utilities?
Portland General Electric provides electric service, while the City of Salem provides water, sewer, and stormwater services in the city. Capacity and connection requirements are parcel-specific.
What hazards affect Salem structural and civil work?
Cascadia and local seismic hazards, wet soils, seasonal flooding, heavy rain, and wildfire smoke are important inputs. Geotechnical, floodplain, and stormwater reviews should be started with the concept site plan.
