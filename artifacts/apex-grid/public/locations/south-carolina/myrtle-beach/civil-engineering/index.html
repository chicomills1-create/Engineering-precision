Civil Engineering in Myrtle Beach, South Carolina

Myrtle Beach civil plans must protect dunes and public access while routing intense rainfall through flat, sandy, high-groundwater sites.

Permit authority

Myrtle Beach and Horry County planning and building staff coordinate local review; floodplain, beach-resource, SCDES, fire, drainage, right-of-way, and Grand Strand Water reviews may be separate.

Code context

Confirm Horry and city drainage, floodplain, beach or dune, erosion, right-of-way, utility, and SCDES stormwater requirements before setting grades.

Local conditions

Myrtle Beach's flat sandy ground, beach wind, surge, salt spray, high groundwater, and seasonal occupancy

Typical project types

hotel sites, beach additions, ADUs, parking, restaurants, subdivisions, and coastal redevelopment

Flat sand needs a real receiving system

A Myrtle Beach outlet should account for groundwater, tide or surge, dunes, downstream capacity, easements, erosion, and the owner's maintenance duty. Infiltration and detention assumptions require field evidence.

Dunes and access constrain small projects

A deck, driveway, ADU, or addition can change dune setbacks, accessible paths, finished floors, flood openings, and runoff toward a neighbor or road. Put these relationships on the surveyed plan.

Temporary controls protect coastal water

SCDES stormwater, local erosion measures, beach-resource constraints, utility locates, and final stabilization need a sequence distinct from permanent conveyance and private maintenance.

Permit and engineering steps

  1. Confirm the Myrtle Beach or Horry County jurisdiction, flood zone, coastal or dune status, disturbance, frontage, survey datum, and utility service.
  2. Obtain current survey and request outfall, groundwater, utility, fire-access, flood, erosion, beach, and right-of-way information.
  3. Coordinate grades, detention or conveyance, dune protection, finished floors, ADU access, erosion controls, utilities, and foundations.
  4. Track city, county, SCDES, flood, and utility corrections and retain revised calculations, maintenance notes, and as-built elevations.

Civil Engineering in Myrtle Beach, South Carolina FAQs

Does a Myrtle Beach ADU trigger coastal civil review?
A detached pad, new impervious area, driveway, floodplain, dune or setback issue, sewer tie, or changed drainage can trigger civil or coastal review. The address controls.
Why is a beach outfall difficult?
Groundwater, tide, surge, sand, erosion, downstream capacity, dunes, easements, and maintenance all affect whether a discharge is workable.
Can grading change a Myrtle Beach building design?
Yes. Finished floor, flood openings, stairs, accessible routes, foundations, utility elevations, and retaining or erosion details may all move with a drainage correction.
