Plan Review Process

Worcester accepts commercial permit applications through its online permitting services and routes them through building, zoning, fire, and other reviews as applicable. Historic, wetlands, public-way, and utility approvals can be separate, and larger projects should confirm the required review sequence with Inspectional Services.

Local Amendments

Worcester enforces the statewide 10th-edition code and its existing-building provisions, with local zoning, historic, wetlands, stormwater, and administrative overlays. Its Specialized Energy Code status is separate from the base building-code adoption and affects commercial energy design.

Utility Coordination

National Grid supplies electricity and natural gas in Worcester; the City of Worcester Department of Public Works provides water and sewer service. Confirm electric capacity, gas service, and municipal connection and street-opening requirements early for redevelopment and laboratory or industrial loads.

Worcester FAQs

What code does Worcester use for commercial permits?
Worcester enforces the 10th edition Massachusetts State Building Code, 780 CMR, incorporating the 2021 I-Codes with Massachusetts amendments. The city is also a Specialized Energy Code community, which affects energy compliance beyond the base code.
Which utilities serve Worcester?
National Grid supplies electricity and natural gas, while the City of Worcester provides water and sewer service. Capacity, service extensions, and street-opening requirements should be verified for the actual project address.
Why is existing-building investigation important?
Worcester's mills, masonry buildings, institutional campuses, and dense redevelopment parcels can conceal structural and utility conditions. Field verification, excavation coordination, and phasing are often as important as the new system design.
