DETROIT | CIVIL ENGINEERING

Civil engineering for Detroit sites

Detroit civil work often starts with a block of impervious land, a legacy use, and incomplete utility records. A workable site plan has to connect grading, access, stormwater, environmental constraints, and public infrastructure rather than stopping at a parking count. Scope, survey quality, and agency jurisdiction are confirmed for each parcel.

Permit authority

BSEED administers Detroit building and site-related permit review, but a project may also need DWSD review, EGLE authorization, Wayne County Drain Commissioner involvement, zoning approval, floodplain review, or road-agency permission. Public-connection and stormwater requirements are not assumed from a neighboring parcel; the owner should obtain current agency direction. A Detroit civil package should identify BSEED, DWSD, Wayne County Drain Commissioner, EGLE, and the road authority by task instead of calling them one approval.

Code context

Michigan's statewide construction code controls building-related work, while Detroit zoning, site standards, utility standards, and stormwater procedures govern the civil package. EGLE permits can apply to wetlands, floodplains, soil erosion and sedimentation, and discharges. The applicable threshold and review route should be confirmed with the city and agencies before final plans. Use the current Detroit zoning, stormwater, right-of-way, and Michigan building-code materials applicable to the parcel; thresholds for environmental permits are verified before disturbance.

Local conditions

Urban Detroit sites commonly contain undocumented fill, former industrial contamination, combined-sewer constraints, tight rights-of-way, and abandoned service laterals. Flat grades make positive drainage and accessible routes especially important. Riverfront or Rouge-area parcels require current FEMA information and consideration of groundwater, flood elevation, and resilient utility placement. Former auto yards, combined sewers, alleys, Detroit River backwater, and variable fill make a record-utility plan inadequate without field locating and environmental coordination.

Typical project types

Vacant-lot infill; brownfield redevelopment; industrial and logistics yards; neighborhood retail; multifamily parking and drives; complete-site utility replacement; river-adjacent redevelopment. Distinct Detroit site work includes a Delray brownfield yard, a Brush Park infill court, and a Rouge River logistics access drive.

Treat the survey as a design deliverable

Parcel lines, alleys, curb elevations, catch basins, manholes, overhead lines, and apparent service stubs can decide whether a Detroit site works. A boundary and topographic survey plus utility-quality review exposes conflicts before grading and pavement quantities are fixed.

Separate stormwater from legacy assumptions

A former paved yard may have relied on a private connection that no longer functions, while DWSD may require a different point of discharge or detention strategy. Coordinate runoff calculations, soil infiltration evidence, water-quality measures, and inspection access with the current city and utility process.

Build environmental sequencing into civil plans

Brownfield parcels need the civil engineer to coordinate with environmental consultants, not bury unknown soil or groundwater conditions in a general note. Erosion controls, construction entrances, contaminated-soil handling, and utility trench limits should match the remediation and construction plan.

Give the public connection an owner

Every proposed inlet, hydrant, sewer lead, driveway, and restoration area should be assigned to the city, DWSD, owner, or road authority so the civil set does not hide an approval gap.

Permit and engineering steps

  1. Submit a separate agency matrix with the BSEED site plan and track DWSD, drain, EGLE, and right-of-way comments by drawing revision.
  2. Confirm parcel ownership, zoning, survey limits, known contamination, floodplain status, and the Detroit agency contacts.
  3. Verify existing and proposed water, sewer, storm, fire, driveway, and public-right-of-way connections with DWSD and the relevant road authority.
  4. Prepare grading, utility, stormwater, erosion-control, accessibility, and construction-phasing documents with calculations.

Civil engineering for Detroit sites FAQs

Is a Detroit civil plan enough to authorize a new sewer connection?
No. DWSD controls its connection and service requirements, and other agencies may have jurisdiction over roads, drains, or environmental permits. A plan can support those applications, but the owner must obtain separate agency approvals.
Why do former Detroit industrial sites need more than a grading plan?
Legacy fill, contamination, buried foundations, poor infiltration, and abandoned utilities can change both constructability and legal obligations. Civil design should be coordinated with survey, geotechnical, and environmental findings rather than treating the parcel as vacant greenfield.
Can a civil engineer select the flood elevation from an online map?
Online FEMA mapping is a screening source, not always a site-specific elevation determination. The design team confirms current effective mapping, survey datum, local requirements, and any floodplain permit triggers with the applicable authority.
Who owns Detroit stormwater review?
The answer depends on the discharge and work: BSEED, DWSD, the county drain authority, EGLE, and a road agency can each control part of the route. The parcel determines the matrix.
