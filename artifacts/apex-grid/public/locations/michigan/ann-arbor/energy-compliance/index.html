Energy compliance for Ann Arbor buildings

Ann Arbor owners often pair Michigan code compliance with aggressive operational or electrification goals. We help teams select a documented code route that fits the building, research schedule, envelope, and utility service while separating required compliance from voluntary performance targets.

Permit authority

Ann Arbor Community Development Services reviews the building energy documentation; University of Michigan, DTE, and owner sustainability programs have separate requirements. Confirm the current Michigan energy-code edition, local forms, and any project-specific campus or incentive criteria before final design. Community Development reviews energy documents, while DTE, University of Michigan, and owner sustainability programs impose separate criteria where applicable.

Code context

Ann Arbor applies Michigan's commercial energy-code framework. The effective IECC or ASHRAE-based route, state amendments, alteration provisions, and performance options must be verified with Community Development Services and state sources because code transitions can change. Mandatory controls and envelope measures remain important in a modeled path. Confirm the effective Michigan route, Ann Arbor forms, alteration provisions, and any campus-owner overlay rather than treating a voluntary performance target as code.

Local conditions

Cold winters and humid summers make air sealing, moisture control, heat recovery, and part-load operation important. Research exhaust can overwhelm ordinary efficiency assumptions; historic masonry and storefronts need careful thermal and hygrothermal detailing. Electrification adds winter demand and service questions with DTE. Research exhaust, cold winters, historic masonry, DTE peak demand, and dense downtown façades make Ann Arbor energy inputs highly occupancy-specific.

Typical project types

Research and laboratory buildings; downtown adaptive reuse; high-performance multifamily; retail and restaurants; university-adjacent offices; HVAC and lighting retrofits. Examples include a North Campus lab, a Main Street historic retrofit, and a high-performance multifamily infill building.

Separate research energy from office energy

A laboratory's exhaust, make-up air, pressure control, and equipment schedule can dominate performance. The model and code documents should show these real operational inputs rather than hiding them in an office occupancy assumption.

Detail old façades carefully

Ann Arbor storefronts and masonry walls have thermal bridges and limited drying potential. Window, parapet, floor-line, and interior-insulation decisions require coordination with moisture and historic constraints.

Document the controls the operator can use

Heat recovery, demand response, scheduling, and ventilation reset are valuable only when sequences, sensors, commissioning, and maintenance responsibilities are clear. DTE service discussions should reflect the selected strategy.

Keep lab exhaust in the baseline

Document fume-hood diversity, make-up air, pressure, heat recovery, schedules, and controls so the energy record represents the Ann Arbor research use.

Permit and engineering steps

  1. Submit the modeled or prescriptive path, envelope moisture details, lab schedules, and controls responsibility with the city energy package.
  2. Confirm use, research schedules, envelope, alteration scope, current Michigan code route, and Ann Arbor or campus submission requirements.
  3. Assign model inputs and mandatory measures across envelope, MEP, lighting, controls, and owner operations.
  4. Complete calculations or forms and coordinate them with architectural details, equipment schedules, and control sequences.

Energy compliance for Ann Arbor buildings FAQs

How does Ann Arbor select an energy-compliance path?
The project team confirms the effective Michigan energy-code route and Ann Arbor submission requirements, then evaluates prescriptive or performance documentation for the actual occupancy and alteration scope. Owner sustainability goals are separate.
Can research exhaust be excluded from energy calculations?
Not automatically. The code path and project model need appropriate ventilation and operating inputs, with required recovery, controls, and safety measures evaluated for the actual lab.
Is an Ann Arbor sustainability target the same as code compliance?
No. A voluntary target may be more stringent, but it does not replace the current permit documentation or mandatory provisions.
Can an Ann Arbor lab use office schedules for compliance?
Only if they accurately describe operation; research exhaust, pressure control, equipment, and safety requirements usually require more specific inputs.
