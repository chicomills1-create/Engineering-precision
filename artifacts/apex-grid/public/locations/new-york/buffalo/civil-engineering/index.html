CIVIL ENGINEERING • BUFFALO

Buffalo Civil Engineering for Sites Shaped by Water and Industry

Buffalo redevelopment often combines old industrial grades, filled waterfront land, aging sewers, contaminated soil, and a tight public right-of-way. Our civil scope connects survey and environmental evidence to grading, stormwater, utilities, access, and approvals without assuming that a legacy connection or pavement section remains adequate.

Permit authority

The City of Buffalo handles local site and building-related review, while the Buffalo Sewer Authority controls many sewer connection and discharge matters. NYSDEC, Erie County, the Erie County Health Department, and NYSDOT may be involved for stormwater, brownfields, public health, or state highway interfaces; agencies depend on site and scope.

Code context

The New York State stormwater permitting program, NYSDEC design requirements, local Buffalo procedures, zoning, and Buffalo Sewer Authority standards may all govern a site. Verify whether the project needs an SPDES permit, SWPPP, post-construction practices, sewer capacity review, right-of-way permit, or environmental remediation coordination.

Local conditions

Lake Erie influence, intense rain, freeze-thaw, shallow or variable groundwater, glacial and fill soils, combined-sewer areas, and legacy industrial contamination affect grading and infiltration decisions. Snow storage, salt, pothole-prone pavements, and spring melt should be considered in the operations plan.

Typical project types

Waterfront mixed-use sites, brownfield cleanup and reuse, warehouse parks, food-production expansions, infill housing, parking and access improvements, green infrastructure, and neighborhood streetscape work.

Legacy drainage needs evidence, not a tie-in assumption

Industrial parcels may have private mains, abandoned lines, unknown inverts, or connections that function only under dry conditions. We use record research, survey, test pits, and utility coordination to define a drainage concept and identify where the Sewer Authority must confirm capacity or connection terms.

Stormwater practices must survive Buffalo winters

Bioretention, infiltration, detention, and green-roof strategies need an operations and freeze-thaw discussion. Pretreatment, salt exposure, snow storage, underdrains, overflow, maintenance access, and groundwater separation can determine whether a practice is durable rather than merely attractive on a plan.

Brownfield grading is a risk-management document

Cut and fill, vapor barriers, clean cover, dewatering, imported material, and utility trenches can affect an approved remediation plan. Civil drawings should align with environmental controls and contractor sequencing so grading does not unintentionally move contamination or compromise a required barrier.

Sewer Authority comments change the civil package

A Buffalo Sewer Authority connection or discharge response can alter invert, detention, pretreatment, and maintenance details after the City set is drafted. We carry those decisions back into grading and utility sheets instead of presenting a municipal approval as permission to make an unreviewed sewer connection.

Permit and engineering steps

  1. Confirm the Buffalo parcel, city limits, watershed, sewer district, flood exposure, brownfield status, NYSDOT frontage, and whether the work uses the City ePermits site or a separate agency intake.
  2. Tie a current topographic/utility survey to Sewer Authority records, test pits, groundwater and environmental reports, and the owner’s winter plowing and snow-storage route.
  3. Prepare the City grading and access sheets, Buffalo Sewer Authority connection/discharge material, NYSDEC SWPPP, and county or DOT restoration details as distinct coordinated packages.
  4. Submit the electronic City review plus Sewer Authority, NYSDEC, Erie County, health, or DOT applications; respond to capacity and remediation comments by agency, not by a generic response letter.
  5. Witness utility, subgrade, erosion-control, and stormwater installation, then issue surveyed inverts, practice maintenance data, tests, and closeout as-builts to each approving authority.

Buffalo Civil Engineering for Sites Shaped by Water and Industry FAQs

What does the Buffalo Sewer Authority need before an old connection is reused?
Capacity, ownership, condition, invert, discharge quality, and current connection requirements must be confirmed with the Authority. A City plan or legacy map is not capacity certification and does not replace test pits or agency approval.
Does every redevelopment need an NYSDEC stormwater permit?
The trigger depends on disturbance area, project classification, location, and exclusions or requirements in the current SPDES program. The owner and design team should determine the applicability and SWPPP obligations with NYSDEC and the local authority.
Why include snow storage in civil plans?
Snow piles can block inlets, damage planting, overload pavement edges, release salt, and obstruct emergency access. A Buffalo site plan should identify storage and melt pathways rather than leave winter operations to an improvised corner of the lot.
