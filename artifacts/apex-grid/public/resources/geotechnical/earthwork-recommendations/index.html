What Are Earthwork Recommendations?

Earthwork recommendations tell contractors how to prepare a site's soils for construction. Here's what they cover and why they matter for foundations.

The Short Answer

Earthwork recommendations are the part of a geotechnical report that tells the contractor how to prepare the site's soils so the structure can be built on them safely. They cover site clearing, removal of unsuitable material, excavation, placement and compaction of fill, moisture conditioning, and preparation of surfaces to receive foundations or pavement. These instructions turn the report's analysis into actionable field procedures, and following them is what allows the designed foundations to perform as intended.

What Earthwork Recommendations Cover

Site Clearing and Stripping

Recommendations specify removal of vegetation, topsoil, roots, debris, and other deleterious material. Organic and loose surface soils are generally unsuitable to build on and must be removed before earthwork begins.

Removal of Unsuitable Soils

Existing uncontrolled fill, soft or compressible soils, and other problem materials may need to be over-excavated and replaced with engineered fill. The report identifies these materials and the depth and extent of removal required.

Fill Placement and Engineered Fill

When fill is needed to raise grade or replace removed soils, recommendations specify acceptable fill materials, how they should be placed in controlled lifts, and the compaction and moisture requirements. Engineered fill placed to specification behaves predictably under load, unlike uncontrolled fill.

Subgrade and Foundation Preparation

Recommendations describe how to prepare the surfaces that will receive foundations, slabs, and pavement — including proofrolling, moisture conditioning, and compaction — so those surfaces provide uniform support.

Why Earthwork Recommendations Matter

Foundation and pavement designs assume the soil beneath them has been prepared a certain way. If a site is filled without control, if unsuitable soils are left in place, or if compaction is inadequate, the ground can settle unevenly and distress the structure regardless of how well the foundation itself was designed. Earthwork recommendations ensure the as-built ground matches the assumptions used in design. Field verification during construction confirms the work was done as specified.

When It Applies

Earthwork recommendations apply to nearly every project that requires a geotechnical report — new buildings, additions, retaining walls, and pavements — because almost all sites require some site preparation. They are especially critical where grade is being raised with fill, where unsuitable soils are present, or where the site has existing uncontrolled fill from prior work.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who follows the earthwork recommendations?

The grading and site contractor performs the work; the recommendations should be included in the construction documents, and the geotechnical engineer typically observes and tests the work.

What happens if recommendations aren't followed?

The ground may not provide the support the foundation design assumed, risking settlement and distress. Deviations should be reviewed by the engineer.

Is engineered fill different from ordinary fill?

Yes. Engineered fill is placed and compacted to specification and verified by testing, so it performs predictably; uncontrolled fill is not and can settle unpredictably.
