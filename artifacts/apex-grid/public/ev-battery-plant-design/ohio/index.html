The battery-plant picture in Ohio

Ohio pairs legacy auto strength with new cell capacity. Ultium Cells' Lordstown plant ships GM/LG cells, Honda's Marysville EV Hub anchors the state's electrification bet, and the $4 billion L-H Battery Company plant in Jeffersonville (Fayette County) began mass-producing lithium-ion cells in July 2026 — pivoting from its original EV mission to energy-storage systems as the market shifted, with Honda acquiring the building assets in a ~$2.9 billion deal. Ohio has real cell-production projects on the ledger — which means the engineering questions here are concrete: dry-room capacity for the announced GWh, utility interconnection for hundred-megawatt loads, and phased designs that get line one producing while later phases build.

NMP solvent handling and recovery

Cathode production runs on NMP solvent — effective, recoverable, and hazardous: combustible with reproductive toxicity. Coating and drying areas need solvent-rated ventilation, LEL monitoring with interlocks, classified electrical installations, and recovery systems that condense NMP from drying-oven exhaust for reuse. Recovery is both environmental compliance and operating economics — reclaimed solvent displaces purchased solvent. The hazmat inventory, control-area calculations, and classified-area plans are set during schematic design, because they dictate room layouts that are brutal to retrofit.

  • LEL monitoring with interlocks; classified electrical in coating zones
  • NMP recovery from drying exhaust: compliance + cost recovery
  • Hazmat strategy set during schematic design — it shapes the building

Structural design for process loads

Gigafactory structure serves the process, not just the code: heavy concentrated loads from coating lines and ovens, vibration limits for coating uniformity and assembly precision, crane and material-handling dynamics across long bays, and column grids that stay clear of process lines. Most plants are phased, so foundations and framing are detailed for future bays from day one — expansion joints placed where phase two attaches, crane runways pre-planned for extension. The structure also carries the vapor-tight dry-room envelope and the fire-protection strategy's rated separations. Process equipment data shapes the framing; the framing never just follows the shell.

  • Heavy concentrated loads + vibration limits + crane dynamics
  • Column grids serve the process lines, not just the shell
  • Phased expansion detailed from day one — future bays, joints, runways
