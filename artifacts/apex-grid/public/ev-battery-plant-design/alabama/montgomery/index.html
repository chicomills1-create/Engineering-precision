Dry-room HVAC: the −40°C promise

Every lithium-ion cell plant lives or dies on its dry rooms — spaces held at −40°C dew point or lower, continuously, because moisture turns electrolyte salt into hydrofluoric acid and ruins cells. The engineering is desiccant-wheel dehumidification with regeneration heat, vapor-tight envelopes with sealed penetrations, interlocked airlocks, and positive pressure — sized from a moisture balance, not a cooling load. Dry-room qualification at summer design conditions, witnessed and documented, is the signature commissioning event. It's the most specialized HVAC in industrial construction, and the most expensive to fix if it's designed wrong.

  • Desiccant dehumidification — cooling coils alone can't reach −40°C
  • Vapor-tight envelope: sealed joints, gasketed doors, sealed penetrations
  • Commissioning proves dew point at summer design humidity, not winter

Hazardous-materials classification and control areas

The IBC/IFC hazardous-materials framework decides how much of what can sit where: control-area calculations per room, occupancy classification, classified electrical areas, and separation distances. Battery plants concentrate NMP solvent, flammable electrolyte, and reactive lithium — each with its own quantity limits, storage rules, and protection requirements. The hazmat inventory is built with the owner's process team and kept current through design, because the most common permit-review failure is an inventory that doesn't match the process description. Get the classification right early and the AHJ review confirms; get it wrong and the review restarts.

  • Control-area calculations per room under IBC/IFC
  • Classified electrical areas for solvent and electrolyte zones
  • Inventory built with the process team — and kept current
