Food Processing Plant Engineering in Arkansas

The poultry capital's home turf: Tyson Foods' headquarters state anchors the densest broiler processing corridor in America.

By Jeremy Mills, CEO & Founder, Apex Grid Engineering — USAF Veteran

The Arkansas food industry

Poultry is Arkansas's top commodity at $5.6 billion, and Springdale is home to Tyson Foods — the nation's largest poultry integrator — with processing plants in Springdale, Rogers, Dardanelle, and Van Buren.

Mountaire Farms is headquartered in Little Rock, Simmons Foods in Siloam Springs, and George's in Springdale, giving northwest Arkansas one of the highest densities of poultry engineering, equipment, and skilled labor in the country.

Food manufacturing concentration ranks among the nation's highest at 39.7 workers per 1,000 employees, and the state continues to attract greenfield projects like Peco Foods' $165 million poultry complex in the northeast.

Key production sectors: Poultry; Rice; Soybeans; Catfish. Agricultural regions: Northwest Arkansas; Arkansas River Valley; Delta.

Engineering systems for poultry processing facilities

Arkansas summers bring heat and humidity that punish refrigeration envelopes; winters bring ice storms that threaten power reliability. We design vapor-tight cold envelopes plus robust backup power and freeze protection for process water.

  • USDA-FSIS-inspected slaughter and further-processing lines
  • Ammonia/CO2 cascade refrigeration for chillers and freezers
  • Scald, sanitation, and CIP hot-water systems
  • Washdown-rated electrical, FRP walls, and hygienic drainage
  • Feather, offal, and high-strength wastewater handling

The Arkansas Department of Environmental Quality permits wastewater discharge, and USDA FSIS inspection governs layout and sanitation for the state's many slaughter and further-processing plants.

Fast-track delivery for Arkansas processors

Food companies expand when demand forces it, which means engineering has to move at the speed of the business. Our phased design releases let contractors mobilize on site improvements and building shell while process-specific MEP is still being detailed, compressing the timeline between approved concept and first production run.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do poultry companies keep building in Arkansas?

Grower networks, skilled labor, and an established inspection and equipment ecosystem. Northwest Arkansas has decades of poultry infrastructure — hatcheries, feed mills, rendering, and further processing — so new plants plug into supply chains and a workforce that already understands sanitary operations.

What does a greenfield poultry plant need most from engineering?

USDA-inspected sanitary layout, ammonia or CO2 refrigeration sized for chillers and freezers, massive hot-water and steam capacity for sanitation, washdown-rated electrical, and a wastewater pretreatment plan that the local POTW will actually accept.

How do I start a food processing plant engineering project in Arkansas?

Send the site address, the products and processes planned, target production volumes and dates, and any existing drawings. We confirm jurisdiction and licensing, then scope the MEP, structural, and civil work with a fast-track schedule option built around your production date.
