Structural Engineering Services

Gravity, lateral, foundation, and retrofit design for steel, concrete, masonry, and timber structures — delivered as PE-stamped drawings and complete calculation packages that municipal reviewers approve the first time.

What Our Structural Engineers Actually Do

Every structure we design starts with a load path. Our engineers trace every pound of dead load, live load, snow, wind, and seismic demand from the roof deck down through beams, columns, shear walls, and footings into the soil — and we document that path in a calculation package a plan reviewer can follow line by line. Gravity design covers member sizing for steel wide-flange framing, glulam and dimensional lumber, reinforced concrete, and load-bearing masonry, checked against ASCE 7 load combinations and the material standards (AISC 360, ACI 318, TMS 402, NDS) referenced by the adopted building code.

Lateral design is where most projects live or die in plan check. We model wind pressures per ASCE 7 directional and envelope procedures and seismic demands using the equivalent lateral force procedure or, where irregularity demands it, modal response spectrum analysis. Diaphragm design, collector and chord forces, shear wall hold-downs, moment frame drift checks, and foundation overturning are all resolved in the calculation set — not left for the contractor to discover in the field.

Foundations are engineered to the project geotechnical report: shallow spread and continuous footings, mat foundations, drilled piers, helical piles, and grade beams, with bearing, settlement, and sliding checks documented. Where no soils report exists, we design to code-presumptive values and say so explicitly, so the jurisdiction knows exactly what basis of design it is approving.

Retrofits, Assessments, and Existing Buildings

A large share of our structural work is existing buildings: seismic retrofits of unreinforced masonry and soft-story buildings under ASCE 41, tenant-improvement openings cut into bearing walls, mezzanines added inside warehouse shells, and rooftop equipment loads on structures that never anticipated them. We perform ASCE 41 Tier 1 screenings and Tier 2 evaluations, produce retrofit drawings that preserve as much of the existing structure as the numbers allow, and coordinate directly with the jurisdiction on alternative-compliance paths when the letter of the current code cannot practically be met.

Equipment anchorage is its own specialty: seismic anchorage calculations for HVAC units, generators, tanks, and racking per ASCE 7 Chapter 13, with anchor design per ACI 318 Chapter 17. If your mechanical submittal was rejected for missing anchorage calcs, this is the fastest structural scope we turn around.

How a Structural Project Runs at Apex Grid

Every engagement begins with a scoping call and a fixed-fee proposal that names the deliverables, the code editions we will design to, and the delivery date. Before the first member is sized, we confirm the adopted building code and local amendments with the permitting jurisdiction — the difference between the 2021 and 2018 IBC, or a county wind-speed amendment, can change member sizes, connection details, and even the viability of a framing scheme. Getting that answer in writing on day one is cheaper than discovering it in a correction letter on day ninety.

Design then proceeds in coordination passes, not in isolation. Framing plans are checked against the architectural reflected ceiling plans for depth conflicts, against mechanical routing for duct penetrations through beams and shear walls, and against civil grading for top-of-footing elevations. Penetrations that must happen get engineered — reinforced openings, headers, and transfer details drawn at scale — rather than left as field problems. Because our MEP and civil engineers sit inside the same firm, these checks happen continuously instead of at a single late-stage clash review.

The submittal package itself is engineered for the reviewer: a table of contents in the calculations, load paths presented in the order a checker follows them, design criteria summarized on the general notes sheet, and special inspection requirements itemized so the jurisdiction can issue the permit without a back-and-forth about Chapter 17 inspections. After permit, we stay on the project — reviewing steel and truss shop drawings, answering contractor RFIs with sketches rather than prose, evaluating field deviations against the original calculations, and issuing the structural observation letters many jurisdictions require before final inspection. A stamped drawing set is the start of our responsibility, not the end of it.

Material selection is treated as an economic decision, not a default. On a typical low-rise commercial building we will compare a steel moment frame against braced frames and masonry shear walls, pricing the tradeoffs in tonnage, foundation demand, and erection speed before the architect commits to a floor plan that locks one system in. Timber projects get the same scrutiny: dimensional lumber versus engineered products, prefabricated trusses versus stick framing, and where mass timber genuinely pencils versus where it is fashion. Because the cost of a structural system is set by the earliest decisions, we push this analysis to the front of the schedule — a week of comparison at schematic design routinely saves five or six figures in steel and concrete at bid time.

Structural Engineering FAQs

Do you stamp drawings in my state?
Apex Grid holds professional engineering licensure in 49 states, so in nearly every US jurisdiction the answer is yes. Tell us where the project is and we confirm licensure and the governing code edition in the same call.
What codes do you design to?
The building code your jurisdiction has adopted — typically an edition of the IBC or IRC with state amendments — with loads per ASCE 7 and material design per AISC 360, ACI 318, TMS 402, and the NDS. We confirm the exact adopted editions with the permit office before design starts.
Do I need a geotechnical report?
For most commercial foundations, yes — and it usually pays for itself in reduced footing sizes. For light residential work, many jurisdictions accept code-presumptive soil values, and we will design to those when appropriate and state the basis of design on the drawings.
Can you fix a plan-check rejection from another engineer?
Frequently. We review the correction letter, the drawings, and the calculations, then either supplement the existing package or re-engineer the deficient scope. Rejections for missing lateral calcs or anchorage design are the most common and fastest to resolve.
How fast can you turn around a structural calculation package?
Simple scopes — beam sizing, equipment anchorage, single wall openings — often ship in days. Full building packages depend on size and coordination, and every proposal we send states the delivery date before you commit.
