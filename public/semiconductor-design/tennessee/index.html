Tennessee's Semiconductor Landscape

Tennessee's semiconductor story is automotive power electronics and federal research: the Ford BlueOval City complex in Stanton (with SK On) — a $5.6 billion EV and battery campus — plus GM's Spring Hill EV operations and Volkswagen's Chattanooga plant have created a power-electronics corridor. Oak Ridge National Laboratory's materials and neutron-science mission adds world-class R&D depth.

The state's no-income-tax structure, TVA power, and central logistics make it a natural site for power-module assembly, battery-electronics, and test operations. For automotive-electronics or ORNL-adjacent R&D facilities, Tennessee offers economics and talent that compete nationally.

Fab Utilities: Power, Water & Gas in Tennessee

TVA serves most of the state with industrial rates among the nation's lowest, backed by nuclear, hydro, and gas — a genuine 24/7-manufacturing advantage. The Tennessee River system provides abundant process water.

Incentives & Site Selection in Tennessee

Tennessee's FastTrack program, the Job Tax Credit, and local PILOT agreements delivered BlueOval City and the battery plants. The state's deal-closing apparatus is proven at multi-billion-dollar scale.

Workforce Pipeline

UT-Knoxville, Vanderbilt, and Tennessee Tech engineering programs, plus ORNL's workforce pipeline and the community-college system's automotive-technician training, supply talent. The battery plants are training power-electronics technicians at scale.

Energy Code & Compliance

Tennessee enforces the IECC 2012-based energy code (with 2018 adoption in leading jurisdictions) — a light-to-moderate compliance touch for industrial facilities.
