West Virginia's Semiconductor Landscape

West Virginia's semiconductor angle is materials and process industry: the state's chemical corridor (the Kanawha Valley's historic chemical plants), high-purity materials potential, and the NETL research mission. The FBI's CJIS complex and the state's growing data-center interest add secure-facility demand.

WVU's engineering programs and the state's available industrial power (coal and gas baseload) offer economics for energy-intensive manufacturing. For specialty-chemical, materials, or secure-facility projects, West Virginia offers low costs and a willing workforce.

Fab Utilities: Power, Water & Gas in West Virginia

Appalachian Power, Mon Power, and FirstEnergy serve industrials with some of the nation's lowest rates, backed by coal and gas baseload. The Ohio and Kanawha rivers provide abundant process water.

Incentives & Site Selection in West Virginia

West Virginia's manufacturing tax credits, the state's data-center incentives, and local property-tax abatements support industry. The Department of Economic Development runs an aggressive recruitment process.

Workforce Pipeline

WVU, Marshall, and West Virginia Tech engineering programs, plus the community-college technical training, supply talent. The chemical industry has trained generations of process technicians.

Energy Code & Compliance

West Virginia enforces the IECC 2009-based energy code (ASHRAE 90.1-2007 path) — a light compliance touch for industrial facilities.

Process Cooling Water
