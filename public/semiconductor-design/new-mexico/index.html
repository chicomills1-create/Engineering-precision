New Mexico's Semiconductor Landscape

New Mexico is a genuine fab state: Intel's Rio Rancho campus — one of the company's largest — runs advanced-packaging operations (Foveros/EMIB) that are central to Intel's heterogeneous-integration strategy. Sandia and Los Alamos National Laboratories drive radiation-hardened and specialty microelectronics R&D, and Rocket Lab's Albuquerque facility received $23.9 million in CHIPS funding for space-grade semiconductors.

The state's pitch is compelling: cheap land, 300+ days of sun, a national-lab talent pipeline, and the Elevate Quantum corridor connection. For advanced packaging, space-grade electronics, or specialty-device fabs, New Mexico offers fab-state capability at non-fab-state costs.

Fab Utilities: Power, Water & Gas in New Mexico

PNM and El Paso Electric serve industrials with Southwest-competitive rates; the state's solar resource is among the nation's best for offsetting cleanroom electrical loads. Water is the constraint — fabs must demonstrate aggressive reclaim in the high desert, and Intel's Rio Rancho water-recycling investments are the model.

Incentives & Site Selection in New Mexico

New Mexico's Local Economic Development Act (LEDA), the High Wage Jobs Tax Credit, and the state's Industrial Revenue Bond program delivered the Intel expansions. The state's film-style aggressiveness on incentives extends to tech manufacturing.

Workforce Pipeline

UNM, New Mexico State, and New Mexico Tech feed the labs and Intel; the community-college system's semiconductor-technician programs were built with Intel input. Lab-to-fab talent transfer is a genuine pipeline.

Energy Code & Compliance

New Mexico enforces the IECC 2018-based energy code with state amendments — moderate compliance burden. High-desert cooling design (large diurnal swings) rewards thermal-mass and free-cooling strategies.
