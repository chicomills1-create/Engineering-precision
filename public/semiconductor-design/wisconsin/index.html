Wisconsin's Semiconductor Landscape

Wisconsin's semiconductor relevance is power electronics, medical devices, and advanced manufacturing: the state's industrial-controls heritage (Rockwell Automation in Milwaukee), GE HealthCare's medical-device manufacturing, and the Foxconn-adjacent electronics corridor in Racine County. The UW-Madison engineering programs add research depth.

The state's manufacturing economy — the most manufacturing-intensive in America by employment share — has deep process-utility and controls expertise. For power-electronics, med-device, or industrial-controls manufacturing, Wisconsin offers a workforce that already builds to the tolerances cleanrooms require.

Fab Utilities: Power, Water & Gas in Wisconsin

We Energies, Alliant Energy, and Madison Gas & Electric serve industrials with Midwest-competitive rates. Water from the Great Lakes and the state's aquifers is abundant. Cold winters make heat-recovery and free-cooling valuable for cleanroom HVAC.

Incentives & Site Selection in Wisconsin

Wisconsin's Manufacturing and Agriculture Tax Credit (among the nation's most generous), the Enterprise Zone program, and local TIF districts support advanced manufacturing. The state's manufacturing-first policy is structural.

Workforce Pipeline

UW-Madison, Marquette, and Milwaukee School of Engineering produce strong engineering talent; the technical-college system's manufacturing programs are the national model. The industrial workforce is deep and experienced.

Energy Code & Compliance

Wisconsin enforces the IECC 2015-based energy code for commercial buildings — moderate compliance burden. Cold-climate heat-recovery design pays back for cleanroom operations.
