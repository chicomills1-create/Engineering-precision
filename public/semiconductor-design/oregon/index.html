Oregon's Semiconductor Landscape

Oregon is Intel's R&D heart: the Gordon Moore Park campus at Ronler Acres in Hillsboro — nearly 500 acres, 22,000 employees — is the headquarters of Intel's global Technology Development organization, where every process node is invented before it transfers to high-volume fabs worldwide. The $3 billion D1X Mod3 expansion added 270,000 square feet of cleanroom for next-generation process development, bringing Intel's total Oregon investment past $52 billion.

The ecosystem extends beyond Intel: Microchip's Gresham fab (part of its $162 million CHIPS award), the Oregon State materials programs, and a deep bench of fab-tool-component suppliers. Hillsboro is one of perhaps five places on earth where leading-edge process technology is created — and the cleanroom MEP expertise concentrated there is unmatched.

Fab Utilities: Power, Water & Gas in Oregon

Portland General Electric and Pacific Power serve the Silicon Forest with Pacific Northwest hydro-backed rates — among the lowest carbon-intensity industrial power in America. Water from the Tualatin Valley systems supports process use, though growth planning requires engagement. The mild climate is a structural advantage for cleanroom HVAC efficiency.

Incentives & Site Selection in Oregon

Oregon's Strategic Investment Program (SIP) — which underwrote Intel's expansions — plus the state's enterprise-zone property-tax abatements form a proven stack. The Governor's semiconductor task force coordinates CHIPS-adjacent investment.

Workforce Pipeline

Oregon State, Portland State (with its semiconductor-focused programs), and the community-college system's fab-technician tracks — built with Intel input — supply talent. The Silicon Forest has trained generations of process engineers.

Energy Code & Compliance

Oregon enforces the Oregon Energy Efficiency Specialty Code — among the nation's most aggressive, ahead of IECC. Cleanroom MEP must model heat-recovery aggressively; the mild climate makes the economics work.
