Semiconductor Facility Engineering

Cleanroom MEP, fab utilities, and structural engineering for the chipmaking boom — designed fast-track and stamped in 49 states by Apex Grid Engineering.

The short answer

Semiconductor facility engineering is the MEP and structural design behind chip manufacturing — ISO Class 5 cleanrooms, ultrapure water, process cooling, bulk and specialty gases, toxic-gas safety, vibration isolation, and 100-megawatt power distribution. Apex Grid Engineering designs and stamps these facilities in 49 states, delivering on the fast-track schedules that fab economics demand.

Engineering for the Chipmaking Boom

The CHIPS Act has unleashed the largest semiconductor construction wave in American history — TSMC's $165 billion Arizona GigaFab, Samsung's $37 billion Texas campus, Intel's $20+ billion Ohio Silicon Heartland, Micron's $100 billion New York megafab. Every one of these projects is a building-systems challenge of extraordinary scale: cleanrooms holding 3,520 particles per cubic meter, water purer than anything in nature, temperatures controlled to half a degree, floors stable to micrometers per second.

The giants — AECOM, WSP, Jacobs — treat fab work as one practice among dozens, staffed through global bureaucracies on their timelines. Apex was built for the opposite: senior semiconductor-facility engineers who answer the phone, overlapping fast-track design packages, a single coordinated 3D model, and licensed PEs stamping in 49 states. When a tool-install window is fixed and the capital meter is running, the MEP set has to be right the first time — and early.

Fab MEP Systems We Engineer

Every semiconductor facility is a coordinated system of systems. Apex designs them as one model — not nine silos.

Recirculation Air & Filtration

300–600 ACH through HEPA/ULPA banks, unidirectional flow design, N+1 fan redundancy for ISO Class 5 bays.

Process Cooling Water

18–22°C loops at ±0.5°C stability, dedicated chiller plants, waterside economizer for free-cooling hours.

Ultrapure Water (UPW)

18.2 MΩ-cm treatment trains — RO, EDI, UV, ultrafiltration — with continuously recirculating distribution loops.

Bulk & Specialty Gases

Nitrogen, argon, oxygen, hydrogen plus hydrides and dopants in orbital-welded high-purity distribution with VMBs.

Toxic Gas Monitoring

Continuous detection at TLV fractions tied to hazardous exhaust, emergency shutdown, and evacuation signaling.

AMC Control

Chemical filtration for acids, bases, organics, and dopants at parts-per-trillion — SEMI F21 classified.

Vibration Isolation

VC-A through VC-E structural criteria, isolated equipment, and verified measurements at tool locations.

Electrical & Power Quality

50–100+ MW distribution, dedicated substations, harmonic filtering, and sag ride-through for lithography.

Fire Protection (FM 7-7)

ESFR sprinklers coordinated with HEPA ceilings, VESDA detection, and clean-agent suppression for critical spaces.

Semiconductor FAQs

What does semiconductor facility engineering include?

The full MEP and structural scope behind chip manufacturing: cleanroom HVAC (300–600 ACH, ISO Class 5), process cooling water, ultrapure water, bulk and specialty gas distribution, toxic-gas monitoring, AMC control, vibration isolation to VC curves, high-power electrical distribution, and FM Global-compliant fire protection — plus the structural engineering for vibration-sensitive tools.

Why do semiconductor projects need specialized MEP engineers?

Generalist MEP firms don't speak fab: they miss AMC control, undersize UPW reclaim, ignore VC-curve vibration criteria, and discover air-balance failures during commissioning. Semiconductor MEP is a specialty — the systems interact (air balance, pressurization, exhaust, gas safety) in ways that only fab-experienced engineers anticipate. Apex's semiconductor practice exists because the facility is the production system the tools plug into.

How fast can Apex deliver fab MEP design?

Fast-track is our default delivery model: overlapping design packages, long-lead equipment procurement at 30–60% documents, 3D-coordinated construction, and commissioning that starts during construction. We structure every semiconductor program around tool-install milestones — because a fab's economics are dominated by time-to-production, and every month of delay costs tens of millions.

Does Apex stamp semiconductor facility drawings in my state?

Apex Grid Engineering is licensed in 49 states plus DC (all but Alaska). Our licensed PEs stamp fab, cleanroom, and advanced-manufacturing drawings in every jurisdiction we serve — one engineering team, one coordinated model, valid stamps wherever your program builds.

What's the difference between a fab, a foundry, and an OSAT facility?

A fab is any chip-manufacturing plant. A foundry (like TSMC or GlobalFoundries) manufactures chips designed by others. An OSAT (like Amkor) handles assembly and test — packaging finished wafers. Each has distinct MEP signatures: wafer fabs need the full ISO Class 5/UPW/gas scope; OSAT facilities need Class 7 assembly cleanrooms with advanced-packaging utilities. Apex engineers all three.
