Indiana's Semiconductor Landscape

Indiana just landed one of the CHIPS Act's signature projects: SK hynix's $950 million advanced-packaging facility in West Lafayette — a high-bandwidth-memory (HBM) packaging plant tied to Purdue University that puts Indiana on the AI-memory supply chain map. The state's automotive and RV manufacturing base adds power-electronics and controls demand.

Purdue's semiconductor programs — including the nation's most aggressive microelectronics workforce initiative — make West Lafayette a talent magnet. For advanced packaging, test, or power-module assembly, Indiana offers central logistics, low power rates, and a workforce pipeline built specifically for the chip industry.

Fab Utilities: Power, Water & Gas in Indiana

Duke Energy Indiana, NIPSCO, and AES Indiana serve industrials with Midwest-competitive rates; the state's grid is adding renewables while retaining coal/gas baseload for 24/7 reliability. Water from the Wabash and White river systems supports process use.

Incentives & Site Selection in Indiana

The Indiana Economic Development Corporation's EDGE tax credits, the Manufacturing Readiness Grants, and local tax abatements delivered the SK hynix deal. Purdue Research Foundation's Discovery Park District offers purpose-built sites for semiconductor suppliers.

Workforce Pipeline

Purdue's electrical, materials, and the Semiconductor Degrees Program — launched with industry input — are the centerpiece; Notre Dame, Rose-Hulman, and IUPUI add depth. The state's Next Level Jobs program funds technician training.

Energy Code & Compliance

Indiana enforces the IECC 2012-based energy code (with ASHRAE 90.1 compliance paths) — a light touch for industrial facilities.
