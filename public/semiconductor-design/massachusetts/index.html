Massachusetts's Semiconductor Landscape

Massachusetts is a CHIPS Act winner with real fab content: Analog Devices ($105 million CHIPS award) and MACOM ($70 million) are expanding specialty-semiconductor manufacturing in the state, and the legacy of Digital, Raytheon, and the Route 128 electronics industry sustains high-mix, high-reliability manufacturing. MIT Lincoln Laboratory's microelectronics work adds defense-grade depth.

The Boston-Cambridge research corridor — MIT, Harvard, and the quantum and photonics startups spinning out of them — generates continuous R&D cleanroom demand. For specialty-device fabs, advanced-packaging R&D, or compound-semiconductor pilot lines, Massachusetts offers the densest technical talent on the East Coast.

Fab Utilities: Power, Water & Gas in Massachusetts

Eversource and National Grid serve industrials at New England's high rates — energy modeling and heat-recovery pay back faster here than almost anywhere. Water is abundant and excellent quality. The grid is reliable, and the state's clean-energy mandates give 24/7 operations a decarbonization story.

Incentives & Site Selection in Massachusetts

The Massachusetts Manufacturing Accelerate Program, the Economic Development Incentive Program (EDIP) tax credits, and MassTech's advanced-manufacturing grants support fab investment. The state's CHIPS-adjacent R&D funding through the MassTech Collaborative is substantial.

Workforce Pipeline

MIT, Harvard, UMass, Northeastern, and WPI produce an unmatched engineering pipeline; the community-college advanced-manufacturing programs train technicians. The talent is elite — and priced accordingly.

Energy Code & Compliance

Massachusetts enforces the IECC 2021-based Stretch Energy Code statewide for commercial buildings — among the nation's most aggressive. Cleanroom MEP must model heat-recovery aggressively and document everything.
