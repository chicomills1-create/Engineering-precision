Colorado's semiconductor identity is quantum and aerospace-grade microelectronics. Boulder's quantum corridor — NIST's laboratories, the University of Colorado, and a dense startup cluster — is building the control electronics and cryogenic infrastructure that quantum computing demands. Microchip operates a fab in Colorado Springs, and the state's aerospace/defense electronics base (Lockheed Martin, Ball Aerospace heritage) sustains radiation-hardened and high-reliability electronics manufacturing.
