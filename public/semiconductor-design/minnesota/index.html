Minnesota's Semiconductor Landscape

Minnesota is a CHIPS Act standout with two funded fabs in Bloomington: SkyWater Technology's 200mm trusted foundry ($16 million CHIPS award, DoD Trusted Foundry supplier) and Polar Semiconductor — the FIRST commercial CHIPS award recipient — receiving $123 million to double its sensor and power-device capacity. IonQ's $1.8 billion acquisition of SkyWater in 2026 added a quantum dimension to the state's fab base.

The Twin Cities' med-device cluster (Medtronic, Boston Scientific, 3M's heritage) means Minnesota already operates more ISO-certified cleanrooms per capita than almost any state — the cleanroom construction trades, validation expertise, and contamination-control culture are deeply established. For specialty-device fabs, sensor manufacturing, or power-device expansion, Minnesota's ecosystem is turnkey.

Fab Utilities: Power, Water & Gas in Minnesota

Xcel Energy serves the Twin Cities with industrial rates that are competitive for the Upper Midwest and a grid adding wind aggressively. Water is abundant — the state's lakes and aquifers simplify process-water planning. Cold winters make free-cooling and heat-recovery exceptionally valuable for cleanroom HVAC.

Incentives & Site Selection in Minnesota

Minnesota's Forward Fund — which delivered $75 million to Polar — plus the Minnesota Investment Fund and JOBZ-style local abatements form a proven stack. The state's CHIPS-adjacent coordination through DEED is genuinely effective.

Workforce Pipeline

The University of Minnesota's nanofabrication center (Minnesota Nano Center) and engineering programs, plus the state's technical-college system, supply fab talent. The med-device industry has trained generations of cleanroom technicians.

Energy Code & Compliance

Minnesota enforces one of the nation's most aggressive energy codes — significantly ahead of base IECC. Cleanroom MEP must model heat-recovery aggressively; the cold climate makes the economics work.
